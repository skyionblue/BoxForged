Found 2026-08-26 fixing `pfb_MinimapCamera` in `CulDeSac_WildWestCity` ([[project_wildwestcity_build]], `docs/BACKLOG.md` B90). Two independent bugs combined to make the minimap render as a flat, empty solid-color square — worth separating them, because only one is scene-specific.

**Bug 1 (scene-specific, the obvious one): wrong framing.** A camera authored for a small room (`position (0,40,7)`, `orthographicSize 18`) doesn't automatically work on a much bigger level — the fix is the unsurprising one: recenter on the real geometry's true bounds and resize `orthographicSize` to cover it. Not the interesting part of this bug.

**Bug 2 (latent in the *prefab default itself*, easy to miss): the camera's own altitude exactly matched the altitude of the thing it needed to see.** `MinimapIndicator._altitudeY` (every entity's minimap blip floats at this fixed world-Y, default `40`) and `pfb_MinimapCamera`'s own default `position.y` are **both `40`** — the same number, in the shipped prefab default. A camera at zero distance from its subject puts that subject inside the camera's own near-clip offset (`nearClipPlane`, default `0.3`, `0.1` in one scene's override): the blip (a thin ~0.1m disc centered at that Y) ends up entirely on the wrong side of the near plane and never renders — no error, no warning, just a camera that faithfully renders its own `SolidColor` clear color and nothing else. This reads exactly like "camera isn't seeing anything" (framing bug) even when framing is otherwise correct, which is what makes it easy to misdiagnose as only Bug 1.

**How this was actually confirmed, not just reasoned about:** screenshotting the *camera's own output directly* (`manage_camera` `screenshot` with `camera:` set to the specific top-down camera, not the main gameplay camera) at each iteration. A theoretical near-clip distance calculation predicted the blip would be invisible; the fix (moving the camera a few meters above the shared blip altitude, e.g. `Y=42` for a `Y=40` blip, with a small `nearClipPlane` like `0.5`) was verified by literally seeing the colored blip appear in that direct screenshot where it hadn't before. Don't trust the math alone here — an off-by-a-few-degrees float-precision quirk in either direction would flip the visible/invisible verdict, and the in-HUD `RawImage` panel is small enough at typical screenshot resolutions (256–512px) to make "is there actually a dot in there" hard to eyeball without a full-resolution direct camera capture.

**How to apply:**
- Before trusting any top-down/orthographic camera's prefab defaults in a new scene, check whether its `position.y` and whatever altitude the things-it-should-see live at (a `MinimapIndicator`-style fixed-Y convention, or any other "float at Y=N for camera visibility" pattern) are the same number or close to it. If so, the camera needs to sit measurably above that altitude (a few meters, not a few centimeters — leave real margin for `nearClipPlane`), not just be repositioned in X/Z.
- This is a **prefab-default-level** latent bug, not something scoped to one scene — any other scene using the same prefab at its raw default position likely has the same non-rendering indicator, just unnoticed because nobody screenshotted that specific camera's raw output in isolation. Flag it for a source-level check (`docs/BACKLOG.md` B90 left this as an open follow-up for Room 1/Ambush Alley) rather than assuming "it must already work elsewhere so it's fine here."
- When fixing this as a scene-local instance override (the right scope when you can't touch the prefab asset), you still need `orthographicSize`, `nearClipPlane`, `farClipPlane`, and `cullingMask` overrides too — moving `position.y` alone doesn't change what an orthographic camera frames (orthographic FOV is size-driven, not distance-driven); only the depth-clipping behavior changes with height.
- A human owner watching iteratively may push back on the exact numbers ("closer to the ground") even after the bug itself is fixed and verified — that's a legitimate separate feel/design note, not evidence the technical fix was wrong. Re-verify the adjusted numbers with the same direct-camera-screenshot method rather than assuming a smaller height delta still clears the near-clip collision — in this case it did (2m clearance was enough), but that's a case-by-case margin call, not a fixed constant.
