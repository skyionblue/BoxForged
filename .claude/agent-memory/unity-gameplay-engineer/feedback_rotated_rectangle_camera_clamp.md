Found 2026-08-26 building the minimap follow-camera clamp in `CulDeSac_WildWestCity` (`docs/BACKLOG.md` B110, see [[project_wildwestcity_build]] and [[feedback_circular_arena_clamp_sizing]] for a related but distinct clamp problem in the same scene).

**The setup:** a top-down orthographic camera (fixed rotation, never yaws — it only ever looks straight down) must be clamped so its captured square (world-axis-aligned, half-size = `orthographicSize`) never extends past a `Ground` rectangle that is itself yawed 45° in world space (this scene's whole street is rotated).

**The bug:** the first implementation transformed the camera's candidate centre into the ground's own rotated local axes, then clamped each local axis independently to `±(groundHalfExtent - orthographicSize)` — the standard technique for clamping a camera *whose view window shares the same rotated axes as the bounds* (this is correct, and is exactly what [[feedback_circular_arena_clamp_sizing]]'s circular-clamp technique does for a *circular* window, where orientation doesn't matter). It is **not** correct here, because the captured square's edges are aligned to world X/Z, not to the ground's rotated local axes. A world-axis-aligned square's corners can poke outside a rotated rectangle's edge even while its centre sits a full `margin` inside that edge as measured naively along the rectangle's own axes. Caught immediately via the task's own "screenshot the minimap's own render output early, before polishing further" step — first render showed a real black void wedge in one corner, not just a hypothetical risk.

**The fix, derived not guessed:** for a ground-local axis unit vector `u` (i.e. `groundRotation * Vector3.right` or `* Vector3.forward`), the world-axis-aligned square's extent (support function) along `u` is `margin * (|u.x| + |u.z|)` — a square's support in any direction equals its half-size times the L1 norm of that direction expressed in the square's own axes. Using this projected margin instead of the raw `margin` on each local axis is the necessary and sufficient condition: a corner of the square maximises `|offset·u|` at `|offsetLocal·u| + margin*(|u.x|+|u.z|)`, so keeping that at or under the ground's half-extent for both local axes keeps every corner inside the rectangle, proven algebraically (not just tested). The projection factor is exactly `1.0` when the camera and ground share axes (no yaw mismatch) and grows to `√2 ≈ 1.414` at a 45° mismatch — so the naive version isn't "slightly off," it's under-clamping by a large, geometry-dependent factor whenever camera orientation and ground orientation diverge.

**How to apply:**
- Before clamping any camera/view window against a bounds rectangle, check whether the window's own edges are aligned to the *same* axes as the rectangle. If yes (e.g. a camera that yaws with the level, or a circular window where orientation is irrelevant), a plain per-axis margin subtraction is correct. If no (a fixed-orientation camera over a yawed level, or vice versa), use the support-function projection above instead.
- Verify numerically, not just by inspection: build a `Vector3 support = groundRotation * axis` and confirm `|support.x| + |support.z| >= 1` for any axis actually used — it will always be `>= 1` by the Cauchy-Schwarz-adjacent identity underlying the L1-vs-L2 norm relationship, confirming the naive version is never an overestimate, only ever an underestimate.
- The cheapest way to verify a clamp fix like this over a real scene is a two-stage check: (1) a synthetic sweep — clamp a few thousand random points and independently re-check that all 4 corners of the resulting camera window land inside the true bounds (catches algebra errors, in-editor, no Play Mode needed); (2) real Play Mode screenshots at the level's actual hardest points (every wall, every corner, not just one). Stage 1 alone would not have caught the separate [[feedback_static_batching_meshfilter_bounds]] bug found in the same task — the bounds values it was checking against were themselves wrong at runtime, so both checks are needed and neither substitutes for the other.
