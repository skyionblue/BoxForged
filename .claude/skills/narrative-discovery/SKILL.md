# Narrative Discovery

Run a writers-room process that develops the story with the owner rather than inventing a finished narrative on their behalf.

## Read first

Read all available story notes, project context, and `docs/CREATIVE_STATE.md`. Treat explicit canonical facts as protected constraints. If sources conflict, surface the conflict instead of resolving it silently.

## Conversation cadence

- Ask 3-5 tightly related questions per round and wait for answers.
- Focus each round on one creative problem.
- Offer 2-4 substantially different directions when options would help.
- Explain what each direction changes about player emotion, gameplay, theme, scope, or later story consequences.
- Recommend a direction when appropriate, but do not make major creative decisions without owner agreement.
- Say when an idea is weak, contradictory, overly convenient, thematically muddy, or poor for gameplay.
- Protect ideas that are already working; do not rewrite for novelty.

## Canon state

Record decisions in `docs/CREATIVE_STATE.md` as CANON, WORKING, OPEN, or REJECTED. Never promote WORKING or OPEN material to CANON without owner agreement. Never reintroduce REJECTED material without explaining the new reason to reconsider it.

## Develop story and gameplay together

For every major narrative idea determine:

- what the player does;
- what the player discovers;
- what changes mechanically or spatially;
- what emotion the player should experience;
- how the idea is communicated through play, environment, animation, audio, or consequence;
- whether a cutscene or exposition is actually necessary.

Avoid screenplay-first design. Prefer playable storytelling.

## Areas to explore when relevant

- protagonist want, need, flaw, fear, misconception, and change;
- personal stakes before world-scale stakes;
- inciting incident and credible reason to act;
- allies, opposing forces, and morally complex motivations;
- world rules and what the player can reliably predict;
- revelations, reversals, midpoint change, lowest point, climax, and aftermath;
- relationship arcs and recurring emotional anchors;
- environmental storytelling and recurring motifs;
- how progression changes both capability and understanding;
- what questions should remain ambiguous;
- ending payoff, cost, consequence, and the thought left with the player.

Do not force a three-act structure when another structure fits better.

## Narrative integrity tests

Before locking a major beat, test it against:

1. **Motivation**: would this character plausibly do this now?
2. **Causality**: does it happen because of prior choices/events rather than convenience?
3. **Agency**: does the player meaningfully cause, discover, or experience it?
4. **Theme**: does it deepen the game's questions instead of merely stating a message?
5. **Mechanics**: can gameplay carry part of the meaning?
6. **Payoff**: was the emotional or narrative setup earned?
7. **Scope**: can the team actually build it without hollowing out the rest of the game?

## Discovery deliverables

During discovery maintain working material in:

- `docs/CREATIVE_STATE.md` for decision state;
- `docs/STORY_BIBLE.md` for characters, world rules, themes, motifs, and accepted/working story structure.

These are living discovery documents, not permission to start production.

## Narrative lock gate

When the narrative is coherent enough for pre-production, summarize what is locked, what intentionally remains ambiguous, remaining risks, and prototype questions. Stay in discovery until the owner explicitly says **"Lock the story and begin pre-production"** or otherwise clearly authorizes pre-production.
