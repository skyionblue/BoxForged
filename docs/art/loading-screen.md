# Unboxed Heroes — Loading Screen Art Direction

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 2026-07-16
**Orientation:** Landscape 16:9 (iOS + Android)
**Status:** Approved for production

## 1. Loading Screen Concept

### Narrative Moment

The loading screen captures the world at the edge of the conflict — the instant before our hero steps into a World drained of imagination. The World Tree stands in the distance, massive and unmistakable, but grey and withered under The Unimaginative's control. The player character — a small, stocky cardboard box ninja — stands in the foreground with their back to the viewer, looking out toward it. The tree is the destination. The hero's posture communicates readiness, not despair.

This is not a moment of triumph. It is a moment of determination before the journey begins. The hero is small against the task ahead, but they are there, and they are ready.

### Emotional Tone

- Primary: **Quiet determination.** The player should feel like they are the small thing that matters in a big broken world.
- Secondary: **Wonder.** The World Tree is enormous, strange, and beautiful even in its drained state. It should feel worth saving.
- Tertiary: **Playful tension.** The craft aesthetic — the cardboard, the marker lines, the hand-made world — undercuts the bleakness with warmth. This is a serious world seen through a child's eyes.

## 2. Composition

### Layout (Landscape 16:9)

```
[LEFT THIRD]          [CENTER THIRD]          [RIGHT THIRD]
                                               
Bleak foreground      World Tree (hero        Broken skyline
rubble, cracked       element) rising         and distant
grey earth            from midground          structures
                                               
   [PLAYER]           
  (foreground,        [SAFE ZONE: bottom quarter — game title + loading bar]
   left-center)       
```

### Camera Angle

Low, three-quarter perspective — camera sits roughly at the player character's waist height and tilts slightly upward. This makes the World Tree loom, amplifies the hero's smallness, and creates a strong ground-to-sky diagonal that draws the eye from the player toward the tree.

### Foreground

Cracked, grey earth — like dried cardboard left in the rain. Surface texture suggests corrugated cardboard that has lost its color. Scattered debris: crumpled grey paper balls, an overturned tin can, a torn strip of kraft tape stuck to the ground. No living plant life.

The player character stands slightly left of center in the foreground, seen from behind. Their box head catches a faint warm light from the right (the direction of the tree). This is the only warm color anchor in an otherwise grey-brown foreground.

### Midground

The World Tree occupies the center of the frame, rising from the middle distance. It is vast — its trunk alone spans a significant portion of the vertical frame. It is made of tightly compressed cardboard layers, like a massive sculptural column of corrugated material stacked and twisted upward. Its branches are bare in the drained state — skeletal, reaching outward in silhouette. A faint haze or particle effect (desaturated dust motes or paper fiber particles) drifts around its base.

The ruins of what were once structures — collapsed fences, toppled environmental objects — frame the base of the tree and reinforce the sense of something once great.

### Background

A flat, pale grey sky — no blue, no clouds with color. The color value of `#D4CFC9` from the drained state palette. Very faint horizon line where distant grey structures (collapsed buildings, more ruined trees) meet the sky. The World Tree breaks through the horizon and extends toward the top of the frame. The sky is lightest near the horizon and slightly darker at the top, creating a subtle gradient that makes the tree silhouette read clearly.

### Focal Point

The World Tree. Everything in the composition — the low camera angle, the diagonal ground plane, the placement of the player character — leads the eye to it.

### Hero Element

The World Tree is the environmental hero element. The player character is the human/emotional focal point — they anchor the viewer's perspective and create the sense of scale.

## 3. Lighting and Color

### Light Source

A single directional light from the right side of frame, low angle — like a weak, overcast sun trying to push through grey clouds. It catches the player character's right shoulder and the right-facing surfaces of the World Tree's bark. It is warm (`#E8C97A` at very low saturation — closer to `#C9BC9A` in the drained state) relative to the ambient grey, but not bright. It is enough warmth to read, not enough to lift the mood fully.

This asymmetric lighting — warm from the right, cool ambient from everywhere else — creates a subtle tension that suggests the warmth of imagination is present but suppressed.

### Color Palette for this Illustration

This scene is set in the **drained state**. The drained palette governs:

| Element | Color | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | `#D4CFC9` | Pale warm grey, lightest at horizon |
| Ground / earth | `#A89F94` | Mid grey-brown, heavy cardboard grain |
| World Tree trunk | `#8A8076` | Darker grey-brown, corrugated texture |
| World Tree branches | `#6B6560` | Dark grey silhouette, very thin strokes |
| Foreground debris | `#9C9490` | Slightly lighter than ground |
| Player character (body) | `#C8BFB0` with `#1A1A2E` accent | Box is drained but outline retains navy for readability |
| Warm accent (light hit) | `#C9BC9A` | Only on player shoulder, tree right face |
| Outline strokes | `#3D2B1F` | Marker black — all outlines, tree branch lines |
| Atmospheric haze | `#D4CFC9` at 40% opacity | Light fog / dust at tree base |

### Mood

Vast and quiet. The drained palette keeps the image from feeling festive or warm, but the craft textures — the corrugated cardboard, the visible marker lines on the player, the sculptural mass of the tree — keep it from feeling photorealistically grim. It is a grey world seen through a handmade lens. The hand-made quality is the hope. The world looks like it was built with care, and that care is not entirely gone.

### Gradient / Atmosphere

- Sky: subtle top-to-bottom gradient from `#C4BCAF` (top) to `#D4CFC9` (horizon)
- Ground: subtle vignette darkening at extreme left and right edges to direct the eye inward
- Tree base: faint radial fog from `#D4CFC9` at 30–40% opacity, softening the tree's connection to the ground and adding depth

## 4. Key Visual Elements

### Player Character (Cardboard Box Ninja)

Seen from behind, slightly left of center frame. Stocky chibi-adjacent proportions — box head roughly 35% of total height, cube-shaped and oversized. The ninja mask marker lines on the box face are visible in profile. Clothing is flat grey-washed to reflect the drained state, but the marker outline on the box retains its dark navy (`#1A1A2E`) so the character reads clearly against the grey ground. Arms at rest or one arm slightly raised — not mid-action, but alert. The character stands on flat feet, weight evenly distributed. They are looking toward the World Tree.

Scale: the player character should occupy roughly 15–18% of the total frame height in the final illustration. Small but not invisible.

### The World Tree

The single most important element. It is built from compressed cardboard layers — imagine a massive cardboard tube column with corrugated texture rings stacked up like geological strata. The trunk splits into major branches at roughly 60% of the visible frame height. Branches are bare, skeletal, drawn with thick marker strokes that thin toward the tips. The overall silhouette should be recognizable as a tree but clearly constructed — this is not a natural oak. It is a World Tree made of the same material as the heroes' heads, which is the point.

The bark texture is grey corrugated cardboard with visible horizontal corrugation lines and dark marker-drawn growth rings. Some sections of the trunk have kraft tape applied — as if someone tried to repair it. These tape strips should be visible as slightly lighter grey rectangles with darker edge lines.

Height in frame: the tree top reaches to approximately 10% from the top of the safe art area (well above where the player character stands). The base of the trunk is at the midground horizon line.

### Foreground Debris

Scattered across the cracked grey ground in the foreground. Each piece should feel like a household or craft item in its drained state:

- One overturned tin can — grey, slightly crushed, marker-drawn ridges
- Two or three crumpled paper balls — slightly lighter grey, visible fold lines
- A strip of kraft tape stuck to the ground — slightly raised at one curling end
- Faint crack lines in the ground surface radiating from the foreground toward the tree (like a dried riverbed or cracked cardboard)

Do not overload the foreground with debris. The ground should feel empty and abandoned, not messy.

### Atmospheric Particles

Very subtle drifting particles at the base of the World Tree — desaturated paper fiber or dust mote look. Not bright, not glowing. Implemented as a light fog pass in the illustration. Scale and opacity should be low enough that they read as atmosphere, not VFX.

### Sky

Plain, flat, gradient grey. No clouds. No sun visible. No stars or moon. Just the quiet, drained overcast. The sky exists to provide contrast for the tree's silhouette and to communicate that the world's imagination has been taken away.

## 6. UI Overlay Notes

The loading screen background art must leave clear, unobstructed zones for the UI overlay elements. Plan the illustration with these areas protected.

### UI Element Positions

| UI Element | Position | Safe Zone Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Game title "Unboxed Heroes" | Top-center, inside top 20% of frame | Keep sky simple and uncluttered in top 20% — no branches, no tree silhouette crossing this band |
| Loading bar | Bottom of frame, full width, inside bottom 10% of frame | Ground in this band should be dark enough (vignette) to ensure the cardboard-strip loading bar reads. Target background value here: `#7A7470` or darker. |
| Loading progress text | Directly above the loading bar, bottom 12–15% of frame | Same dark-enough ground rule applies. |
| Studio / publisher logo | Bottom-right corner, bottom 8% of frame | Keep bottom-right foreground ground clear of debris elements. |

### Typography Context

Title treatment uses the "Permanent Marker" font (already in project at `Assets/_Project/Fonts/PermanentMarker.asset`). The title should appear to float slightly above the background — render it in `#3D2B1F` (dark brown marker) with a 2px offset hand-drawn drop shadow in `#F5EDD6` (aged paper). Do not use a standard drop shadow filter — the shadow should look slightly hand-drawn and offset diagonally down-left.

### Art Direction for the Protected Zones

- **Top band (top 20%):** Pure sky. No branches, no tree silhouette, no debris. Keep it as the plain gradient sky described in the lighting section. The title will sit here and needs clear contrast.
- **Bottom band (bottom 15%):** Dark vignette ground, minimal detail. The loading bar UI uses a cardboard-strip visual style — a horizontal strip of kraft brown with black marker tally marks filling as the load progresses. This needs a dark background to read. Ensure the ground in this zone trends toward `#6B6560` or darker.
- **Center band (middle 65%):** This is where all illustration content lives — player character, World Tree, debris, atmospheric effects.

## 7. Asset Delivery Spec

### Primary Background Asset

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| File name | `ui_loading_bg_drained.png` |
| Resolution | 2732 × 1536 px (iPad Pro max — scales down to all targets) |
| Format | PNG-24, no alpha channel (fully opaque background) |
| Color space | sRGB |
| Safe art area | 2560 × 1440 px centered (96px bleed on all sides for safe cropping) |
| Unity folder | `Assets/_Project/UI/LoadingScreen/` |
| Unity import settings | Texture Type: Sprite (2D and UI); Max Size: 2048 (auto-compress on mobile); Format: ASTC 6x6 (iOS), ETC2 RGBA8 (Android); Generate Mipmaps: enabled; Filter Mode: Bilinear |

### Versioning

If alternate compositions from Prompts B or C are produced for A/B review, name them:

- `ui_loading_bg_worldtree.png` — environment shot (Prompt B)
- `ui_loading_bg_spincycle.png` — dramatic shot (Prompt C)

All three can be imported into Unity; the active one is assigned via the LoadingScreenController at runtime.

## 8. Placeholder Strategy

If final art is not ready before the loading screen needs to go live in a build:

1. Use a flat `#C8BFB0` (drained palette primary) solid fill as the background.
2. Place the "Unboxed Heroes" title text (Permanent Marker font, `#3D2B1F`) centered in the top quarter.
3. Use a simple Unity UI Slider styled with the project's cardboard color as the placeholder loading bar.
4. This placeholder requires zero new assets and can be wired immediately.

Final art can be dropped into `Assets/_Project/UI/LoadingScreen/` and assigned in the LoadingScreenController without any code changes, as long as the file names match the spec above.
