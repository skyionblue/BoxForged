## PIECE 1 — "The Western Intro"

**In-game cutscene. Duration: ~45–60 seconds. Shot count: 8. Aspect ratio: 16:9.**

Both kids already in hero form — box heads on. The girl rounds a corner into a grey suburban cul-de-sac and stops. Starting from her feet, the world transforms into a Wild West scene. The boy catches up behind her. Together they face a grey Unimaginative figure standing still at the far end of the golden street. She steps first.

---

### SHOT 1 — The Corner | 6s

Medium shot, camera at child eye level, slightly low angle. The Cowgirl hero — brown corrugated cardboard box head, wide-brim cardboard cowboy hat on top, denim shirt, fringe leather chaps, boots with spurs, lasso coiled at hip — steps around a grey concrete corner from the left side of frame. She is alone. The world behind her and ahead of her is fully desaturated: pale grey asphalt cul-de-sac, washed-out beige houses, dead grey lawns, overcast white sky. She takes two steps forward and stops in the center of frame. She does not hesitate — she stops because she has seen something. Her posture shifts: weight forward, chin up. She does not look behind her. 2D cartoon style, thick black outlines, hand-drawn marker aesthetic, blue-grey desaturated palette for the entire frame.

### SHOT 2 — She Sees It | 5s

Her hand-drawn marker eyes — simple ink lines on cardboard — are fixed on something ahead, unmoving. The world reflected in front of her (visible in the background out of focus) is still grey. This is a beat of pure stillness before something changes. No movement except the faintest tilt of her head — a fraction of a degree left, like a surveyor taking a measurement. 2D cartoon style, thick black outlines, desaturated blue-grey world behind her.

### SHOT 3 — The World Begins to Change | 7s

Wide shot, looking down the cul-de-sac from behind the Cowgirl at medium distance. She stands in the center foreground, facing away from camera. The transformation begins at her feet: a ring of warm amber-gold color spreads outward from the soles of her boots like ink spreading on wet paper. Cracked dry earth replaces grey asphalt inside the ring — terracotta red-brown, sun-bleached, with visible cracks. Where the transformation touches the houses at street-level, wooden saloon fascias appear over the vinyl siding. A tumbleweed rolls through the expanding warm zone. The sky at the far end of the street begins to warm from white-grey to a pale gold. She has not moved — the world is changing around her, starting from where she stands. 2D cartoon style, thick black outlines, strong color contrast between the advancing amber-gold zone and the retreating blue-grey world.

Saloon facades cover every house front, complete with hand-painted wooden signs. Mailboxes now have small swinging saloon-door panels. A covered wagon sits where a minivan was parked. The dirt road stretches forward, rutted and dry. The sky burns deep gold. The Cowgirl stands center-left in the frame, fully in the golden world — she belongs here, she made this. The transformation is complete. She has not moved a single step since it started. 2D cartoon style, full warm amber-gold palette, thick outlines, hand-drawn marker aesthetic.

He nearly bumps into her. He sees what she sees. He goes still. She was already here. She does not acknowledge his arrival. She was not waiting. They stand side by side — she on the left, he on the right, her box head slightly forward of his. ## PIECE 2 — "She Was Already Ready"

**Two versions. Art style, color direction, and shot composition are identical — the short version is a condensed cut of the long version.**

---

### PIECE 2 (LONG VERSION) — Social / In-Game Prologue

**Duration: ~45–60 seconds. Shot count: 8. Platform: YouTube Shorts / in-game prologue. Aspect ratio: 9:16.**

She was training before the world needed her. The Hush didn't create her purpose — it revealed it. A girl who had been practicing alone for years without knowing why, and when the world went grey, she barely looked up. She already knew what to do.

---

### SHOT 1 — Dawn Drills | 7s

Wide shot, early morning, a suburban backyard in full color (pre-Hush world). The Girl in regular clothes — dark hair in a ponytail, simple t-shirt, jeans, sneakers — moves through a series of precise solo footwork drills in the center of the backyard. The grass is slightly damp. Long morning shadows stretch from her feet. There is no teacher. No audience. No phone in sight. The drills are not playful — they are practiced and controlled, the movement of someone who has done this many mornings before. No score. No applause. She just goes. Camera is static at medium distance, letting her fill the frame from her own motion. 2D cartoon style, full warm color palette (the pre-Hush world), thick black outlines, hand-drawn marker aesthetic.

---

### SHOT 2 — The Hush | 6s

Static medium shot inside the same backyard, slightly closer on the Girl mid-drill. In the background, through the fence slats and over the rooftop — all at once, silently — every light in every house goes dark. No flash, no bang: just a quiet grey bleed spreading across the background like water absorbing into paper. The warm colors drain from everything behind her. She notices. She slows to a stop. She looks up — not startled, more like someone who heard their name called from a distance. She looks at her hands. The world is grey behind her. She looks back at the yard. She is still in color — but only barely. 2D cartoon style, warm color in foreground fading to desaturated blue-grey in background, the contrast is visible but subtle at this stage.

---

### SHOT 3 — She Keeps Going | 5s

Medium shot from a slightly lower angle. The world is now fully grey — backyard, sky, fence, everything behind the Girl is desaturated blue-grey. She stands in the center of frame for one more beat. Then, without a word, without a decision-face, she drops into stance and continues her drills. The grey world does not matter. The repetition is the point. She was always doing this. The camera holds still on her as she moves. 2D cartoon style, fully desaturated blue-grey world, the Girl herself rendered in slightly warmer tones than everything else — she is not yet in color but she is the most alive thing in the frame.

---

### SHOT 4 — The Garage | 6s

Interior close shot of a grey garage. Dull tools on a pegboard. Grey dusty shelves. The Girl steps into frame from the left and scans the space with quiet focus — she is looking for something specific without knowing what it is yet. Her eyes move left-right, unhurried. Then she stops. She has found it. Camera cuts or pans slightly to reveal a small dark cardboard box on a shelf — flat light, modest, almost invisible among the grey. She steps forward and picks it up with both hands. She turns it over once. She looks at it for a long moment. It is just a box. 2D cartoon style, desaturated grey interior, the cardboard box slightly warmer in tone than its surroundings — the first hint of amber.

---

### SHOT 5 — Drawing | 7s

Close-up on the Girl's hands. She is holding the cardboard box steady against a workbench with one hand. In her other hand, a black marker. She draws on the front of the box — three lines: two for eyes, one curved line below. Simple. Direct. Not decorative. She draws without hesitation, like she already knew what to put there. The marker strokes are thick and confident, visible as hand-drawn lines. When she finishes, she holds up the box and looks at it. It looks back. This is enough. 2D cartoon style, tight frame on hands and box, neutral grey background, the box and marker lines carry all the visual weight.

---

### SHOT 6 — The Transformation | 5s

Medium shot from slightly below, inside the garage. The Girl holds the box at chin height, looking at it. Then she puts it over her head — smooth, deliberate, not theatrical. The moment the box settles into place, color explodes outward from her in a sharp ring: the garage walls, the tools, the shelves all snap into warm amber-gold color simultaneously, as if a switch has been thrown. The box head sits on her shoulders, marker eyes forward. She does not look around at the newly colored world. She does not react with wonder. She was already in fighting stance. She did not have to adjust. 2D cartoon style, dramatic color-snap transition from grey to full warm amber, thick outlines, hand-drawn marker aesthetic.

---

### SHOT 7 — Into the Street | 6s

Wide shot from behind the Girl as she walks out the back door of the house and across a grey suburban street. Color radiates outward from her feet in a slow expanding circle as she walks — the grey cracked asphalt, the grey lawns, the grey mailboxes warm and color up as she passes. She does not slow down. She does not look back. She was ready before the world needed her. The street stretches ahead. She walks toward it. Camera holds static at a low angle, letting the color circle expand toward us. 2D cartoon style, blue-grey world beyond the color circle, warm amber within it, strong contrast at the boundary.

---

### SHOT 8 — She Was Always Training | 7s

Static wide shot. The Girl — Cowgirl hero form now fully visible: box head, wide-brim hat, chaps, spurs, lasso — walks forward down the grey street into the distance. The color circle moves with her. She grows smaller in the frame as she moves away from camera. After she passes the midpoint, cut to black. Text card appears, hand-lettered in marker style: **"She was always training."** Below it, smaller: **UNBOXED HEROES**. Hold for 3 seconds. 2D cartoon style, thick outlines, hand-drawn marker text card.

## PIECE 3A — "The Cowgirl's Choice"

**Social media. Duration: ~30–45 seconds. Shot count: 6. Platforms: TikTok / Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts. Aspect ratio: 9:16.**

The most emotionally grounded of the four pieces. She had a phone. She loved it. The Hush took it. She grieved. On day four she chose cardboard. A girl who had something real to lose and chose imagination anyway — that is her power.

---

### SHOT 1 — The Phone | 6s

Close-up, interior night. A girl's hands holding a phone. The screen glows — warm, soft, the only light in the frame. The room behind her is dark and formless. We see only the glow on her face: she is ~8 years old, warm skin tone, dark hair. She loves this thing. It is hers. The glow is amber-warm, alive, precious. Camera holds still on the phone and her face. This is the only warm color in the world. 2D cartoon style, tight intimate frame, the phone glow as the sole light source, deep blue-grey surrounding darkness, thick black outlines.

---

### SHOT 2 — The Glass | 7s

Same close-up angle, same room. The phone screen goes dark — not gradually, instantly. The glow cuts off. She stares at it. The screen is now just black glass, reflecting nothing. She tries to turn it on: presses the button, once, twice. Nothing. The room is darker now — the one warm light is gone. She looks at the dead screen for a long moment. She sets it down on a surface beside her — carefully, gently, not in anger. She sits on the edge of her bed. Camera holds. Let this breathe. This is loss, and it is real. 2D cartoon style, fully desaturated blue-grey palette, the dead phone just a dark rectangle, thick black outlines.

---

### SHOT 3 — Three Days | 5s

Time-lapse feel achieved through three quick cuts held together in one unbroken camera position: the same medium shot of the Girl sitting at the edge of her bed, hands in her lap. Outside her bedroom window: grey dawn, grey afternoon, grey dusk. Her posture is the same in each — not collapsed, just still. She is not crying. She is processing. On the third beat, she looks down at her right hand, open, palm up — the hand that held the phone. It is empty now. The grey light from the window is the only illumination. 2D cartoon style, static camera, desaturated blue-grey, thick outlines.

---

### SHOT 4 — The Garage | 7s

Wide shot, grey garage interior. The Girl steps in from the left. She moves with purpose now — the stillness is over. She scans the shelves. Old cardboard hat boxes stacked in the corner — dull grey-brown in this light, slightly warmer than everything else. She crosses to them. She picks one up. It is bigger than the small box from Piece 2 — this one fits over her head. She turns it in her hands. She looks at it. She knows what this will cost her. She has already decided. She sets the box on the workbench and picks up a marker. 2D cartoon style, desaturated grey garage, the cardboard slightly warmer in tone, the marker in her hand the warmest object in the frame.

---

### SHOT 5 — The Choice | 6s

Medium close shot. The Girl holds up the finished box — a brown corrugated cardboard face with drawn eyes and hat-brim lines, close to the look of the full Cowgirl box head. She looks at it. The camera holds on this: the girl and the box, face to face. She knows what the phone was. She knows what this is. They are not the same. She is not pretending otherwise. She puts the box on her head. The moment it settles, a single pulse of warm amber light washes outward from her in a soft ring — not the full color explosion of Piece 2, something quieter, more personal. The garage remains grey. But she is in color. 2D cartoon style, intimate frame, the warmth radiating from her alone, grey world unchanged around her.

*Reference image: `flow-references/Female-Cowboy-concept.png`*

---

### SHOT 6 — She Steps Out | 7s

Wide shot from inside the open front door of a house, looking out. Pale grey daylight fills the doorway. The Cowgirl — box head, wide-brim hat, lasso at hip — walks out the front door from inside toward the camera, then past us and out into the street. As she crosses the threshold, color begins spreading from her feet across the grey concrete porch, across the grey front path, fanning into the grey street. She does not pause. She does not look back. She is the color now — she is carrying it with her. Camera holds as she walks forward and out of frame. Grey street behind her has begun to warm. Cut to black. Text card, hand-lettered marker style: **"She chose this."** / **UNBOXED HEROES**. Hold for 3 seconds. 2D cartoon style, strong grey-to-amber color contrast, thick black outlines, hand-drawn marker text.

## PIECE 3B — "First Sight of the World Tree"

**In-game cutscene. Duration: ~45–60 seconds. Shot count: 8. Aspect ratio: 16:9.**

The stakes reveal. Both kids see the World Tree for the first time and understand what needs to happen. This is a cinematic reveal, not an action beat — pace it slowly. The girl sees it first, reads it before the boy does, and moves before she explains anything.

---

### SHOT 1 — Walking the Grey Street | 6s

Wide shot, looking down a grey suburban street from slightly above child eye level. Both kids in hero form walk in the foreground from left to right — the Cowgirl leading, half a step ahead of the Ninja who follows on her right. The world is fully desaturated: grey houses, grey pavement, grey mailboxes, white-grey overcast sky. The Cowgirl's posture is forward and easy — she is alert but unhurried. The Ninja is close behind, moving with the same momentum. Their footsteps are the only motion. The street is otherwise empty and silent. 2D cartoon style, fully desaturated blue-grey world, warm tones only on the two characters, thick black outlines, hand-drawn marker aesthetic.

*Reference image: `flow-references/Boy_walks_in_cul-de-sac_overlay_202607241934.jpeg`*

---

### SHOT 2 — She Stops | 5s

Medium shot at child eye level, from slightly ahead of both kids, facing back toward them. The Cowgirl stops mid-stride. Her head tilts up — she has caught something above the roofline. Her posture changes: weight shifts forward onto the front foot, chin rising. The Ninja almost walks into her back, catches himself, stops beside her. He does not know why she stopped yet. He looks at her. Then he follows her gaze upward. The camera does not yet show what they are looking at — it stays on their faces/heads, building anticipation. 2D cartoon style, desaturated grey world, both characters slightly warmer in tone than the street behind them, thick outlines.

---

### SHOT 3 — Above the Rooftops | 7s

Slow camera tilt upward, beginning at grey rooftop level and rising. The grey houses fill the lower frame. As the camera rises above the roofline, the top third of the frame reveals sky — and just above the peak of the tallest rooftop, the very crown of the World Tree. The reveal is gradual: first just the tips of branches — made entirely of stacked and fused cardboard boxes, warm golden-brown, glowing with life against the grey sky. As the camera continues to tilt, more of the upper canopy comes into view: an enormous, ancient, impossibly large tree whose every leaf is a cardboard box, whose every branch is a trunk of laminated cardboard. The upper half is warm and golden. The sky around its crown begins to glow amber. 2D cartoon style, dramatic slow reveal, warm golden canopy against grey overcast sky, thick outlines.

*Reference images: `flow-references/World_Tree_made_of_cardboard_202607241934.jpeg` and `flow-references/Enormous_ancient_tree_cardboard_202607241934.jpeg`*

---

### SHOT 4 — The Industrial Base | 6s

Wide shot, pulling back slightly. The World Tree now fills most of the frame. The upper half glows warm and golden — a living canopy of cardboard boxes. The lower half is different: wrapped in grey industrial scaffolding, grey pipes, grey machinery processing cardboard that falls from the branches above. Grey conveyor belts. Grey loading docks. Grey figures in grey suits moving among the machinery with clipboards. Everything at the base has been industrialized and seized. The life at the top is being harvested at the bottom. The tree is still alive — but it is surrounded. 2D cartoon style, stark contrast between warm golden canopy and grey industrial base, thick outlines, the color split is visible at roughly the mid-point of the tree.

*Reference image: `flow-references/Suits_seizing_World_Tree_202607241934.jpeg`*

---

### SHOT 5 — The Girl Reads It | 5s

Close on the Cowgirl's box head and upper body, from a low angle, the World Tree visible behind her over her shoulder. Her posture tells the story: she has gone very still. Then, a slow shift — her weight moves forward onto her front foot. She has figured it out. Cardboard flows from this tree to every box in every suburb. The Unimaginative have surrounded it and locked it down. No tree, no cardboard. No cardboard, no boxes. No boxes, no imagination. That is why everything is grey. She understood this in the time it took to tilt her head. She does not share this realization out loud. Her body has already made the decision. 2D cartoon style, close-up on the Cowgirl's posture and box head, World Tree visible and golden behind her, thick outlines.

*Reference image: `flow-references/Female-Cowboy-concept.png`*

---

### SHOT 6 — Without a Word | 4s

Medium shot of both kids side by side. The Ninja looks at the Cowgirl. He wants to know what she's thinking. She does not explain. She looks at the tree. Then at him. Then back at the tree. This exchange takes three seconds. He understands. Not from what she said — from what she didn't. He looks at the tree with the same certainty she has. She is on the left of frame, slightly forward. He is on the right, slightly behind. 2D cartoon style, both characters in warm tones against the grey world, the golden glow of the tree visible in the background, thick black outlines.

---

### SHOT 7 — One Step | 5s

Medium low angle, close behind the Cowgirl's boots. Her spurs catch ambient light. She takes one deliberate step forward. Her boot print in the grey pavement. Then a second step. Behind her, the Ninja's feet enter frame a half-beat later — he follows. The camera holds low on the ground, framing their feet, the cracked grey pavement ahead of them, and the grey industrial wall of the World Tree's base visible far in the distance. She is already moving. He follows. 2D cartoon style, low ground-level angle, grey pavement, warm tone on their boots, thick outlines.

---

### SHOT 8 — The Last Tree | 8s

Wide establishing shot, low angle looking up. Both kids walk forward in the lower third of frame — the Cowgirl center-front, the Ninja just behind and right. Ahead of them: the massive grey industrial walls surrounding the base of the World Tree, stretching up out of frame on both sides. Above the grey walls, the golden cardboard canopy of the tree extends across the upper half of the frame, vast and warm against the pale grey sky. They are small. The tree is enormous. She does not slow down. He does not either. They walk toward it. Camera holds as they grow smaller. Slow fade. Text card appears over the faded image, hand-lettered in marker style: **"The last tree."** / **UNBOXED HEROES**. Hold for 4 seconds. 2D cartoon style, dramatic scale contrast between the small children and the enormous tree, warm golden canopy, grey industrial walls, thick black outlines.
