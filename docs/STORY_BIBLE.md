# Story Bible

Use this as living narrative discovery memory. It may contain CANON and WORKING material; authoritative state is tracked in `CREATIVE_STATE.md`.

## Emotional core

A girl who never stopped seeing fights to get color back into a world that forgot it had any. Not to save the world in the abstract — to get color back into one neighbor's face.

## Central dramatic question

Can imagination reclaim a world that forgot it had any — and was the thing that killed it actually wrong?

## Themes and tensions

- **Imagination vs. procedure**: The Unimaginative are not villains. They write the forms, file the permits, manage the systems. They are not wrong that someone has to. The grey is what happens when that is all there is.
- **Seeing correctly vs. seeing accurately**: Kid does not pretend the backyard is a dojo. She sees what it is underneath. Every Skeptic is right about the facts. They just don't know what facts matter.
- **The cure and the wound**: The Elders were right about the diagnosis. Whether they were right about the cure is the question the game lives inside. It never resolves it.
- **Connection lost and refound**: The Cowgirl is looking for someone. The Female Ninja doesn't know she is being looked for — she arrives at the same place for her own reason. Recognition hits them both at the same moment. Neither one gets to say "I found you." They find each other. That's the difference.
- **Children as the last imaginers**: The kids who were already going outside, already turning sticks into swords — they were there all along. The Hush just made them visible.

## Intended player feeling

The last imaginative kid in a world that forgot how. Every upgrade is "you *imagined* a better weapon," not "you got a stat bonus."

Core emotions: **nostalgia** ("I remember doing exactly this"), **urgency with wonder** ("the world needs us and we're doing it our way"), **empowerment** ("my weird idea is the right idea"), **hope** ("imagination wins — it always wins").

## Protagonist — Kid

- **She/her/hers. Always. In every document.**
- **Want**: to get color back into Mr. Wen's face. To get the apple tree to bloom. She doesn't articulate it this way, but this is what she's walking toward.
- **Need**: to keep going past certainty — to discover that courage and certainty are not the same thing.
- **Flaw / blind spot**: She goes because she *knows*. That certainty is her engine — and the thing the Skeptic chips away at. Her growth is not learning to doubt. It's learning to go anyway when the certainty is gone.
- **Fear**: That she will lose the sight — not the fight. That one day she'll look through the box and it will be cardboard. That the Skeptic will be right. This is why she recruits: every kid who can also see is proof the sight is real. She is never alone in it as long as others see it too.
- **Personal stakes**: Mr. Wen. Never named in-game. Not a quest marker. His window boxes, empty.
- **Arc**: **Certainty → Courage.**

### Arc shape
- **Act 1**: She goes because she knows. The box shows her the world correctly and she has never doubted it.
- **Act 2**: The Skeptic's words land heavier. Other kids notice and follow — not because she asked, but because courage is visible in a way certainty isn't. She's still going, but the certainty is thinning.
- **Act 3**: At the World Tree, she finds Mr. Wen's tomatoes in the roots. She knows. That is her moment — private, complete, earned. She has done what she came to do.
- **Off-screen**: What happens to Mr. Wen after belongs to the companion anime. The game leaves his restoration off-screen.

### The Elder BoxHead recognition (emerging)
The Elders acted on uncertain math. They did the work without being sure it was right. When Kid arrives at the World Tree and meets The Two Elders, she and they recognize something in each other — two things that kept going past the point where certainty ran out. The question of whether they were right, and whether she was, is the same question. The game still does not answer it. But now the silence means something different than it did.

### The box
The box is a lens, not a creator. She cuts a hole, looks through it, and sees the world correctly — the imagined layer that was always there. The box does not make her imaginative. It reveals that she always was. She has not taken it off because the world has not been fully reclaimed yet.

## Key relationships

- **Kid + Mr. Wen**: Her anchor. Never named in-game as a quest target. A martial arts instructor — had a dojo a few blocks away, taught kids after school. Grew tomatoes. On Sunday mornings he sat in the roots of the old tree at the edge of the neighborhood. He knew where the World Tree was before Kid did. After The Hush: grey. Window boxes empty. The Lens cannot reach him — he is not somewhere imagination can simply fix. She finds his tomatoes in the World Tree roots and knows. That is enough.
- **Kid + The Cowgirl**: The Cowgirl joins Kid's cause because she notices what Kid is doing and wants to be part of it. Kid was always imaginative; the Cowgirl *chose* the box after losing her phone — that gives her a gravity Kid doesn't have yet. Their prior relationship, if any, is not the game's story to tell. The Cowgirl also has her own mission running in parallel: finding the Female Ninja.
- **Kid + The Female Ninja**: Kid recognizes something in her — someone who was already training before The Hush, already preparing for something without knowing what.
- **Kid + The Skeptic**: The opposite of her. The Skeptic does not pursue or shout. It arrives, speaks one flat sentence, and waits. Its words land. They do not stop her.
- **Kid + The Two Elders (Elder BoxHead)**: The people whose decision created the world she is fighting in. To be developed.
- **The Cowgirl + The Female Ninja**: The Cowgirl has been looking. The Female Ninja has heard what Kid is doing and comes to join — recruitment is what moves her. She seeks out the Cowgirl to be part of the effort. They arrive at the same place drawn by the same thing and find each other in it. Recognition is simultaneous. Neither one gets to say "I found you." Happens off-screen from Kid; Kid walks into them as an existing fact. Phase 2+; zone TBD.

## World and narrative rules

- Imagination is real. The imagined layer is not pretend. When Kid sees the backyard as a dojo, the dojo is what it actually is underneath. She is seeing correctly.
- The box is a lens. Everyone can, in theory, pick up a box. Most people see a box. Kid picks it up and sees through it.
- The Great Hush happened in the present day. No countdown, no announcement. Adults say it quietly, as two words, and then stop.
- The Elders are exactly two people — lifelong friends since junior high, worked multiple IT jobs together. They ran the math. They were not certain. Engineers don't believe in certainty. Their names never appear.
- The Unimaginative are not cruel. They are suspended. They are right about everything they know. They just know less about what things are.
- The Skeptic(s): Skeptic Grunts are kids who never learned to see. A Skeptic picks up a bo staff and holds it like a bo staff. Kid picks up the same bo staff and knows it is a quarterstaff that has been waiting for someone to pick it up right. They are not wrong. They just know less.
- The game does not resolve whether The Elders were right. Do not resolve it.

## Opposing forces

- **The Unimaginative**: Overarching antagonist group. People who came home from The Hush and waited for instructions that never came. Seized the World Tree as "infrastructure management." They do not notice Kid's reclamation efforts because it offends their ideology — they notice because something breaks in their systems. Grey zones going vivid, kids disappearing from grey neighbourhoods, colour bleeding back into managed areas: these show up as anomalies, management failures, things that are not behaving as expected. They escalate not from fear of imagination but from bureaucratic instinct. Something in their systems is wrong. That cannot be permitted.
- **Skeptic Grunts**: Recurring enemy type across all zones. Kids who never learned to see. Zone-appropriate weapons — they pick up the same objects Kid does, and are dangerous with them, but they don't know what they're holding.
- **Gnome Soldiers**: Recurring infantry across all zones (like Koopa Troopas in Mario Bros). Present in every level.
- **The Skeptic (singular)**: The nameless recurring presence. Not quite a person. What happens to the space where imagination used to be, given a shape and a box. Its box is grey — not painted, just never decorated. It does not pursue. It arrives, speaks one flat sentence, and waits. Its presence drains the Imagination Overlay. This is not intentional. The Skeptic is simply there.
- **Zone bosses**: Each zone has its own boss tied to the imagination of that specific place.
  - World 1 (Cul-de-Sac): SpinCycle — washing machine drum heavyweight brawler
  - World 2 (Backyard/Dojo): **The Grasscutter** — rusted reel-mower imagined as a tengu blade-master. Two phases: Kata (deliberate, teaches you) → Rev (the machine taking over). Cherry blossoms on defeat.

## Story-gameplay relationship

- Each zone reclaimed = color returns = someone specific somewhere gets something back
- Boss defeats are moments of restoration, not conquest — the defeat moment and the Imagination Restore are one event
- Upgrades are expressed imaginatively: "your broomstick is now a *legendary* staff" — never stat numbers
- The Cowgirl and Female Ninja appear as co-op characters when co-op unlocks; their search-and-reunion is the co-op narrative engine
- **Recruitment = World Tree health = world visually changing**: Kid shows kids something through the box; those who see join the coalition; the World Tree grows visibly stronger — more vivid color, more life bleeding into the world. This is visible, not a stat.
- **HKW bridge**: Real kids completing real skills at Hard Knocks Workshop feed into the same coalition. Future Phase 3+ feature: real HKW participants named in the World Tree.

## Journey / major beats

- **World 1 (Cul-de-Sac)**: Kid arrives, defeats SpinCycle, the street blooms warm gold
- **World 2 (Backyard/Dojo)**: Kid + possibly Cowgirl; Grasscutter boss; cherry blossoms on defeat
- **(Future)** Cowgirl and Female Ninja reunite — simultaneous recognition; Female Ninja comes for a reason (TBD); happens off-screen from Kid; zone TBD
- **(Future)** The Cardboard Mill — first glimpse of the World Tree; mid-game climax
- **(Future)** The World Tree — Kid reaches the base; Elder BoxHead = The Two Elders revealed
- **(Ending)**: Kid at the base of the World Tree (or in its roots, box still on). The mills restart — differently. Cardboard moves again. Somewhere, another kid puts a box on her head and decides her room is a castle.

## Revelations and reversals

- **Elder BoxHead = The Two Elders**: The mysterious figure at the World Tree is the engineers who caused The Great Hush. They are not enemies. They are the people who started all of this, watching to see if they were right.
- **The Cowgirl's search ends**: The Female Ninja is her best friend. TBD — how Kid learns this, and what it means.

## Ending and aftermath

The mills restarted — not under grey management, not processing. The cardboard that came out was different. You could feel it at the crease. Within a month, another kid somewhere put a box on her head and decided her room was a castle. Adults talk about infrastructure. Some of them still look up sometimes for no reason.
