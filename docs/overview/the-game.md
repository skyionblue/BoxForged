# BoxForged — The Game, In Plain Language

*This is the settled, owner-approved version of the story and world — the
CANON only. It leaves out in-progress debate on purpose. Full canon detail
and everything still being worked out lives in `docs/CREATIVE_STATE.md` and
`docs/STORY_BIBLE.md`.*

## The pitch

Everyone was addicted to screens. Then the internet broke — everywhere, all
at once ("The Great Hush"). One kid's parents never let her have a phone in
the first place. So when everything went quiet, she went outside, found a
cardboard box, put it on her head — and saw everything: the backyard is a
dojo, the cracked cul-de-sac is a Wild West showdown. The box doesn't create
this world, it reveals it — imagination that was always there.

The people who caused The Great Hush have seized the last living tree on
Earth — the last source of cardboard — because without boxes, the lens goes
dark forever. They forgot about the kids.

## Kid, the protagonist

- A girl (she/her — this is settled, not up for revisiting).
- What actually drives her isn't saving the world in the abstract — it's her
  neighbor, Mr. Wen, a martial arts teacher who went grey after The Hush.
  She never says his name in-game; he's not a quest marker, he's the reason
  any of this matters to her.
- Her arc: **Certainty → Courage.** She starts out sure of what she sees. As
  the world gets harder, the certainty thins — her growth isn't learning to
  doubt, it's learning to keep going once the certainty is gone.
- Her fear isn't losing a fight, it's losing the sight — waking up one day
  and it's just cardboard again.
- She recruits other kids by showing them the world through the box. Every
  kid who sees it too is proof she isn't imagining it.

## The world's backstory

- The Great Hush: two infrastructure engineers ("The Elders") shut the
  internet off on purpose. Not villains, not certain they were right. The
  game never resolves whether they were.
- The people running things now ("The Unimaginative") aren't cruel — they're
  bureaucrats reacting to broken systems, not a crusade against imagination.

## The world, level by level

- **World 1 — The Cul-de-Sac (Western-themed).** Boss: SpinCycle, a washing
  machine drum imagined as a heavyweight brawler. Built and playable.
- **World 2 — The Backyard / Dojo.** Boss: The Grasscutter, a rusted push
  mower imagined as a tengu blade-master — a two-phase fight (deliberate
  "Kata" phase, then the machine "Rev" phase). Cherry blossoms on defeat.
  Built and playable.
- **World 3+**: this is where the podcast audience's ideas come in — the
  team builds the foundation, the audience builds what comes after.

## Other characters (introduced later, Phase 2+)

- **The Cowgirl** — searching for her missing best friend.
- **The Female Ninja** — that missing best friend, though neither knows it
  yet. They find each other by both being drawn to the same place, at the
  same moment — a story beat for a future zone.
- **The Skeptic** — not a person with a backstory, more a presence: what's
  left where imagination used to be. Says one flat sentence and waits.
- **Skeptic Grunts** and **Gnome Soldiers** — recurring enemy types that
  show up across every world.

## How you play

- Solo runs, ~10-15 minutes each, roguelite-style (start fresh each run).
- Combat is dodge-parry-jump — read the enemy and react, not stamina
  management.
- Boxes are cosmetic; "Fighting Styles" (starting with Ninja and Cowboy) are
  the actual gameplay layer.
- Free to play — ads plus optional purchases, nothing pay-to-win.
- iOS + Android, landscape only.

## A note on the companion anime

There's also an anime in development (a separate project). It's a
**deliberately different universe** — in that world, The Great Hush never
happened. Don't expect the two stories to line up; they're the same themes
approached from opposite directions on purpose.
