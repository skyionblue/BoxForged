# The World Tree

---

## Lore Entry

The World Tree is three hundred years old. It grew in a field that used to be a farm, then a suburb, then nothing. The Unimaginative call it a resource. Their field reports call it "the primary cellulose yield site." Their engineers call the surrounding mills "processing infrastructure."

Kid calls it the tree.

Through the box, it looks different. The bark is dark and deep-grained, the color of a library. The roots go somewhere the eye can't follow. The canopy is wide enough that standing under it feels like standing inside a building — a building that breathes, that leans slightly into the wind, that makes a low sound when nothing is moving.

The Unimaginative didn't cut it down. That's the part that's hard to explain. They left it alive because you need a living tree to produce living cardboard. Dead tree, dead fiber, dead box — and a dead box is just packaging. They needed it alive. So they built the mills around it and left it standing in the center, ringed by grey walls and grey machines, and they took everything it produced.

The tree is still alive. Kid can feel it from a mile away. It hums through the box — not like music, like something remembering music.

Reclaiming it means: the mills open. The cardboard flows. Boxes reach every kid in every suburb on Earth who has been sitting in a quiet house since The Hush with nothing but their own mind for company — which, it turns out, is the most important thing there is.

It means the lens goes back on.

## First Glimpse Narration

It's bigger than I thought. I could see it before I could see the mills. Just the top of it, over everything. Green against grey. The Unimaginative built walls around it but they couldn't build walls tall enough. It kept growing. I stop walking. I just — look at it for a second. It hums. I can feel the hum in the box. I think the tree knows I'm here. I think it's been waiting.
