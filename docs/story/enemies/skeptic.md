# The Skeptic (Grunt Enemy)

**What they actually are:** Kids. Same age as Kid — eight, ten, twelve. Before The Great Hush, they had a phone from an early age and spent all their time there. Not the dark kind of screen — just the comfortable scroll, other people's lives at a safe remove. When The Hush came and the scroll stopped, they went outside and stood in a yard and it was just a yard. The Unimaginative found them quickly, gave them a structure and a role, and they fit. They fit perfectly.

They are not soldiers. They are not recruited with speeches. They were handed a clipboard and told what to do and they did it because it was clear and because clear is what they needed.

**What the box sees:** The same kid. Grey box, no color, no crayon — just cardboard, the way cardboard is before anyone does anything to it. Plain clothes. Nothing on them that says anything individual. In the imagination overlay, they don't transform into something else. The box doesn't have a version of them. They are already exactly what they appear to be.

That's the unsettling part. Every other enemy becomes something — the gnome becomes a soldier, the leaf pile becomes a lurker. The Skeptic is just a kid holding a clipboard.

---

## What They're Doing Here

They're not here to fight Kid. They're here to process the zone. The Unimaginative sent them to inventory, tag, and prepare whatever is in this room for removal. Kid is a disruption to that task. They are not angry about the disruption. They move to stop it the way you'd close an open window. Procedurally. Because it's in the instructions.

They genuinely cannot see what Kid sees. She swings a katana and they see a girl with a stick. She clears a room of Gnome Soldiers and they see a child knocking over ceramic garden ornaments. Nothing about their read of the situation is cruel. They are simply, completely, unequivocally right about the material facts, and the material facts are all they have.

---

## Combat Role

**Type:** Skirmisher-support. Appears in groups of two or three. Does not lead an encounter — positions at range or behind heavier enemies and applies pressure from outside melee distance.

**Weapons:** The same weapons available in the zone.

A Skeptic in the Backyard Dojo swings a bo staff. A Skeptic in the Cardboard Mill has the same grapple rope Kid finds in the Garage. Whatever Kid is fighting with, the Skeptic grunts have an equivalent. The player needs to understand this the moment they enter any zone: these kids got here first and they picked up the same things.

The difference is not the tool. It is the seeing.

Kid picks up a curtain rod and knows it is a telescoping lance — variable reach, blunt cap, surprise on the length change. A Skeptic picks up the same curtain rod and holds it like a curtain rod. They swing it at the correct angle to apply a horizontal force. They are right about everything they know about the object. They just know less than Kid does about what it is.

This is why they lose.

Not because they're weak — a Skeptic grunt with a bo staff fights with the same staff, the same weight, the same range. But Kid's version of the weapon has a name. A function. A purpose that extends beyond the material. When Kid swings it, she is doing something. When they swing it, they are applying force.

The weapon moves differently in Kid's hands. The player will feel this. Do not explain it.

**Special:** They never attack twice in a row. Between every action they take a step back and look at Kid, as if confirming she's still doing what they thought she was doing. This is their check-in behavior. It reads as hesitation but it's not — it's procedure.

**Design note — the weapon contrast:** In every zone, the Skeptic grunts carry whatever weapons are available in that world. They get the same tools Kid gets. The thematic point is in the hands — Kid's grip, her stance, the way she leads with the thing she imagines the weapon to be. The Skeptic's grip is neutral. Correct. They are holding an object with the appropriate amount of force for its weight. They are completely right about it. They just don't know what it is.

## Behavioral Notes (for animators / designers)

Skeptics don't patrol. They work. When the player enters a room, they are already doing something — reading a sticker, affixing a tag, cross-referencing a document. They don't notice Kid right away. There is a beat where Kid enters and the Skeptic is just standing there with a clipboard, doing a job.

When they do notice her, they don't change posture. They don't flinch, don't go wide. They just turn to face her and begin moving toward her with the same purposeful, unhurried stride they use for everything else. The disturbing part is the pace. A kid should run. They walk.

They move in the careful way of someone who has been trained not to run — because running means panic and panic is not in the instructions. They step around obstacles rather than through them. They give wide berth to other enemies in their way, not from fear, just from not wanting to disrupt them. They are extraordinarily polite to everything in the room except the disruption.

When Kid parries them, they stumble back and look at her. Not at their own hands. At her. Like they're updating an assessment.

When they fall, they drop the weapon first. Then they sit down — not dramatically, just suddenly not standing anymore. The weapon sits next to them. It is just an object again. It was always just an object. Seeing them put it down makes the difference visible in a way that fighting didn't.

---

## Audio

- No combat shouts. No war cries. If they make a sound during a swing it's an exhale — effort, not aggression.
- On hit: a short, flat sound. Not a grunt of pain. More like the sound of something being noted.
- Weapon impact: matches the zone's weapon sound profile — same audio as Kid's equivalent weapon, slightly flatter in tone. No imagined resonance. The same stick makes a different sound in Kid's hands.
- When they fall: silence first. Then the weapon hitting the floor. The weapon sound comes after.
- They never call for help. They don't have a call-for-help behavior. That's not in the instructions.

## Sample Lines

*(Said during combat, flat, matter-of-fact, not threatening)*

"You're not supposed to be here."

"Stop."

"This has already been processed."

"You're making this harder."

*(On taking a hit — said without anger, more like correcting a record)*

"That doesn't help."

*(Said while regathering their scattered papers)*

"One moment."

## What Their Presence Means

A room with Skeptics means the Unimaginative have assigned personnel. This zone is actively being processed — not just held, not abandoned, but worked on right now. Someone with a manifest decided this room was worth a crew.

The Skeptics didn't choose to be here because they want to stop Kid. They were assigned here because they fit the task. They're good at the task. If the task were something else, they would be equally good at that.

That is the most honest and most difficult thing about fighting them: they're not wrong. They were given a job that plays to their actual skills, and they are doing it well, and the job is erasure.
