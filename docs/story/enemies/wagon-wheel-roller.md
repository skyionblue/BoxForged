# Wagon Wheel Roller

**Zone:** The Cul-de-Sac

**What it actually is:** A wagon wheel. Old cast iron, the kind from a decorative yard display — one of several that had been propped against the fence at the end of the cul-de-sac for years as lawn art. Nobody ever used them. Nobody ever moved them. They just stood there, slowly rusting into the grass, becoming part of the fence line.

The Unimaginative tagged the display and left the wheels in place: no cardboard, no fiber content, not worth removing. Three months of stillness in a grey neighborhood. Then: rolling.

**What the box sees:** A wheel-knight — a thick iron disc with a dented hub for a face and spokes that extend like arms when it rolls. At speed, the spokes blur. It is enormous in motion and almost nothing at rest. At rest, it's just a wheel leaned against a fence.

---

## Lore

It used to be decorative. That is the thing about the Wagon Wheel Roller — it was never a tool, never a vehicle part, never something with a job. It was already an object pretending to be from somewhere else. The imagination read it immediately. The Unimaginative looked at it and saw a liability: untagged, unsecured, rolling.

They were right about the rolling.

---

## Combat Role

**Type:** Tank/rusher. Its primary threat is kinetic — it builds speed crossing the room and hits with everything it has gathered on the way. Up close it is manageable. At range it is a problem. The player needs to learn the angle: a direct approach is certain impact. A sidestep at the last half-second puts the Roller past Kid and into the wall, where it takes a full second to reorient.

**Special:** On contact it does not simply stop — it reverses the spin. The moment after a missed rush is a bounce, which means the reorientation window is shorter than it looks. The player who dodges left expects the Roller to hit the wall and stay there. It doesn't. It is already coming back.

At range, the Roller can fire one of its spokes as a thrown projectile — a single long spoke that skips across the ground before lifting slightly at the end. Readable with a half-second of attention. The tell is the hub slowing its spin before the throw.

---

## Behavioral Notes (for animators / designers)

The Wagon Wheel Roller does not idle — it sways. Even stationary, it rocks on its edge, back and forth, the hub tracking Kid's position. It is not threatening. It is patient. The sway communicates that it has done this before, that rolling is what it does, and that it is simply waiting for the distance to close before doing it again.

When it begins its rush: no windup. No signal. One moment it is swaying, the next it is in motion. The only tell is the sway stopping — a half-beat of perfect stillness before the roll. The player has to learn to read the stillness.

When defeated: it doesn't shatter or collapse. It runs out of momentum. The spin slows. The hub droops. It leans sideways and settles into the grass with the sound of an iron ring coming to rest. What it is now is what it always was: a wheel, lying on its side, in a yard.

---

## Audio

- Rolling: a steady iron-on-ground sound, rising in pitch as speed increases
- At rest: the creak of something heavy settling
- Spoke throw: a short whistle, then a low skip-skip-skip on the ground before impact
- Defeat: the long slow ring of cast iron going still

---

## Sample Lines

The Wagon Wheel Roller does not speak. It has no voice, no bark. It has momentum.

---

## What Its Presence Means

A Wagon Wheel Roller in a room means the Unimaginative's grip on this zone is active but unpolished — the tools they're using are improvised, environmental. The Roller wasn't assigned here by a manifest. It just started moving, and The Unimaginative recognized it as useful, and let it be useful. This is the Cul-de-Sac: the first zone, the outer edge of the controlled territory, the place where the organization's authority is present but its resources are thin.

The Roller is what you send when you don't have anything better yet.
