# Leaf Pile Lurker

**What it actually is:** A pile of dead leaves, raked into a heap and never bagged.

**What the kid imagines it as:** A slumped humanoid shape made of curled brown leaves — no face, just the suggestion of shoulders and a bowed head. It comes apart a little every time it moves, and gathers itself back up. It is sad more than it is scary.

## Lore

Somebody raked these. You can tell. The pile is too neat to be an accident — somebody made it on a Saturday, meaning to come back with bags, and then didn't. Then nobody did. And the pile sat through the fall, and the winter, and it stopped being leaves that were going to get bagged and started being a thing that was waiting.

It doesn't want to fight. That's the part that gets you. It waits, buried and still, until you're close, and then it rises because rising is the only thing it knows how to do anymore — the way you'd reach for a phone that isn't there. It hits you because you're the first thing to come near in a long time. When it falls, it just comes apart. Leaves everywhere. Like it was always about to.

The Unimaginative didn't build these. They just stopped raking. That's all it takes. Leave a place alone long enough and it forgets it was ever tended.

## Behavioral Notes (for animators / designers)

Dormant, it is a leaf pile and nothing more — no glow, no breathing, no tell. It should read as set dressing until it doesn't. When the player is funneled close, it rises *slowly*, unsteady, leaves sloughing off the shoulders and swirling back up — a thing assembling itself out of habit. Its attack is a single clumsy overhand collapse, more of a fall onto the player than a strike. There is no menace in the wind-up, only a kind of tired inevitability. When killed, it doesn't die dramatically — it *scatters*, and for a half-second the leaves hang in the air before settling, and then it's just a pile again. Let that beat land. It's the saddest thing in the room.

## Audio

- No words. It can't. Just a dry rustle — leaves shifting — as it rises, and a soft collapsing *whumph* when it falls apart.
- The rustle should sound almost like a sigh if you're not listening closely. Don't confirm that it is one.
