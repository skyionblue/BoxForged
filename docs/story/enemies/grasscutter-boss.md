# The Grasscutter — Boss

**What it actually is:** A rusted push reel-mower, abandoned in the tall grass at the back of the yard.

**What the kid imagines it as:** A drum-chested tengu blade-master. The reel of curved blades is its spinning heart. The push-handle folds behind it like a pair of war-wings. The wheels are heavy iron sandals. When it moves, it shakes petals loose from the tree, and it never seems to be in a hurry, because it doesn't have to be.

## Lore

Something used to cut this grass. Every week, straight lines, back and forth, the good sound of a Saturday. Then the lines stopped, and the grass came up past the blades, and the blades rusted in place mid-turn, and the mower sat down in the green and became part of it.

The Unimaginative posted it here for "lot clearance." That's the word on the tag still tied to the handle. They don't see a blade-master. They see a tool that stopped working and a job that needs finishing — the finishing being the whole backyard, cut down to wet concrete, petals to ash. It has been waiting in the tall grass to finish the job. It is very good at cutting things down. That was always what it was for.

But this is the place where kids *learned* — where a stick first became a sword and someone stood up straight and meant it. A blade-master understands that better than anyone. That's why it fights like an honorable duel first, before it becomes the thing The Unimaginative made it into. Somewhere in the rust it still remembers being tended. The fight is whether it remembers in time.

## Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — Kata.** It fights like a teacher: deliberate, honest tells, one lesson at a time. Every attack is a thing you're meant to learn to answer. It is not trying to end you. It is testing whether you belong here.

**Phase 2 — Rev.** The reel stops being a heart and becomes an engine. This is The Unimaginative's version of it — the tool, the clearance detail, the job that needs finishing. No more lessons. It cuts lanes across the whole court and drags the ground out from under you. The honor is gone. What's left is just the machine doing what it was told.

## Defeat Line

The reel jams. Grinds. Stops.

A gust comes up out of nowhere and takes every loose petal with it, and for one held breath the whole court is pink in the air.

Then the cherry tree — the little one, the one too young to be here — opens all at once into full bloom.
