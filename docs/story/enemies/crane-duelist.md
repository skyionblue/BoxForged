# Crane Duelist

**What it actually is:** A plastic pink lawn flamingo on a single bent-wire leg, staked in the grass.

**What the kid imagines it as:** A one-legged spear duelist in crane stance — pink-lacquered armor, a wide conical straw hat, balanced on one thin leg, a long beak-spear held level and still. Elegant. Patient. Waiting for a real opponent.

## Lore

It has stood on one leg, in this exact spot, for years. The others left. The others got knocked over, or hauled away, or gave up and lay down in the weeds. This one stayed standing. That takes practice. That takes wanting it.

It does not chase. Chasing is for things that haven't decided what they are. It waits for you to come into range, turns to face you, and offers a duel — one thrust, honest and long. If you read it, the whole fight is yours. If you don't, it will remind you, once, that it has had a very long time to think about balance.

It does not miss twice.

## Behavioral Notes (for animators / designers)

Idle, it is almost sculpture — the head tracks the player in slow, deliberate turns, but the body barely shifts on that one leg. This is a thing that has learned to hold still for a long time. When the player crosses into range, it does not lunge; it *settles* — weight sinks, hat tilts down, the beak-spear draws back level. That's the tell. It wants the duel to be fair, and it wants you to see it coming, because a duel you didn't see coming isn't a duel.

If the player circles behind, the crane reads it as bad manners and answers with the Pivot Sweep — a fast, low, spinning kick of the spear. Not elegant. Almost annoyed. After a full thrust it is off-balance on that one leg for a beat, wobbling, the hat askew — the only time it looks like plastic on a wire instead of a swordsman. That's the counter window. That's when it remembers what it really is.

## Audio

- "Again." *(flat, patient — a sparring instruction, not a threat. It wants another pass.)*
- A single hollow tap — the wire leg planting for a thrust — then the whistle of the beak-spear cutting air.
