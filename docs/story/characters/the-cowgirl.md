# The Cowgirl

## Backstory

She had a phone. She got it for her ninth birthday — the same one she asked for, the exact color, with the case she'd picked out, and she loved it the way kids love the first thing that feels entirely theirs. She used it to watch videos of horses. She used it to text her best friend things that were easier to type than to say. She used it to take pictures of everything, because she was the kind of kid who noticed things and wanted to keep them.

The Hush took it all at once.

The first three days, she cried — not only from grief, which surprised her. Some of it was something she couldn't name: a weight lifting that she'd been so used to she didn't know it was there. She'd been reachable, all the time, by everyone. She hadn't noticed what that cost.

On the fourth day, she found the box in the garage. Her dad's old hat boxes, from when he wore hats. Brown cardboard, small, the proportions were wrong. She cut it down. She drew a brim with a fat marker. She drew a star — tried to make it look like something official, badge-shaped. Then she put it on.

She doesn't talk about what she saw. She doesn't have to. She picked up the lasso that was already in the garage — clothesline rope, coiled — and walked outside.

She grieves the phone sometimes, still. Not the phone, exactly. Some of the things on it. Her best friend's number, which she no longer has. She's looking for her. That's not the whole reason she's fighting, but it's in there.

## Personality Portrait

Where the Kid is forward momentum, the Cowgirl is deliberate weight. She takes in a situation before she moves through it. She notices things — the lean of a fence post, the sound of an enemy before it rounds the corner, the way the grass is different on one side of the yard. Her observation is tactical, but it's also something more than that. She sees.

She is warm in a way the Kid isn't quite, yet. She checks on things. After a fight, she looks back at what she's left. She is the one who notices when something's been reclaimed — the color returning to a wall, a plant straightening. She doesn't always say it. But she sees it.

She is decisive without being reckless. She gathers information, she forms a read, and then she goes. She is not patient in the passive sense — she's patient the way a coiled rope is patient. Still until it isn't.

The difference between the Cowgirl and the Kid: the Kid never doubted this world. The Cowgirl chose it. She knows what she gave up to be here. She's here anyway. That gives her a gravity the Kid doesn't have yet.

## Inner Voice

Things the Cowgirl thinks but doesn't say out loud:

"I had pictures of this street on my phone. I remember how it looked when it was alive. That's what I'm putting back."

"He just runs toward things. I don't know if that's brave or if he hasn't learned to be careful yet. Probably both."

"The rope has weight. When you throw it, you know where it's going. I like that. I like things that go where you put them."

"She's out there. I'll find her. I'll find her after this part."
