# The Skeptic

## What the Skeptic Is

The Skeptic is not a person who lost their imagination. The Skeptic is what happens to the space where imagination used to be, given a shape and a box and a direction.

There is no wound in the Skeptic. No tragedy, no formative moment of disillusionment. Disillusionment implies you were once illusioned — that something was taken from you. The Skeptic never had it to lose. They are the absence of a thing, wearing a box.

The box is grey. Unpainted, undecorated, the original corrugated brown bleached entirely out. Not because someone painted it grey — because nothing was ever added. The Skeptic is what cardboard looks like when no one picks it up.

They move through the world at a slight remove from it. Quietly. They are not in a hurry. They do not need to be. They are waiting for the imagination to run out on its own, and they believe it always does. They've watched it happen. Every kid who eventually stopped playing. Every adult who forgot how. They've been watching this particular thing end for a long time.

They are not cruel. Cruelty requires caring. They simply note, clearly and correctly (they believe), that none of this is real. They do not understand why that needs to be said so many times. They say it anyway.

## What the Skeptic Wants

Not to win. Winning implies a contest. The Skeptic wants the record to be correct. When the imagination flickers out — and it will — they want it noted that they said so. They want the Kid to have been warned. They are not gloating in advance. They are being accurate.

When the Skeptic's presence drains the color from the world, they are not doing it deliberately. They are simply — there. And their presence is enough.

## How the Skeptic Moves

Slowly. Not menacingly — there's nothing theatrical about it. They walk the way someone walks who has nowhere particular to be. They arrive, they observe, they speak, they wait. When they attack, it is less like aggression and more like a correction being applied.

They do not pursue. If the Kid runs, they let him. They'll be here when he comes back. They're always here.

## What the Skeptic Says

Flat. One sentence. No follow-up. Leave silence after. The silence is the second blow.

"That's a stick."

"You'll stop eventually."

"I've seen this before."

"None of that happened."

"It was always just a backyard."
