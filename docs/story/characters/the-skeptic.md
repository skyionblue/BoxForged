# The Skeptic

**Version 3.0 — Revised 2026-08-07**
*Major correction: Skeptics are a recurring grunt enemy fought throughout every level.*
*This file covers both the enemy type (Skeptics, plural) and the recurring individual (The One Who Keeps Watching).*

---

## Canonical Weapons Decision

**Skeptic grunts use zone-appropriate weapons — the same weapons Kid finds in each zone.** Not clipboards. Not document stacks. Those are their job tools, visible when they are working, but when they fight they pick up what is available: a bo staff in the Dojo, a grapple rope in the Garage, a waterwhip in the Mill. The difference is not the tool. It is the seeing. Kid picks up a curtain rod and knows it is a telescoping lance. A Skeptic picks up the same curtain rod and holds it like a curtain rod. They are right about everything they know about the object. They just know less than Kid does about what it is.

Full combat and audio design: `docs/story/enemies/skeptic.md`.

---

## What Skeptics Are

They are kids.

Same age as Kid — eight, ten, twelve. Before The Great Hush, they had a phone from an early age. Parents gave them one early, which felt like a gift, which is what made it so effective. They spent their afternoons in a screen. Not the bad kind — just the endless comfortable scroll. Videos of other kids doing things. Content about things without ever touching the things. A hundred people showing you a sunset so you never had to find one yourself.

When The Hush came, those kids went outside. They stood in a yard. They looked at it.

It was just a yard.

Not loss, not silence where something used to be. Just a yard, correctly observed. They didn't grieve the imagination because they hadn't known it was there to grieve. You don't miss what you never learned to see.

The Unimaginative found them quickly. Didn't recruit them with speeches. Just came in with structure and a role and something to do that made sense. And the kids who couldn't see what Kid sees — they fit. Completely. Naturally. The Unimaginative world is organized and clear and asks nothing of you that isn't already written down. For a kid who spent years absorbing other people's lives through a screen, a clipboard and a clear set of instructions felt almost like home.

## How to Write a Skeptic

They are not villains. They are not cruel. They do not enjoy stopping Kid. They are doing a job that plays to the only skills they have, and they are doing it well.

**Voice:** Flat and informational. One sentence at a time. Never an exclamation. Never a question unless it's procedural.

**Intent:** They want Kid to stop disrupting the zone so they can finish their task. That's all. They are not invested in her failing. They just have something to do and she's in the way of it.

**The gap:** They see a girl swinging a stick at garden ornaments. They see a child running through a backyard full of leaves. They are not reading the imagination layer — they can't. They are working from the material facts only, and the material facts describe a disruption in an area that has been tagged for processing.

See `docs/story/enemies/skeptic.md` for combat role, behavioral notes, audio, and sample lines.

## The One Who Keeps Watching

*This section covers a specific, individual Skeptic — not to be confused with the grunt type.*

Among all the Skeptics assigned to all the zones, one keeps appearing where they weren't assigned.

They first show up in the Cul-de-Sac. They show up again at the Backyard koi pond. And the Garage workbench. And the School Hallway. And every zone after, in a quiet room after the fight is over, in a doorway or a corner or just standing at the edge of the space Kid just reclaimed.

They don't have a clipboard. They're not processing anything. They just stand there and look at what Kid did and then they say one sentence and then they go.

They are not a boss. They do not fight. The one-who-keeps-watching is strictly a non-combat encounter — they appear after the room is clear, they say their line, and they leave before Kid can do anything about it.

This is a different behavior from every other Skeptic. Every other Skeptic in every other room is there to complete a task. This one is not completing anything. They are just coming back, zone after zone, to look.

The player will notice by the third zone. They will not know what to make of it. Neither will Kid, in the fiction. There is no explanation. The game does not flag this as significant. It just happens, the same way a sound keeps repeating at the edge of a dream — often enough that you know it's real, not often enough to name it.

## The Grey Box

Grey. Unpainted. No crayon marks, no drawn-on windows, no name in marker along the side. Not damaged — just never touched by any of that. It is what Kid's box looked like before Kid found it.

Except nobody found this one.

---

## The Line

Flat. One sentence. No follow-up. Leave silence after. The silence is the second blow.

The one-who-keeps-watching does not say anything about imagination. They name something in the material world and let Kid understand what it implies. They are not making an argument. Arguments mean the outcome is uncertain. They state a fact.

What they never say: anything about pretending. About games. About whether any of this is real. Those are arguments, and they don't argue.

**By zone:**

**Cul-de-Sac:**
"Somebody was going to bag those leaves."

**Backyard Dojo (Koi Pond):**
"Nobody's coming back for the chairs."

**The Garage:**
"Nothing left to hang there."

**The School Hallway:**
"School's closed."

**The Basement:**
"Nobody's coming back to play this."

**The Attic:**
"This was all going to be thrown out anyway."

**The Cardboard Mill:**
"They were going to build something here."

**The World Tree:**
*(No line. They appear near the trunk, in the quiet after the fighting is done. They stand there. Their hands are open at their sides. They look at the tree. They do not speak.)*

*The silence at the World Tree is not dramatic. It is the one-who-keeps-watching, for the first time, having nothing correct to say. The game does not flag this. They leave. Where they were standing: a small patch of moss. Green. Already there. Just visible now that they've moved.*

---

## How the One-Who-Keeps-Watching Moves

Like a kid who has somewhere specific to be, but who has learned that nothing urgent ever helped. Not slow with menace. Slow with completion — the way you move when the argument is already settled in your mind.

They arrive. They look at what Kid did with something that isn't contempt — contempt would require finding it interesting enough to oppose. They look. They say one sentence. They leave.

They do not pursue. They do not need to. The line they leave behind does their work for them.

They always leave behind one grey thing. Not deliberately. It just happens wherever they stand. The petals stop falling on that spot. The color goes flat at the edge. When they go, the grey thing stays — and Kid has to step around it, or past it, or over it.

She never says anything about it. But she keeps half-looking at it.

## The Design Question This Corrects

*Previous version described The Skeptic as a single individual who appeared dramatically once per zone, delivered a line, and left a grey object. That model was built before the decision that Skeptics are a grunt enemy type.*

The correction: Skeptics (plural) are combat grunts found throughout every level. They work for the Unimaginative as zone-processing personnel. They have combat roles, behavioral notes, and audio design — see `docs/story/enemies/skeptic.md`.

The one-who-keeps-watching is a distinct narrative encounter layered on top of the grunt type. They appear once per zone, post-combat, non-fight, and are the same individual each time. They wear the same grey box the grunts wear, but they aren't part of the processing crew. The player learns to read the difference by feel: the grunts have clipboards and tasks; this one just stands and looks.

The narrative weight of the recurring individual is preserved. The zone-clearing combat rhythm is preserved. They coexist without contradiction.

## What This Means for Kid

Before this was understood clearly: Kid was fighting an organization.

Now: Kid is also, repeatedly, fighting kids who look like the one-who-keeps-watching. Kids who can't see it. Kids who were given a screen too early and a clipboard too late. She's clearing rooms of them, level after level.

She doesn't articulate this. She just keeps going.

But the question the player carries by the end is not "will Kid win?" The player believes Kid will win. The question becomes: *what happens to the kids who can't see it?*

That question is what gives the ending its weight.

The adult narration in the ending describes a kid two thousand miles away putting a box on her head and deciding her room is a castle.

The player will wonder, without being told to wonder, whether the one-who-keeps-watching is still in a room somewhere.

Whether, with the mills running again and cardboard moving again, they might pick one up.

The game doesn't say. That silence is the ending doing its job.
