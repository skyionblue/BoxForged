# The Kid

## Backstory

Before the box, he was already the kind of kid who made things. Cardboard shields from Amazon deliveries. Rubber-band catapults. Balsa-wood swords that snapped on the first swing, and he'd just make another one. His parents never bought him a tablet. They never explained why — they'd just redirect him. Outside. Kitchen table. Here's some tape. He resented it, sometimes, the way you resent not getting something everyone else has when you don't yet understand what it cost them.

The day The Great Hush happened, he was in the backyard trying to build a trebuchet from PVC pipe and a plastic bin. He heard a distant noise — not a bang, more like a held breath being released. Then his neighbor stopped mowing. The guy across the street sat down in his driveway. His mom came outside and looked at her phone for a long time and then put it in her pocket and didn't take it out again.

He didn't fully understand what had happened. He went back to the trebuchet.

Two days later, he found the box. It was in the recycling bin — oversized, brown, the kind that held a small appliance. He picked it up because it was the right size to be a shield. He turned it over. Cut the flap corners with a pocketknife. He looked through the hole, and the backyard looked different through it. Not literally — the same grass, the same fence, the same dead apple tree. But he saw it as something, the way you see a cloud as a rabbit. And then the rabbit started moving.

He ran inside for markers. He's been running somewhere ever since.

## Personality Portrait

The Kid does not perform enthusiasm. He is genuinely, fundamentally inside whatever he's doing. When he fights a Gnome Soldier, he's not pretending it's a battle — it is a battle. When he calls the cul-de-sac a main street, he's not being creative — he's reporting what he sees. The box does not make him imaginative. It reveals that he always was.

He is not precocious. He doesn't have clever things to say about the world. He has conclusions, stated simply, and then he acts on them. He sees the shape of a situation and moves. This can read as fearlessness, but it isn't. It's closer to not having time to be afraid when there's something that needs doing.

He will not be talked out of anything by someone who hasn't tried it. The Skeptic's words land — you can see them land — but they do not stop him. He pauses. He looks at what he's fighting for. He keeps going.

What drives him: he wants the apple tree to have its blossoms back. He doesn't articulate it that way. He wouldn't know how. But that's what he's moving toward. Everything that's gone grey — every flat quiet place that used to be alive — he wants it back. He knows, somehow, that it's still there underneath.

## Inner Monologue

Things the Kid thinks but doesn't say out loud:

"I know this is the right way. I don't know how I know."

"If I can see it, it's real. Everything else is just people who stopped looking."

"The house on the corner used to have a tire swing. Someone took it down. I'm going to find out where they put it."

"I'm not scared. I'm. I'm — okay, a little scared. But I'm going anyway."
