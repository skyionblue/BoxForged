# The Female Ninja

## Backstory

She was training before The Hush. That's the thing about her that nobody fully understands, including her. She'd been doing it for years — forms in the backyard at five in the morning before school, falling down a hill to learn how to roll, learning to be quiet in a room. She'd watched videos to learn technique, then gone outside to practice what she'd seen until her knees hurt and the ground was too dark to see. She did not fully know why. She had no teacher, no dojo, no tournament in her future. She was just — training.

The phone went with The Hush, but she barely noticed. She'd already absorbed what she needed from it. The repetition was in her body.

The box she found was small and black — already, somehow, exactly right. She didn't draw on it much. Three lines. That was enough.

What The Hush clarified for her: she'd been preparing for something. She knew that now. The abstract urgency she'd never been able to name had a name. The World Tree. The grey spreading zone by zone. The cardboard that needed protecting. She'd been training for exactly this, and she'd had no way to know until it happened.

She does not marvel at this. She notes it. She gets to work.

## Personality Portrait

She is the quietest of the three. Not shy — quiet is different. She speaks when she has something specific to say, and she almost always has something specific to say when she does. She doesn't fill space. In a world generating so much noise about what's been lost, she is still.

Her way of seeing is precise. She notices the load-bearing thing, the structural truth, the hinge point of a situation. In a fight, she reads the enemy's rhythm before it swings. In a room, she reads the exits before the threats. She does not explain her process. She just moves correctly.

She admires the Kid, though she would not use that word, or any word. What she'd think, looking at her: she goes. She just goes. She's spent years cultivating stillness, but she recognizes that her kind of forward movement is its own skill, not a deficit. She suspects she doesn't know that yet.

The Cowgirl she trusts. That's rarer. She can't always articulate why she trusts someone, but she trusts the Cowgirl's observation — she knows the Cowgirl sees things before acting, and that's the quality she respects most.

What she'd never admit about either of them: they make the work feel less alone. Not because she needs company. Because when you've been training in the dark for years without knowing why, and suddenly you know why, and you're not the only one — that matters. She just won't say so.

## Inner Voice

Things the Female Ninja thinks but doesn't say out loud:

"I knew what I was doing. I didn't know what it was for. Now I do. That's enough."

"She moves like she's not afraid of being wrong. I've been trying to unlearn that training for years. Maybe I've been training the wrong thing."

"The pattern has a gap. It always has a gap. Be still. Find the gap. Move."

"I wasn't lonely. But this is better than not lonely."
