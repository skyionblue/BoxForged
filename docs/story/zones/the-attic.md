# Zone Lore — The Attic

**World 6.** Reality: an attic above a house — low-slung rafters, plywood flooring, pink insulation batting between the ribs of the roof. Cardboard boxes everywhere, stacked four high in some places, labeled in permanent marker, most labels wrong or outdated. Christmas decorations. A broken fan. Three lamps, none matching. A rocking chair with one spindle missing. Baby clothes in a bin labeled SPRING CLOTHES. A folding table. A suitcase nobody opened after the last trip. And in the far corner, behind the water heater: something else. Not boxes of things. An empty space, cleared deliberately, with one cardboard box still on the floor — the kind you sit on, the kind you keep. The floor around it is scratched clean. Like someone came up here often, for a long time, and did something in that corner that they didn't do anywhere else.

Imagination: a Wizard's Tower — not the magical kind, not the movie kind. The old kind, the accumulated kind. Bookshelves that go past the ceiling into somewhere else. Towers of labeled archive boxes that are also the architecture — the walls are made of things kept. The lamps, in the imagined space, all glow warm amber. Every one of them, even the broken ones. The rocking chair is still. The insulation between the rafters is copper-red, the color of embers. The whole space smells of old paper and something underneath it, something the paper was trying to preserve.

The cleared corner in the back: that's where the tower goes highest. The walls thin out there. The cardboard thins out. There's just the ceiling — and above the ceiling, open sky, and the rafters framing it like a window.

Someone stood there and looked at that.

THE ATTIC

## Zone Identity

The Attic is where things go that people couldn't let go and couldn't keep looking at.

There is a difference between the Basement and the Attic. The Basement had things in it that were being used — records that were played, carpet that was walked on, a pedal that was pressed. The things in the basement were mid-sentence. The things in the attic are end-of-sentence. Not discarded. Not forgotten. Just — moved. Filed. Placed somewhere that isn't here, where life is happening, where things have to be done.

The boxes are labeled wrong not because nobody cared. Because people care in waves, and by the time you labeled the box the wave had passed and you were approximating. WINTER STUFF. KITCHEN STUFF. MOM'S THINGS. The label is a gesture toward caring, which is not the same as caring fully, but which is much closer to caring than the things in a dumpster.

The Unimaginative came up here and found no cardboard in manufacturing quantity. They tagged fourteen boxes as Waste Paper and removed nothing — the manifest error rate was too high. They took photographs. They left.

They didn't see the corner.

Kid comes up here and the box reads this as a tower of accumulated knowing — every kept thing a book on a shelf, every labeled box a chapter in a record of a life. The wizard who lived in this tower didn't do magic tricks. The wizard kept things because they understood that keeping things is its own kind of knowledge. The things you hold onto tell you what you are.

In the corner: the space that somebody cleared. No boxes there. Just floor, swept. Just a cardboard box sitting in the center of it, alone. Just the ceiling thin above it and the sky beyond.

What Kid understands, the moment she sees it: whoever cleared that space knew what they were doing. They didn't stumble into it. They chose it.

## Enemy Roster

### The Wrapping Golems

*(Rooms 1, 2, 3 — standard encounter enemy)*

**What they actually are:** The Christmas decoration boxes. Specifically the ones that weren't opened last year, or the year before — packed too carefully, or the holidays went differently than expected, or someone didn't come home. The tissue paper is still inside them, folded exactly the way it was when it was packed. The tape is still holding. The seasonal decorations inside are still arranged the way someone who cared very much about packing Christmas decorations arranged them.

The Unimaginative tagged SEASONAL ITEMS as low-priority assets. They left them stacked by the water heater.

They've been there, waiting for December, and December didn't come.

**What the box sees:** Thick, slow-moving forms wrapped in layers — not armor, *packaging*. Folded corners and tape strips catching the lamplight. They move with the deliberate weight of something that has been very carefully stored and is very reluctant to be disturbed. Each one holds something inside it — a bulge at the chest, the shape of a glass ornament or a metal star, just visible through the cardboard. They protect it without meaning to. It's just how they're built.

**Role:** Tank/slow attacker. They hit hard when they commit. They take time to commit. The windup is long — almost theatrical — like someone drawing back to swing something they've never swung before. The window opens during the windup. Close it and they miss. Don't close it and they hit with everything they've been holding for a year and a half.

**Behavioral Note:** Wrapping Golems do not patrol. They stand where they were placed. They face the direction they were facing when they were stacked. When Kid enters a room, the nearest Golem turns first — a slow rotation, joints stiff, packaging crinkling audibly. Then the next. Then the next. Like someone finding a light in the dark — one by one, orienting toward it.

They don't rush. They walk. The walk is steady and absolutely unwilling to deviate. If something is between them and Kid, they push through it — boxes, props, each other. They were designed to hold their shape no matter what's inside. They still are.

When defeated: the outer layers shed like tissue, drifting to the floor. What's inside slides out: a glass ornament, still intact. A paper star. A wooden reindeer, wrapped in tissue, exactly right. They land on the stone floor undamaged.

**Barks:** None. The rustle of tissue. The crack of stressed cardboard. That's all.

### The Dust Motes

*(Rooms 1, 2, 4 — swarmer/harasser)*

**What they actually are:** The insulation batting. Pink fiberglass insulation stuffed between every rafter — fluffy, weightless when new, compressed and settling after years in the heat of a summer attic. It isn't dangerous. You're not supposed to breathe it for hours. A minute is fine. Everyone says a minute is fine.

The Unimaginative saw it and marked it EXISTING INFRASTRUCTURE. Left it in place.

The heat from the lamps — all the lamps, all on — has been agitating it.

**What the box sees:** A cloud, roughly. A slow, hovering scatter of amber-lit particles that moves together without moving like a flock — more like a ripple in water, the wave-pattern underneath. They drift toward Kid. They get in her eyes. They obscure her read on the room. They are not trying to hurt her. They don't have the density for it. They are just — in the way, constantly, hovering at face level, getting between Kid and what she needs to see.

**Role:** Vision-obscurer/distractor. They reduce Kid's ability to telegraph enemy attacks — the tells are still there but harder to read through the haze. Individually, one cluster is manageable. Combined with a Wrapping Golem windup, they become the actual threat: the Golem's tell is visible for three seconds. If the Motes are in Kid's face for two of those three seconds, the window is half as long.

**Behavioral Note:** Dust Motes don't attack. They drift. The drift is random at first, then increasingly directional once Kid starts fighting — moving toward the loudest thing in the room, which is Kid. They don't coordinate with the Golems. The Golems don't coordinate with them. They just both happen to be in the room, doing what they do, and the result is harder than either would be alone.

**What clears them:** Movement. Run through a cluster and it disperses. It reforms in twelve seconds. If Kid stays moving, the Motes can't settle on her. If she stops to plan, they close in.

**Barks:** A faint hiss. Static. No words.

### The Suitcase Stalker

*(Rooms 3 and 4 — elite. One per room.)*

**What it actually is:** The suitcase. The one nobody opened after the last trip — it's been up here for almost a year with the same things in it that were in it when it was packed. Not because the trip went badly. Because when it was time to unpack, it felt easier to close the attic door. And then easier again. The wheels still roll. The extend handle still works. It's in very good condition. It wasn't ready to be up here.

**What the box sees:** A relentless, wheeled pursuer — low to the ground, handle extended, wheels moving with a smoothness that nothing in this room should have. It rolls. It does not stop rolling. It circles. It reapproaches. It is not fast, but it is continuous — there is no idle state, no rest, no moment when it decides not to close the distance. It is perpetually in the act of arriving.

**Role:** Persistent pressure. It doesn't have a powerful attack. It has one attack — a full-weight diagonal rush from five feet — but the threat is that it always comes back to five feet. Clear it away and it's back. Deal with a Golem and the Suitcase is already behind Kid again. The room cannot be cleared until it's defeated because it will not stay cleared.

**Enemy Behavior Note — for animators:**
The Suitcase Stalker moves like something that was never not-moving — not like an animal that decides to walk, but like a wheel that was always rolling and was only briefly stopped. Its handle is extended. It faces Kid exactly. When it rushes: the handle retracts, the suitcase goes horizontal, leading with its widest face. After impact or miss: handle extends again, reorients. Five seconds. It's coming back.

The most important moment: when Kid defeats another enemy, the Suitcase Stalker pauses. Half a second. Its wheels stop. The handle lowers slightly — not fully. Then it comes again.

It is not grieving. It doesn't have the capacity. But something in that pause reads like a system that registered a change and isn't sure what to do next. A pause in the middle of continuous motion is the loudest thing in the room.

**Barks:** Wheel-roll on stone. The extension click of the handle. Nothing else.

### The Cleared Corner

*(Room 5 — Final Room, before the boss door)*

The corner has been cleared of everything that wasn't placed intentionally.

What's there:

A cardboard box, sitting on the floor, alone. Not a box of things. A box someone sat on, and worked from, and wore down at the corners by sitting on it often. The corners are rounded. The bottom is soft with use. The sides have the particular deformation of a box that was a seat for a long time. Kid-sized, if the kid was a little older than Kid.

Around it on the floor, marks from something that was placed here often and removed: the small rectangular ghost of a toolbox, or a stack of books, or a chest — something wooden, positioned in front of the box like a desk.

On the rafter directly above the box: a mark, made with marker. Not a word. A symbol — a box shape, simple, with a small crown drawn above it. Not fancy. Not decorated. Like something someone drew quickly to mark a place, the way you put your name on something that belongs to you.

Kid looks at it for a beat.

She doesn't say what she thinks it means.

### The Marks on the Boxes

*(Scattered throughout — Rooms 1, 2, 3. Environmental detail.)*

Several of the stacked boxes in the attic have a small mark on them — the same crown-above-a-box symbol, stamped rather than drawn. Simple. Like a seal.

The marks are all on one side of the boxes. The side facing the cleared corner.

Someone was here often enough to mark the boxes they looked at. The marks face toward where they sat.

Kid notices this in Room 2. She doesn't say it out loud. She just looks at one box, then another, then another. All facing the same direction.

She looks toward the corner. She can't see it from here.

She keeps moving.

### Silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is the sixth time the player sees this kid. Every time before, they've been standing with some clarity of posture — still, certain, somewhere specific to be.

In the Attic, they put their hand on the box.

That's the only time the Skeptic has touched anything.

The animator should not underline it. The hand goes down. The hand comes up. The one who keeps watching leaves.

**The read for the player:** They've been watching someone say one flat sentence and leave for six zones. This time, the Skeptic paused before speaking. This time, they touched something.

The player doesn't know what to do with that. Neither does Kid.

Neither does the Skeptic, if there's anything underneath the surface they're allowed to not-know-what-to-do-with. Let that be uncertain. Shoot both — the hand touch as meaning something, the hand touch as a check for temperature. Let the edit decide.

## The Boss — The Inventory

*(Room 5 — The Tower Top)*

---

### What it actually is

The water heater. Upright, cylindrical, still warm. The Unimaginative found it running on backup power, tagged it ACTIVE INFRASTRUCTURE, and left it alone. It has been running continuously, keeping water warm in a house where no one is turning on the taps. It does not know the water isn't being used. It is doing its job.

Around the water heater: the fourteen boxes the Unimaginative tagged as Waste Paper. They didn't remove them. They were supposed to come back. They haven't come back.

The box labeled waste paper is the one with the crown-above-a-box mark on every visible side.

---

### What the box sees

An archivist built of accumulated heat — the water heater as a central column of warmth, the waste paper boxes as scrolls and ledgers arrayed around it, the whole assembly moving with slow certainty, tending to something it was assigned to tend. It is not aggressive. It is thorough. It is doing the last thing it was told to do, with full commitment, in a room where the assignment has long since ceased to matter.

Its name — chiseled into the stone rafter above the cleared corner:

**THE INVENTORY**

---

### Boss Intro Text

*(Flavor card — one sentence, before the fight.)*

"It's still counting things. Nobody told it to stop. It's been up here for three months, keeping everything exactly where it was."

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — The Assessment.** The Inventory moves through the room in a circuit — not toward Kid, not away from her. It is completing its round. Every third step, it pauses and rotates to face the nearest object in the room: a box, a lamp, the rocking chair. It stays like that for two seconds, then continues.

This is not an attack pattern. This is just what it does.

The attack: when Kid moves toward the cleared corner — toward the seat-worn box, toward the crown mark — The Inventory stops its circuit. It faces Kid. The boxes around it rotate toward her like witnesses. Then, all at once: a wave of catalogued objects. Not thrown — *dispatched*. Each one launched precisely, in sequence, with the exactness of something that knows what it's handling. A lamp. A folded blanket. A bin of baby clothes. They are not aimed to injure. They are aimed to move Kid away from the corner.

The Inventory is not attacking Kid. It is filing her under: DO NOT ENTER.

**Phase 2 — The Override.** The boxes around it have been depleted. It is alone now — just the central column, the warmth, the continuous hum of something that has been running for three months in the dark.

It rushes. Not the Stalker's rolling pursuit — a committed, direct charge toward whatever Kid is standing near. It is trying to remove her from the premises. Its assessment is complete. She is not supposed to be here.

What Kid has: the drumsticks, the rope, the grapple. The Inventory has mass and momentum and nowhere else to go — it is running its last contingency. If Kid can hook the rafter above the cleared corner and swing into the space while The Inventory commits to its rush, it passes under her. It overcommits. Three full seconds to recover.

The lesson: use the corner. Whoever cleared that space knew what they were doing. It's still the best position in the room.

**Phase 3 — The Last Count.** The Inventory stops. Something in it has reached the end of a process. It does not attack. It rotates — slowly, full circle — and faces the cleared corner. It faces the seat-worn box. The crown mark above.

Then it turns to Kid.

It does not attack with urgency. It attacks with everything that's left, methodically, as if completing a final entry in a record. It is checking something off.

What Kid understands, fighting through Phase 3: it was guarding this. Not the boxes — not the labeled junk and the waste paper. This corner. Specifically this corner. Whatever was in this corner, whatever was done here, The Inventory knows it was important and it was told to protect it and it has been doing that for three months and it will do it until it can't.

It doesn't know the thing it's guarding is already gone.

The person who sat here left a long time ago. Only the marks are left. The Inventory is guarding the marks.

### Victory Moment

The Inventory settles. The hum drops in pitch, long, slowing — the way a machine sounds when the power fails gradually rather than all at once. Then quiet.

The boxes on the floor are still. The lamps are still glowing.

Kid walks to the cleared corner. She stands where the box is. She looks up at the crown mark on the rafter.

Up close: the marker has dried out in places. The lines are thicker in some spots, thinner in others — the pen was running low. Whoever drew this pressed harder when it started fading. They wanted it to stay.

Below the crown mark, barely visible in the same dried-out marker: a word. One word, written small, the way you write something that's just for you.

*STILL.*

Kid reads it. She doesn't say it aloud.

*A beat.*

*A long beat.*

KID: "Yeah."

## Zone Lore Hook

The Attic is where things go when people stop looking at them but can't let go.

The Unimaginative understand keeping — they keep records, manifests, asset tags, logs. But they keep things in order, which means keeping means categorizing, which means anything that resists categorization cannot be kept. The attic resisted. WINTER STUFF is not a category they can use. MOM'S THINGS has no SKU. They tagged fourteen boxes Waste Paper and left.

What they missed: the reason things are kept is not the same as the things themselves. You keep the baby clothes because of what was in them, who wore them, where you were when you folded them the last time. The box is not the point. The Unimaginative only know how to value the box.

Kid comes up here and the box reads this as a tower of accumulated knowing because every kept thing is a kind of knowing — not information, *memory*, which is heavier. The wizard who lived in this tower spent years up here among the things that couldn't be thrown away, among the labels that were approximations of caring, among the lamps that were broken but kept because they were someone's, and they did something in this corner. They built something. They figured something out.

The marks remain.

Whatever they were building — Kid is still holding it together. She doesn't know that yet. She will.

### World Tree breadcrumb *(second-to-last — the stakes are close now)*

The fourteen boxes tagged Waste Paper by the Unimaginative: Kid finds one open in Room 4. Not ransacked — opened carefully, the flaps folded back. Inside: papers. Handwritten. Dense. Diagrams. Not maps exactly — something between a diagram and a plan, the kind of drawing that means both *here is how it works* and *here is what we're going to do*.

The diagrams are of a tree. Large, cross-sectioned, the root system detailed. Notes in the margins in handwriting Kid has seen once before — the same hand as the correction on the school hallway chalkboard. *The World Tree (mythological).* Not mythological. Accurate. The diagrams are *accurate* — exactly the shape of what Kid has been moving toward, the thing she's felt like a change in pressure.

Below the diagrams, in the same hand: a list. Twelve items. Eleven are crossed out. The twelfth is not.

The twelfth item reads: *find the next one.*

Kid folds the paper. She puts it in her pocket with the record sleeve from the Basement.

She doesn't say anything. The Female Ninja isn't in this room yet.

Some things you carry alone until you understand them.

## Tone Note — What Makes This Zone Different

Every zone before the Attic had a clear antagonist relationship — the enemies were doing something to the place. The Cul-de-Sac had marshals enforcing order. The Garage had permit pulpers managing inventory. The School Hallway had an administrative system doing its job. The Basement had things locked in.

The Attic has no antagonist relationship. The Unimaginative barely touched it. They came, they tagged fourteen boxes, they left. The enemies here are not doing anyone's bidding. They are just — present. The Golems are waiting for December. The Stalker is always arriving. The Inventory is completing a process nobody cancelled.

This is the first zone where the thing Kid is fighting isn't The Unimaginative's doing. It's what happens to a kept place when no one comes to keep it.

The stakes change. The Cardboard Mill is next — and unlike the Attic, the Mill is something the Unimaginative built on purpose, seized with intention, and are running. The Attic is where the personal things were. The Mill is where they went industrial.

Kid comes up to the Attic and finds that someone was here before her, doing something that mattered, leaving marks. She comes down with two more pieces of paper in her pocket. She comes down knowing the World Tree is real — she already believed it, but believing something and holding a diagram of it are different weights.

The weight is heavier now.

She goes down the stairs. She puts her hand on the loose step. She steps over the soft spot.

She didn't know about the soft spot before she came up. She knows now.

*That's what this zone is.*
