# Zone Lore — The Cardboard Mill

**World 7.** Reality: a seized factory — a working one. Not ruined, not abandoned. Running. The Unimaginative took it the same way they take everything: efficiently, without violence, by reclassifying it. They filed a use-permit for a facility that was already operating. The original workers came in one morning and found new managers in the hallways and new labels on the doors, and by lunchtime the manifests had been reissued. Nobody stopped production. Nobody stopped anything. The line just ran under new administration.

The building is concrete and corrugated metal. Overhead fluorescents in long rows, most of them working. The floor has the grey-black stain of a place that has been continuously used. Conveyor belts. Pulping tanks — long, low vats where raw fiber enters as wet slurry and exits as compressed sheet. Cutting rigs. Stacking cranes that swing on overhead rails. At the far end: the pressing room, where the sheets become board, where the board becomes the boxes.

The boxes are grey.

They were not always grey. The original facility made boxes for moving companies, for retailers, for anyone who needed them. They were brown, natural, the color cardboard is when no one has told it to be something else. The Unimaginative changed the formulation. Not out of cruelty — for consistency. Grey is neutral. Grey is a surface that receives a label without suggesting anything else. Grey is correct for an organization that believes in correct surfaces.

Everything that has been taken from every zone — the leaves, the leaves under the porch, the records in the Basement, the labeled boxes in the Attic — it all comes here. It enters the pulper as something and exits as raw material. The pulper does not distinguish. It is not designed to.

At the center of the mill floor, visible from the entrance: three stacking cranes moving in a slow, continuous cycle. They have been moving this cycle for months. They will move it until they're told not to. They have not been told not to.

Through the box: the mill is a forge. Not the ruined one from the Garage — a living one, working at full heat. The conveyor belts are rivers of molten something-on-the-edge-of-becoming. The pulping tanks are crucibles. The stacking cranes are hammer-arms swinging overhead in arcs too precise to be accidental. The fluorescents overhead are forge-glow. The box sees all of it clearly for the first time: this is the place where imagination is unmade. This is the last step before the World Tree.

The name carved above the entrance, in the imagined space:

**THE UNMAKING MILL**

## Zone Identity

Every zone before this one was a place the Unimaginative found and took over. The Attic they barely touched. The Basement they sealed. The School Hallway they administered. The Garage they processed. The Cul-de-Sac they enforced.

The Cardboard Mill they run.

This is the difference Kid feels without being able to name it. Every other zone had the texture of occupation. This one has the texture of ownership. The Unimaginative did not walk into this building and redirect what was happening. They walked in and this is what they built. They improved on the grey output. They increased throughput. They optimized the conveyor timing. This is their design expressed at full scale, full speed, in a building purpose-built to do what it does.

What it does is unmake things.

Not destroy — unmake. The difference is important. Destruction leaves evidence. The pulper leaves fiber, which becomes board, which becomes box, which becomes grey neutral surface, which becomes a container for something assigned to it by a manifest. Nothing is destroyed. Everything is processed. The records in the Basement existed. The records in the Basement were pulped. Both things are true, and the second is more efficient than the first.

Kid does not know this yet in Room 1. She learns it across the zone in the same way she learned the Attic — by finding the things that weren't meant to be found. By the time she reaches the pressing room, she understands what she's been walking through.

She goes through it anyway. She goes through everything.

## Enemy Roster

### Factory Drones

*(All rooms — standard encounter enemy. The primary grunt in this zone.)*

**What they actually are:** The machines. Not autonomous — operated. The Unimaginative assigned Skeptic grunts to run the assembly line, and the grunts and their equipment have been working together long enough that the distinction has blurred. The drone is a Skeptic grunt at a station, with the station's tool in hand. The worker and the work have become the same thing.

**What the box sees:** Industrial figures — thick-limbed, flat-headed, moving with the jerk-and-pause of assembly-line motion. They do not move continuously; they move in increments. Step. Stop. Orient. Step. Stop. Orient. Like something executing a program rather than walking. Each one carries the zone-appropriate weapon: in this zone, the same grapple ropes and reach weapons Kid finds. The Drones use them as loading and positioning tools — a grapple hook for hauling a crate becomes a grapple hook that grabs Kid's ankle. The reframe is immediate and mundane.

**Role:** Pack harasser. They attack in coordinated bursts — two step forward, one repositions. When one is engaged, another uses the distraction. They do not protect each other. They do not respond when one falls. They continue their assigned pattern. The remaining Drones keep the rhythm even when there are fewer of them to hold it.

**Behavioral Note — for animators:**
Factory Drones do not patrol in the human sense. They circuit. Their idle path is a short, looping track — the same three steps, same three steps — because that is what the job requires. When Kid enters their radius, they do not deviate from the circuit. They add Kid to it. She becomes a stop on the loop. Orient. Assess. Act. Return. The horror of it is that it barely changes. She is one more thing to process.

When a Drone is defeated: it slows before it falls. The step-pause rhythm extends. One step. Long pause. One step. Then it sits on the floor with its legs straight out, like someone who has been standing for a very long time and has finally been told they can stop.

**Barks:** Nothing verbal. The sound of equipment moving. A chain-clink. A gear-shift. Effort sounds with no voice behind them.

### Grey Architects

*(Rooms 2, 3, 4 — elite enemy. One or two per room.)*

**What they actually are:** The shift supervisors. Skeptic grunts who have been here longer than the others — the ones who were assigned in the first week and never reassigned. They know the building. They know the timing of every machine. They are not more imaginative than the other Skeptics. They are more efficient, which in this context is more dangerous.

**What the box sees:** Taller figures — not physically larger, but they carry themselves differently. Where the Factory Drones circuit, the Grey Architects stand. They watch. They have the posture of someone reading a room the way Kid reads a room — a rapid, continuous assessment of what is where and what it could do. In the imagined space, they wear something like armor, but built wrong: structured like a blueprint rather than a shield, flat planes at sharp angles, the geometry of something designed to redirect rather than absorb.

They carry the same zone weapons the Drones carry. What makes them different is how.

The Grey Architect does not use the grapple rope to hook Kid's ankle. They use it to position Kid where they want her. There is a sequence behind every move they make — two steps for the Drone to engage, the Architect pulls Kid off the Drone's blind side, the Drone repositions. It is not a coordinated plan in any sense the Architect could explain. It is pattern recognition applied continuously. They see the room the way a person who has been doing the same job for six months sees it: every surface, every interval, every predictable variable.

**Behavioral Note — for animators:**
The Grey Architect stands at the edge of the room. They do not move toward Kid until they have information — two or three exchanges with the Factory Drones go first. They watch. Their head tracks Kid but their body doesn't turn until they've decided something. When they move, they move with conviction: no adjustment, no hesitation, straight line to the position they already chose before they started moving.

When Kid parries a Grey Architect, they do not stumble back like the Drones. They step back. One step, deliberate. They look at Kid. Then they look at the room — checking the position, recalculating. The recalculation is visible. The player will recognize it. It is the same thing Kid does.

This is the most unsettling enemy in the mill: not because they are strong, but because they are working.

When defeated: they lower their weapon slowly. They sit on the floor with their back straight. They look at where they were standing — at the position they chose. They are auditing the decision. Something in the posture says: *the plan was correct. I don't know what went wrong.*

**Barks:**
"Redirect."
"Hold the position."
"Stop."

*(Said without urgency. Commands are statements here — not instructions to Kid, instructions to the system.)*

### Attic Golems — Mill Variant

*(Rooms 3 and 4 — the large form. One per room.)*

**What they actually are:** The stacking cranes. The overhead rigs that swing on rails and position the pressed board. The Unimaginative did not animate them. They were already moving. They have been moving continuously for months. What has changed is that something in the system now recognizes the intrusion on the floor and cannot not respond to it.

**What the box sees:** A form assembled from the mill's own material — pressed board layers, crane-arm extensions, the overhead rail as a spine. Massive. Slow in the way of something that was built to move tons rather than bodies. The swing of the crane arm is its primary attack: a slow, committed arc across the room, covering enormous space, starting from a position Kid can see coming from across the floor. There is no feint. There is no adjustment. It swings because it swings. The only question is where Kid is when it arrives.

**Role:** Room controller. The Golem does not chase Kid. It swings. It covers one-third of the room per arc. Three arcs cover the room. The Drones and Architects on the floor know the swing timing. They do not avoid it — they never need to. Kid needs to learn the timing to fight anything else in the room.

**Behavioral Note — for animators:**
The Golem does not track Kid. Its arc is fixed — the same arc it has always run, because that arc corresponds to the actual loading path of the crane before any of this happened. Kid is an obstacle in an operational path. The crane does not adjust for her. She adjusts for it, or she doesn't.

The vulnerable moment: the end of the arc, when the crane arm reaches the far limit of its rail and pauses — half a second of full stop — before the return sweep. Kid learned variable range in the Attic. She learned to use the corner. The lesson here is the window in the pause: the machine tells her when it's available. She has to be listening.

When defeated: the overhead rail locks. The arm settles at its farthest reach — extended, stopped, the way a clock arm stops when the mechanism is finished. The room gets quieter. The sound of the mill is still there. But one of its rhythms is gone, and the gap is audible.

**Barks:** The sound of machinery. Rail-grind. Weight under tension. Nothing a voice would make.

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Cardboard Mill Appearance

*(Room 3 — The Crane Room. Second-to-last zone appearance. Post-combat, pre-Golem. The Factory Drones are cleared. The Grey Architect is down. The Golem is still cycling. Kid is reading its arc.)*

---

### Context

This is the second-to-last time the player sees the one who keeps watching. The player has been carrying this figure for seven zones. By now they know the pattern completely. They know to look for the quiet room after the fight. They know there will be one sentence. They know what gets left behind.

What is different here: they are closer.

In every zone before this, the one who keeps watching appeared in a doorway, a corner, the edge of the space Kid reclaimed. Here they are in the room — not at the edge of it. Not in a threshold. Standing near the crane's center rail, where the arm passes overhead every twelve seconds in its arc.

They are not afraid of the Golem. The Golem's arc passes five feet above them. They already know the timing.

---

### The visual moment

The one who keeps watching is not looking at the Golem. They are looking at the floor. At the grey board stacked along the near wall — pressed, cut, labeled, waiting for assignment. Their posture is the same as every zone. Still. Flat. No clipboard, no task.

But their hands are at their sides, not crossed, not together. Open.

That's new.

*(By the Attic, they touched something for the first time. Here, their hands are open. The animator should not underline this. It is either meaning something, or it isn't. Both readings should be available.)*

---

### The line

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "They were going to build something here."

*They do not look at Kid when they say it. They say it to the stacked board. To what the board used to be.*

*The crane arm sweeps overhead. Neither of them moves.*

*They go.*

---

### What they leave behind

A handprint in the grey dust on the board stack. Not pressed hard — just where their hand rested on the edge while they were standing there. A contact mark. The grey is barely different from the grey around it. Kid would have to look closely to see it.

She does look closely.

She doesn't know why.

---

### The read for the player

This is the line Kid said herself in the zone intro. She said it because the one who keeps watching said it at the Attic — she has been carrying their words.

Now they say it again, here, in the place that undoes things.

The player needs to sit with what that means. Don't explain it. Let it land.

The one who keeps watching is not a villain. They are not recruiting Kid to their side. They are not waking up. They are stating a material fact: there was something intended here, and it became this instead.

Whether they are beginning to understand what they cannot see — leave that uncertain. Let the hands being open carry all the weight it needs to carry without naming it.

## Room Structure and Pacing

The entrance. Conveyor belts quiet — nothing incoming, the pulpers in the next room running but not fed yet. The overhead lights flicker when the cranes move: a power fluctuation from the machinery.

First encounter: three Factory Drones running their circuits. Standard spacing. Kid learns the step-pause-orient rhythm.

**Safe zone beat:** After the room clears. Kid finds a narrow platform above the conveyor — a maintenance walkway, accessible by jump. Above: the crane rails, unoccupied for now.

### Room 2 — The Pulping Floor

The wet room. The pulping tanks run the length of the space — long, low, full of slurry at 40°C. The smell here is stronger: wet paper, warm fiber. The steam from the tanks is constant and low, not enough to obscure vision, enough to shift the light. Fluorescents through steam. Everything has a slight haze.

Encounter: four Factory Drones and one Grey Architect. The Architect positions at the far wall near the tank controls. The Drones move on their circuits — one of which passes the tank edge, close enough that a missed dodge puts Kid near the lip.

The tanks do not damage on contact — Kid can grab the edge. But the Architect knows Kid doesn't want to be in the tank, and positions accordingly, pulling her toward it with the grapple rope.

**Continuity note — weapons:** This is where Kid finds the zone's primary weapon: **The Waterwhip** (the pressure hose used to move slurry through the system). In reality: a thick industrial hose, heavy, with a brass fitting at the end. In imagination: a water whip — fluid, extending, carrying the temperature of the tanks. Ranged-melee. It hits hard at mid-range. Close in and it loses leverage — designed for this room's spacing, which means every other room requires adjustment.

**Diegetic tutorial:**

*(Kid picks up the hose. The brass fitting swings at the end. She tests the weight — too heavy to flick, needs full arm movement.)*

KID: "Heavy. I need the room for it."

*(She looks at the tank. At the Drones on their circuits. At the space between them.)*

KID: "I need them in the open."

### Room 4 — The Cutting Floor

The room before the pressing room. Large bladed rigs on either side — guillotine-style cutters on hydraulic arms, timed to size the sheets. They are still running. They are cutting air.

Encounter: three Factory Drones, two Grey Architects. The Architects know the cutter timing and use it: they position Kid in the path and wait. The blade comes down. They move aside.

This is the hardest combat room in the zone. Kid has the Waterwhip, full range of motion, and a room full of things that want to use the room against her.

### Room 5 — The Pressing Room (Boss Approach)

The final corridor before the boss. The board is pressed here — massive horizontal plates that descend every thirty seconds, compress whatever is between them, rise. The cycle is mechanical, visible, loud. The sound of this room is the sound of the building — the base note underneath everything else.

The one who keeps watching is not here. Their appearance was in Room 3. This room is empty.

It is the first room in the zone with no encounter. Just the press cycling. Just the sound.

Kid walks through.

She times the press without thinking. She has been timing things since the Attic Golem.

She steps through in the gap.

At the far end: a door. Through the door: the boss arena. Through the arena's far wall — visible through the high windows — the top of the World Tree, impossible to miss, enormous and green against the grey.

Kid stops at the door.

*(She doesn't go through yet.)*

KID: "I can see it."

*(A long beat.)*

KID: "I can see it from here."

## Boss — The Permit Pulper

*(The Pressing Room — the center of the mill's production floor. This is the largest space in the building.)*

---

### What it actually is

The facility manager's office — the room and everything in it, combined with the mill's central press mechanism. The office was at the center of the original factory: glass-walled, elevated slightly above the floor on a short platform so the manager could see the whole production line. The Unimaginative kept the office in place and integrated it. The glass walls are now part of the press frame. The desk — a heavy metal one, the kind that was in this room before the original workers arrived — is fused to the center platform.

The Permit Pulper is the desk and the press and the stacks of reassignment forms that have been accumulating for months, and it has been running the mill's central process since the occupation. It is not malevolent. It is thorough. Everything that enters this room is assessed for compliance and processed accordingly.

Kid enters this room. She is not compliant. The assessment begins.

---

### What the box sees

A massive administrative construct — a bureaucratic knight built from compressed material. The desk as a chest-plate of solid metal, filed smooth. The press arms as shoulder-mounted rams that extend and compress. The glass walls of the original office, cracked and dark, as visor panels around a featureless face. The stacks of forms — reassignment manifests, waste-paper designations, asset-reclassification orders — as a cloak of accumulated decisions trailing behind it.

Every decision it made is still attached to it. It carries everything it ever processed.

Its name — stamped into the floor of the arena in the imagined space:

**THE PERMIT PULPER**

---

### Boss Intro

*(One line. Flavor card. Before the music starts.)*

"It's been reassigning things for eight months. The desk was here before the Unimaginative were. It doesn't remember the difference."

---

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — The Assessment.** The Permit Pulper does not attack immediately. It processes Kid first. It moves through the room in a circuit — the same overhead survey path the original manager would have walked — and it is gathering information. Two full circuits. Kid can attack during this phase. Each hit registers. The Pulper adjusts its position slightly — not retreating, updating. Like something revising a form.

The attack: a compression slam. The press arms extend, the Pulper commits to a position, and everything in a significant radius flattens. The windup is visible and long — the press arms rise, the glass visor panels go bright, the form-cloak billows. The counter-window opens at peak rise. Hit it during the windup and the slam misses Kid and stresses the mechanism. Fail to hit it and the slam hits. Hard.

**Phase 2 — The Reclassification.** Some of the form-cloak has been shed — destroyed through hits, paper settling to the floor. The Pulper can move faster without it. The circuit shortens. The assessment windows compress.

New behavior: the Pulper begins using the zone weapons. The grapple rope — the same rope Kid found in the Garage, the same the Drones and Architects used — the Pulper deploys it. In its hands, it is a positioning tool, the same way it was for the Grey Architect. It pulls Kid toward the press-slam radius. It does not wrap or tangle. It pulls. Kid can cut the pull mid-arc with the Waterwhip.

This phase asks Kid to use both weapons: Waterwhip for mid-range, grapple counter for repositioning. The Pulper is running the system at full capacity. So is Kid.

**Phase 3 — The Last Manifest.** The press arms are damaged. The cloak is gone — the floor is covered in scattered forms, visible but not readable. The Pulper moves with urgency that it has not shown in the two prior phases. It is not panicking. It is completing a process. Whatever it is doing, it intends to finish it.

It crosses the room in a direct line toward Kid. Not a circuit. Not a positioning move. A crossing. It is the most direct thing it has done in the fight.

What it is doing: trying to reassign the World Tree fragments.

Kid has the fragments in her pocket. The Pulper knows they are there — it has been assessing the room this entire fight and the fragments are the most significant non-compliant assets in it. It is moving toward Kid to complete its primary mandate: find the fragments, reassign them to waste-paper status, route them to the pulper.

It will not stop until it does this or it cannot.

Kid has to interrupt the crossing three times with full Waterwhip extensions. The range is right for this room — she learned it on the Pulping Floor. The crossing is direct, which means the Pulper gives up the circuit and gives up the position-reading and gives up everything except momentum. Momentum Kid can work with. She has been working with momentum since the Wagon Wheel Roller.

**The final moment:** The Pulper slows. The press arms lower — not poised, lowered, resting. It stops in the center of the room. The visor panels go dim.

It does not collapse. It stands. It has completed as much of its process as it was able to complete. It is standing with the completion it has.

Kid walks past it to the far wall.

She looks through the high window.

### Victory Moment

The press arms rest. The glass panels are dark.

Kid stands at the far window. Through it: the World Tree. Enormous. Green against grey. The canopy moves in a wind she cannot feel from here — but she can see it moving. Something up there is alive.

She puts her hand on the glass.

*(Beat.)*

The hum of the building is still there. The machines are still running — the cutters in Room 4, the conveyors in Room 1. She has not turned the mill off. She has come through it.

KID: "Almost."

*(She takes her hand off the glass.)*

KID: "One more."

### Zone Victory Beat

*(Plays over the Imagination Restore bloom. Short. Sensory.)*

The grey board on the floor pulses once — a bloom of natural brown moving through it like a wave, the color cardboard is before anyone tells it what to be. It settles back. But something in the fiber has remembered.

## Weapons Introduced

### The Waterwhip

*(Found: Room 2 — the Pulping Floor, the pressure hose used to direct slurry flow.)*

**Real object:** Industrial pressure hose. Thick rubber casing, brass fittings at both ends. Heavy. Designed to move high-temperature slurry through a system under pressure.

**Imagined weapon:** The Waterwhip. Ranged-melee. A heavy, extending strike at mid-range — it carries momentum the way a chain carries momentum: the throw starts from the shoulder, travels the length of the hose, arrives with everything built up on the way. Close in and it loses leverage. Step back to seven or eight feet and it does what it was made to do.

The brass fitting hits with weight that other weapons in the set don't have. It does not have a point. It does not need one.

**Flavor text:**
"Pressure hose → The Waterwhip"
"It was moving slurry at 120 psi. It can do other things. It knows what weight feels like at the end of a long line."

**Combat note:** The Waterwhip teaches heavy-momentum timing — the swing commits before the hit, meaning Kid has to read the room before she commits, not after. It pairs with the grapple rope: rope to reposition the enemy into the waterwhip range, waterwhip to close. Kid has been learning this pairing since the Grey Architect demonstrated it against her.

**Diegetic tutorial moment:**

*(Room 2 — Kid picks up the hose. Tests the weight. The brass fitting swings.)*

KID: "I can't flick this. It wants full arm."

*(She steps back from the nearest Drone. Watches its circuit. Waits for it to pass the open space.)*

KID: "There. Seven feet. That's where it works."

Here, she sees it used as a repositioning tool. By Phase 2 of the Permit Pulper, she is using it the way the Architect used it — pulling enemies into her range rather than reaching across space.

## Tone Note — What Makes This Zone Different

The Attic was personal. The things in it were someone's. The Attic was neglected in the specific way that means someone cared too much to throw things away and too little to keep taking care of them.

The Cardboard Mill is not personal. It is industrial. The things in it are assets on a manifest. The people running it are not running it badly — they are running it exactly as designed, at full capacity, with no waste and no deviation. This is the Unimaginative operating at the height of their ability.

This is what they built. This is what it does.

Kid walks through it zone by zone, room by room, and does not destroy it. The machines keep running when she leaves each room. The conveyors still move. The cutters still cycle. She is not here to turn it off — she is not sure she could. She is here to get through it.

What changes as she moves through the zone: she carries something they cannot process. She has the World Tree fragments. She has the notes from the Attic. She has the Attic diagram. She has the record sleeve and the chalkboard equation and the correction and the marks and now the blank forms she folded and put in her pocket. She carries everything they tried to unmake and she has made it through eight months of ongoing industrial operation and she is at the window and she can see the tree.

The Unimaginative's best argument — the mill running at full capacity — did not stop her.

She goes through the door.
