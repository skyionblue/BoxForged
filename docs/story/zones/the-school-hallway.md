# Zone Lore — The School Hallway

**World 4.** Reality: a long corridor in an elementary school, lockers lining both walls, linoleum floor, drop ceiling with panels missing. The lights still work — half of them. The Unimaginative have been through here: every classroom door has been chained shut with a numbered padlock. The signage is still up. Hallway B. Room 214. Fire exit. The signs point to places that no longer have anyone in them. Imagination: a castle gauntlet — long stone corridor, torchlit, with iron portcullis gates where the chained doors are, and arrow slits where the lockers were. The ceiling vaults up into darkness. The floor is cut stone, worn smooth by the boots of people who walked here for a hundred years, going somewhere that mattered.

---

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Main Corridor. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the 3-second assembly beat as the stone builds around her.)*

THE SCHOOL HALLWAY

The lockers used to bang. That's the first thing I notice — that they're not banging. When this place was full, you could hear it from the parking lot: hundreds of metal doors, all at once, all different. Now they're stone arches with iron bars and everything behind them is dark and I can hear myself breathing.

The floor knows what it is. You can tell from the way it sounds. Four hundred kids walked here every day for thirty years, and now it's just me, and it sounds like me.

The first portcullis is raised. The second one isn't.

KID: "Okay. I'm going."

## Zone Identity

The school hallway is the motion between. Not the classroom — the classroom is where you sit. The hallway is where you move. It's the three minutes between everything, the place where noise builds and releases, where you run into the person you've been trying to find all day, where you drop your lunch and someone you don't know picks it up. It is alive precisely because it's in between.

The Unimaginative chained the classroom doors because empty classrooms, unmanaged, become problems. But they left the hallway alone because a hallway is just a corridor. Infrastructure. Not worth tagging.

They were wrong about that.

The box reads a hallway as a gauntlet because that's what a hallway is when you have to pass through it — a test of whether you know how to move. Long, narrow, no cover, nowhere to stop. You have to keep going. The castle version understands this completely. The torches are lit. The portcullis gates are down. There is one way: forward.

What Kid learns here: the hallway was always moving. She just has to move with it.

## Enemy Roster

### The Hall Wardens

*(Rooms 1, 2, 3 — standard encounter enemy)*

**What they actually are:** The hall passes. Laminated cardboard tags on lanyards, the kind teachers clip to a board — OUT, NURSE, LIBRARY. The Unimaginative found them hanging by the main office and didn't tag them for removal. A hall pass is a permission slip, and the Unimaginative appreciate permissions. They left them in place. The hall passes have been hanging for three months in a building with no students, no teachers, no one to give them to.

They started walking.

**What the box sees:** Sentinels in livery — flat, laminated heralds with the crests of their orders still readable on their chests. OUT. NURSE. LIBRARY. They move in a practiced patrol, and they block passage with the absolute authority of something that was given permission to block passage. They don't swing. They present — a flat-armed stop gesture, repeated, until the obstacle clears or is removed.

**Role:** Patrol/blocker. Medium speed, medium durability. They don't chase past a threshold — their authority extends only so far down the hall, and they know it. At the edge of their patrol range, they stop and watch. They won't follow. They have their assignment.

**Behavioral Note:** Hall Wardens move in pairs. One walks the center; one walks the wall. They are synchronized — not by signal, by habit. When the center Warden stops, the wall Warden stops. When one turns, the other turns three steps later. It looks coordinated. It isn't. They have just been walking the same hall for so long that they have worn each other into rhythm.

When Kid enters their range, the center Warden raises one arm. Flat. Parallel to the floor. The gesture is not aggressive. It is formal. It means stop. The wall Warden closes the gap on the opposite side. They are making a corridor across the corridor.

What breaks the formation: if Kid disrupts one, the other stops entirely. They were trained in pairs and they do not function well alone. The solo Warden stands still for a beat too long — the rhythm has been broken and it is trying to find the beat again. This is the window.

**Barks:** Silent. The laminate creaks when they move. In the gauntlet version, this sounds like armor.

### The Locker Lurker

*(Room 2 and 4 — ambush enemy. Replaces the Leaf Pile Lurker in this zone.)*

**What it actually is:** The lockers. Not the contents — the lockers themselves, specifically the ones that didn't get fully closed when everyone left. A door hanging open by six inches is an invitation. Warmth goes out, something comes in.

**What the box sees:** A figure that is more gap than substance — it lives in the space between the iron bars, the shadow between wall and portcullis. It doesn't fully exist until it commits. Until then, it's just a darkness that has roughly the shape of a kid.

**Role:** Ambusher/flanker. It doesn't attack from range. It waits for Kid to pass and hits from the side. The tell is sound: the creak of an iron bar shifting weight before the lunge. One beat between creak and strike.

**Behavioral Note — for animators:**
The Locker Lurker doesn't move between its ambush points. It dematerializes from one gap and rematerializes in another — not teleporting, more like the shadow shifting. The player hears a creak. They look left. Nothing. They look right. Nothing. They take a step.

The Lurker was already on their right side. It was always on their right side. It was just too dark to see.

After the hit, it retreats to the nearest gap and goes still. It does not chase. Chasing would mean leaving the dark, and it does not leave the dark. If Kid lights up the area near a gap — any effect that illuminates — the Lurker delays. It flattens itself back into shadow and waits for the light to end.

**Barks:** A faint metallic clang. Once. Then nothing.

### The Permit Pulpers *(returning from Garage, different context)*

Two Permit Pulpers appear in Room 3. They're not patrolling inventory — they're guarding something. A chained portcullis. They stand in front of it, one on each side, the way guards stand at a gate.

They don't know there's nothing behind the door worth guarding. They were placed there. They are doing their job.

Same fight behavior as the Garage. Different meaning. In the Garage, they were doing paperwork. Here, they are guards. The Unimaginative promoted them, in a sense. The player will notice the familiar enemy is in a new configuration. Don't call it out. Let them feel the escalation.

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Corridor Appearance

*(Room 3 — Halfway Point. The corridor's mid-section, where the trophy case used to be. The case is empty.)*

*Design note: The Skeptic grunts in this zone carry clipboards and move in fixed patrol patterns between classroom doors. This figure is not patrolling. They are standing in front of an empty trophy case, looking at the floor. No clipboard. No manifest. The grunts never stop. This one has.*

---

### The visual moment

The trophy case runs eight feet along the wall, glass front, wooden shelves. Everything has been removed — plaques, cups, trophies. The empty brackets are still there, labeled: STATE RELAY CHAMPIONS, REGIONAL SPELLING BEE, ALL-DISTRICT SOCCER 2019.

The glass is clean. The shelves are bare. The labels point at nothing.

The one who keeps watching is standing in front of the case. Not looking at it. Looking at the floor in front of it. Their head is slightly down.

They don't see Kid come in. Or they do and they don't move. Either is possible and the animator should not choose — let it be genuinely ambiguous.

Then they look up. They see Kid. They look at the case for one second. They say their line.

Then they leave. Down the corridor Kid hasn't cleared yet. Toward the boss.

---

### The line

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "School's closed."

*The labels on the empty brackets are still lit in the gauntlet version — chiseled stone plaques, gold letters. STATE RELAY CHAMPIONS. REGIONAL SPELLING BEE. ALL-DISTRICT SOCCER 2019. In the reality underneath, they're just printed labels on empty shelves.*

*The one who keeps watching isn't saying the trophies are gone. They're saying the school is closed — which is true. Unambiguously true. The school is closed.*

*What Kid is standing in is still a school. What Kid is fighting for is still a school. The one who keeps watching is not wrong. Neither is Kid.*

---

### What they leave behind

Nothing visible. But Kid walks past the trophy case to get to the next room, and the labels catch the torchlight, and every bracket still says exactly what was there.

The absence is the thing. The labels describe what was. The one who keeps watching just pointed at it. That's all.

---

### Silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is the fourth time the player sees this kid. The box is the same. The walk is the same. The flat delivery is the same.

What is different: this time, they were looking at the floor first. Not at the empty case. At the floor in front of the case. As if they were looking for something that would have fallen.

This is the most ambiguous the Skeptic has ever been. Do not explain it.

**When they leave:** Toward the boss. Down the corridor. Into the part Kid hasn't reached yet.

The player has now watched the same kid walk toward the boss four times. They don't know what to do with that information. They keep playing. They'll find out.

## The Boss — The Hall Monitor

*(Room 5 — The End of the Hall)*

---

### What it actually is

The hall monitor station. The desk, the chair, the clipboard, the tardy log. It was at the end of the main corridor, where the two hallways intersect. Every student who came in late passed through it. It kept the record.

The Unimaginative walked past it and saw furniture. They left it. A desk is not cardboard. A clipboard is not cardboard. Nothing at this station required removal.

The tardy log has three months of empty lines in it. Three months of days when no one was late because no one came.

The station has been sitting at the end of the corridor, keeping the record, for three months, with nothing to record.

It has been adding its own entries.

---

### What the box sees

A magistrate built from authority and furniture — the desk is its body, the chair is its throne, the clipboard is its weapon and its shield. It is vast in the way that minor officialdom becomes vast when there's no one to question it: what starts as a desk, a clipboard, a tardy log, becomes — through three months of uncontested authority in an empty building — something that fills the whole intersection.

The tardy log it carries is open. Every page is full. It has been filling them itself.

Its name — chiseled into the stone lintel above the intersection in the gauntlet version:

**THE HALL MONITOR**

---

### Boss Intro Text

*(Flavor card — appears before the fight. One sentence.)*

"The last three months of entries are in a handwriting nobody recognizes. The log is full. You are late."

---

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — The Record.** The Hall Monitor fights at range, hurling TARDY SLIPS — flat, rectangular projectiles with enough mass to stagger. They are thrown in precise formations: three in a row, a gap, two more. The timing is regular. The pattern is learnable. It is not a random assault — it is a schedule. The tells are the clipboard moving: it pages to a new entry, flips the page back, and throws. The page-flip is the tell.

Between volleys, it calls out a category. LATE. UNPREPARED. DISRUPTING. Each category changes the formation pattern. The player has to read the word before the next volley.

The words are displayed in the gauntlet as stone-carved lettering that appears above the boss — large, readable. On the reality layer, it's just the Hall Monitor writing in the log.

**Phase 2 — The Detention.** The clipboard closes. The log seals. The Hall Monitor drops to the floor and becomes a pure physical threat — the desk-body rushing across the intersection, the chair-throne sweeping wide. It has moved from administration to enforcement. No more patterns. No more categories. Just the weight of the desk, moving fast, and Kid having to stop predicting and start reacting.

What Kid uses in Phase 2: the grapple. The desk is large enough to have structural anchor points — bolts, legs, edges. When the Hall Monitor commits to a rush, Kid can hook an anchor point and redirect: pull herself out of the path, or hook an enemy behind the boss and yank it into the rush. The boss will hit it instead of Kid.

The grapple was introduced in the Garage for pulling and closing distance. Here, it's used for redirection. Kid figured this out. The Cowgirl didn't have to tell her.

**Phase 3 — The Final Entry.** The log cracks open one more time. The Hall Monitor stands still, reading a specific page. The page it's been writing for three months: TODAY'S DATE. TODAY. The last entry. It was always going to end today.

It closes the log for the last time. Then it attacks with everything.

This is the final phase: every pattern from Phase 1, every rush from Phase 2, running simultaneously. It is chaotic, not rhythmic. The Hall Monitor has stopped administrating and started fighting. For the first time, it doesn't know what comes next either.

What Kid knows: she's been in the hall for the whole zone. She knows how to move in a corridor. She doesn't need cover. She needs distance and timing and the rope.

### Victory Moment

The log falls. Open. The last page is the one with today's date on it, and below the date: nothing. The entries stop. The page is empty.

The tardy slips settle on the floor like fallen leaves. They are just paper. They were always just paper.

The corridor exhales. It's not a sound — it's a quality, the feeling of a held breath released. The drop ceiling tiles are gone: above Kid is a vaulted stone corridor going up into torch-warm dark. The portcullis gates are all raised. Every chained door is free.

Kid looks down the corridor — both ways. Clear.

KID: "It's open."

*She means the hallway.*

*She means the school.*

*She means something bigger than both. She doesn't say that part.*

## Zone Lore Hook

The school was where you were told what to know. It organized knowledge and distributed it to children in rooms, one room at a time, through corridors that moved you between one kind of knowing and the next. The corridors were never the lesson. They were the between-time — the three minutes of noise and motion and everything that wasn't a lesson.

Kid goes through the corridor and it becomes a gauntlet because the corridor was always a test, just not the kind anyone graded. Can you get from one end to the other? Can you get there fast enough? Can you carry what you brought and still move?

The Unimaginative chained the classrooms because empty classrooms need to be controlled. But they forgot that the hallway was never about the classrooms.

What the hallway was about was the moving.

## Weapons Introduced

### The Grapple Rope — Expansion

The grapple rope from the Garage gets its second use taught here: redirect, not just close. The gap in Room 1 teaches pull-to-me and pull-me-to-it. The boss fight in Room 5 teaches redirect-the-charge. Kid discovers this herself. It is not explained.

*She throws the rope. It hooks. She goes.*

*She does. The Warden redirects past her into the wall. A beat of surprised silence.)*

## Tone Note — What Makes This Zone Different

Every zone before the School Hallway was outside or a threshold. The backyard, the garage — even the garage has a driveway. There's still sky somewhere in the frame.

The school hallway has no windows. It goes in one direction. You cannot see the outside from inside it.

This is the first zone that is purely interior, and the imagination knows it. The gauntlet is tighter than the dojo. The enemies are more formal — Hall Wardens don't hate Kid; they just have a job. The boss is administration, not a creature.

The school hallway is where Kid first encounters the possibility that the Unimaginative were here doing something organized and deliberate — not chaos, not malice, but a system. The chained doors are numbered. The log was kept. Someone made choices about this place, and the choices were orderly.

That's harder to fight than something that just wants to hurt you.

Kid doesn't think about this directly. But the zone's tone carries it. The whimsy is thinner here. The urgency is closer.

She comes out the other end and the hallway is open and the lockers are banging and it's all still hers.

But she learned something in there about what she's up against.
