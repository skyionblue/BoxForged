# Zone Lore — The Garage

**World 3.** Reality: a two-car garage stripped of its cars, everything grey-tagged and stacked for removal. Workbench cleared. Shelves half-emptied. One bare bulb. Imagination: a deep forge-dungeon carved into living stone, lit by cinder and ember, where weapons are made from heat and will.

---

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Side Door. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the 3-second assembly beat as the forge builds around her.)*

THE GARAGE

The door sticks the way it always has and then opens into something it never was before. The workbench is gone — it's a forge now, black iron, hot at the center, and I can feel it from here. The pegboard on the wall still has the outlines of tools nobody took when they left. In the dungeon version, those outlines are glowing. Like the shapes know what should be hanging there.

The ceiling drops ten feet and then drops ten more. The bulb that never worked is a torch that does.

I've been in this garage a hundred times. I never saw it like this until now.

KID: "Let's go."

---

## Zone Identity

The Garage is where things get *made*. Not found, not borrowed — made. The Dojo was where you learned. The Garage is where you build the thing you learned with.

The Unimaginative tagged it for clearance because a cleared garage is storage space. Manageable. Empty is easier. They boxed up the tools, stickered the shelves, pulled the cars. What they couldn't take: the outlines on the pegboard where tools used to hang. The oil stains on the concrete that map exactly where someone stood and worked for twenty years. The smell that doesn't go away.

Kid walks in here and the box reads it as a forge because that is the truest version of what a garage is: a place where you make the thing nobody makes for you.

---

## Enemy Roster

### The Permit Pulpers

*(Room 1 and 2 — standard encounter enemy)*

**What they actually are:** Filing cabinets. The Unimaginative brought them to catalogue and process the garage's contents. They have been here long enough that they've started moving on their own.

**What the box sees:** Bureaucratic stone constructs — faceless, dense, carrying sheaves of grey documents like shields. They move in formation. They don't attack fast. They attack *completely* — wide, sweeping clearance arcs, like they're not trying to stop you so much as file you away.

**Role:** Tank/crowd-control. Slow, durable. They advance like a wall being built.

**Behavioral Note:** Permit Pulpers don't patrol — they process. They move from corner to corner of the room in a fixed grid pattern, as if checking inventory. When Kid enters their radius, they don't react immediately. They complete their current path to its end point, *then* turn toward her. The delay isn't hesitation. They are finishing what they started. That's the unsettling part. They were in the middle of something.

**Barks:** None. They emit a low mechanical hum. When they close in, the hum gets louder.

---

### The Leaf Pile Lurkers *(returning from Dojo, different behavior)*

In the Garage, the Lurkers are under the workbench, in the corners, in the shelf gaps. The Garage is full of dark places. They've been here since before the Unimaginative came. Nobody told them to be here. They just ended up here, the way leaves always end up in garages.

Same sad ambusher energy. Different terrain. Don't call out their return — it just happens. The player will recognize them.

---

### The Rust Golem

*(Room 3 — mid-zone elite, one per room)*

**What it actually is:** A welding cart: oxygen tank, acetylene tank, the hose and torch, the dolly. Abandoned mid-project, rusted together.

**What the box sees:** A fire elemental sealed in iron — a figure with a torch arm and a tank for a torso that breathes blue at the joints. When it attacks, the torch flares. The cart wheels leave scorched lines on the stone floor.

**Role:** Fire-ranged elite. It stays still until Kid gets close. Then it commits: torch-sweep melee for anything inside five feet, long-range flame column for anything farther. No in-between.

**Behavioral Note:** The Rust Golem idles facing a wall. It is always in the middle of a job it can no longer finish. When Kid rounds the corner and sees it from behind, for one second it looks like a figure that just stepped away from a task. Then it registers her presence and turns. The turn is slow. The attack is not.

**Enemy Behavior Note — for animators:**
The Rust Golem doesn't stalk. It's not a hunter. It was mid-task when the Unimaginative froze this place, and it has been holding that position ever since — torch aimed at a weld seam that no longer needs welding. When Kid enters the room, its head swivels on its tank-neck like a smoke alarm sensing heat. Then, with no further announcement, it commits the whole body toward her. There is something terrible about watching something that large move that suddenly. It doesn't threaten. It resumes.

## The Cowgirl — First Appearance

*(Room 2 — The Storage Bay. Mid-zone. This is her introduction.)*

Kid clears the room and the last Permit Pulper folds. The grey receipt it was carrying drifts to the floor.

The door at the back of the bay is already open. Not forced open — propped open with a brick, like someone's been coming and going.

A rope is tied around the door handle. Not for the door. It's just there, coiled, as if someone set it down and stepped away for a moment.

Then there's a voice from behind the shelving unit on the left.

---

THE COWGIRL: "You came through the side door."

*Not a question. She steps out from behind the shelf. Her box is brown, brimmed, with a hand-drawn star on the front — lopsided, but on purpose. She's holding one end of the rope. The other end is tied to nothing in particular. She was checking the room before Kid got here, and she's fine with Kid knowing that.*

THE COWGIRL: "I came through the back. Different rooms."

*A pause. She takes in the box on Kid's head. Kid's weaponry. The way the room looks now that it's been cleared. She makes a read.*

THE COWGIRL: "I've seen three of these places so far. You move fast."

*She holds out the free end of the rope. Not as a gift. As a useful thing.*

THE COWGIRL: "This floor is rough. The rope helps."

---

### Who she is in this moment

She's been here before Kid. She's been in this garage before — not this garage, but garages like it. She found her box in one. She knows what the forge looks like when it lights up. She doesn't explain this. She doesn't explain anything. She just offers the rope and lets Kid decide what to do with it.

She's not Kid's sidekick. She's not waiting to be rescued or unlocked. She was already doing the thing. She found her own way in. The meeting is a collision of two people both moving in the same direction — they arrive at the same place from different doors.

She will not follow Kid. She moves parallel. Where Kid goes left, she goes right. They clear rooms together. She doesn't narrate. She acts.

---

### Design note — the introduction mechanic

The rope she hands Kid is a grapple. Not a swing — no Tarzan, no arc, no pendulum. It pulls. Either Kid throws it at an anchor point and it yanks her across the gap, or she throws it at an enemy and hauls them toward her. Two uses. Both are about closing distance on your own terms.

The Cowgirl doesn't explain this. She hands over the rope, she goes the other way, and Kid figures it out in the next room. The next room has a gap Kid can't jump, and an anchor point on the far wall. That's the whole tutorial. The mechanic teaches itself.

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Workbench Appearance

*(Room 4 — The Bench. Placement: after the room fight. The Cowgirl is not present for this — she went the other way. This moment is just Kid and the one who keeps watching.)*

*Design note: The Skeptic grunts who appear in Rooms 1 and 2 carry clipboards and move in their fixed inventory-check pattern. This figure has no clipboard. They are not processing the bench — they are standing in front of it, reading the sticker labels, and then they stop at the wrench outline. That deviation from procedural behavior is the tell. The grunts don't deviate.*

---

### The visual moment

The workbench runs the full length of the far wall. In the forge version, it's a great stone slab with tool-racks above it — silhouettes of hammers and tongs and shapes Kid can't name, all glowing at the edges, waiting for hands.

In the reality version, it's a cleared workbench. Everything's been sticker-tagged for removal. The pegboard above still has the outlines: the shadow of every tool that used to hang here, painted on when the workspace was first built so you'd always know where things went.

The one who keeps watching is standing in front of the bench. They reach out and press one finger against the outline of a wrench on the pegboard. Then they lift the finger. The outline is still there.

They turn. They look at Kid.

Then they say their line.

Then they go.

---

### The line

*(Confirmed. Flat delivery. One sentence. Then silence.)*

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "Nothing left to hang there."

*The outlines on the pegboard stay lit — in Kid's version of the room, they're glowing. In the reality underneath, they're just paint. The one who keeps watching isn't wrong about which one is true. Neither is Kid. Both of them are looking at the same wall.*

*What the player feels is not the line. It's the fact that the Skeptic touched it. The outline didn't change when they did. The glow is still there. They pressed their finger against it and it just — stayed.*

---

### The silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is the third time the player sees this specific kid — the one who keeps watching — and by now, if they've been paying attention, they recognize the box. Something is accumulating — not dread, something quieter. The same face. The same flat affect. The same precise observation of whatever the room actually contains.

The player has been fighting Skeptic grunts throughout three zones by this point. They know what a Skeptic looks like in a room. So the recognition here is layered: same box type as the grunts they've been fighting — and yet. No clipboard. Not doing a job. Just standing at the bench like they came here specifically for this.

**Idle behavior:** They walk the length of the bench slowly, reading the stickers. Not the outlines — the stickers. They're reading the inventory system. They're doing their job.

**The touch:** The finger on the wrench outline is the deviation. It's the one non-procedural thing they do in this scene. They stop at it. They touch it. They look at it. Then they move on. Whether they know what they did — whether there's a flicker of something — is for the animator to decide. Keep it ambiguous. One frame too long on their face, or don't hold at all. Both choices are correct.

**When they leave:** Through the back door. The same direction the Cowgirl came from, or close to it. This is not a coincidence. Do not flag it. Let it sit.

---

### What they leave behind

A sticker on the edge of the bench. One of the Unimaginative's grey removal tags. It reads: PROCESSED.

It's on the corner of the bench where the outline of the wrench is.

The glow stays. The sticker stays. Kid has to work at the bench to progress — she does it right next to the sticker, and the sticker doesn't stop anything.

---

### How the space feels when they leave

The bench is still hot. The outlines still glow. The Cowgirl's rope is hanging on a hook near the exit — she looped it there for Kid before she moved ahead.

Everything is present-tense and ready to use.

The sticker is the only grey thing in the room.

## The Boss — The Pegboard Warden

*(Room 5 — The Final Bay)*

---

### What it actually is

The pegboard. The whole thing: eight feet wide, four feet tall, full-coverage tool storage on a garage wall. The Unimaginative removed everything that was hanging on it except for one item they couldn't catalogue: a single old hand saw, still in its slot, handle forward.

The pegboard has been standing empty against the wall, the outlines of two hundred absent tools painted onto it, for three months.

It has been waiting for something to do.

---

### What the box sees

A skeletal knight made entirely of gap and absence — a figure assembled from the outlines, not the tools themselves. It is built from the shapes of what's missing. Every arm is a wrench-arm. Every joint is a hinge-silhouette. The hand saw is its single real weapon, a two-handed cleaving draw. The rest of it is the ghost of a workshop, armored in what should have been there.

---

### Boss Intro Lines

*(Title-card flavor — choose one per playthrough, or escalate across retries.)*

**Option A:**
"Every tool that ever hung here left its shape behind. It has all of them now."

**Option B:**
"The saw was the only thing they couldn't number. It never had a sticker. It never got a tag."

**Option C:**
"Something has been standing in this garage for three months, holding the shapes of two hundred missing tools, waiting for someone to come and use it against."

---

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — The Outline.** It fights from range, throwing gap-shapes: wrench-arcs, hammer-swings, tong-snaps. All of them are silhouettes — you can see through them, but they still hit. The tells are clear. It is a puzzle boss at first: the attacks are the shapes of familiar tools, and Kid starts reading them like the pegboard's own instructions.

**Phase 2 — The Saw.** The outline form shatters. What's left is just the saw — moving on its own, fast, skating the floor in long draw cuts, no tell, no pattern. This is the phase where The Unimaginative won: the last real tool, unbound, doing exactly what it was made to do, for no reason except that it's all that's left.

What Kid has to do in Phase 2 is stop reading the pattern and start moving on instinct. The game has been teaching this the whole zone. The forge doesn't give you a diagram. It gives you heat and a hammer and you figure it out.

---

### Defeat Line (Kid falls)

KID: "I didn't read it right."

*A beat.*

"I know what it's doing now."

---

### Victory Moment

The saw stops mid-draw and sits on the stone floor. No drama. It's just a saw.

The pegboard outlines go dark — not all at once. One by one, the shapes go out, like something leaving.

Then, slowly, the outlines come back. Not grey. Not faded. Vivid, full color, one at a time — each one a different color, the way a kid would color them in. Marker-bright. No two the same.

The bench is warm. The forge is going.

KID: "I know what goes here."

*She looks at each outline. Not reading. Knowing.*

KID: "I'll find every one."

*(She won't stop until the pegboard is full. She doesn't say this. But she won't.)*

## Zone Victory Beat

*(Plays over the Imagination Restore bloom. Short. Sensory. Kid's POV.)*

The heat comes on all at once. The forge opens and everything warms up — the stone, the floor, the air.

I can smell iron. I can smell something burning. Not bad burning. The good kind.

The Cowgirl comes back through the far door. She looks at the pegboard.

She doesn't say anything. Then:

THE COWGIRL: "One down."

KID: "We're going in."

*(The house. She means the house.)*

---

## Zone Lore Hook

The Garage is the hinge. Outside is imagination practiced in the open — wide skies, long sight lines, room to run. Inside is imagination in the dark, in the tight places, in the rooms where you don't have as much room to be wrong.

The Garage is the last step before the house. It's the room that knows both sides. You can still see the driveway from here — the outside, the cul-de-sac, the sky. And you can see the door to the kitchen.

What Kid learns here: the forge doesn't ask if you're ready. It's already hot. You either pick up the hammer or you don't.

### World Tree breadcrumb *(light — do not reveal)*

The forge runs without fuel. Kid notices it, the way she noticed the cherry tree. She doesn't name the connection. She just looks at the fire for a second longer than she needs to before she goes.

The Cowgirl notices her looking.

THE COWGIRL: "Don't ask me. I stopped asking."

*(She's seen something burning without fuel before. She has a theory. She's keeping it to herself.)*
