# Zone 2: The Cul-de-Sac

**What it actually is:** A cracked suburban dead-end. Minivans. Houses. Birdbath. Mailboxes. Stop signs. Perpetually mid-afternoon golden hour.

**What the kid sees:** A Wild West main street. Minivans become covered wagons. Houses become saloons. Birdbath becomes a water trough. Mailboxes become telegraph offices. Sky is burnt amber, dusty gold.

---

## Zone Intro Narration

He rounds the corner and the cul-de-sac opens up wide and amber and enormous.

The minivans are wagons. He can see the wheel spokes. He can see the canvas covers bleached by a sun that burns hotter here than anywhere else on the block. The houses are two-story saloons with hitching posts and the birdbath is a water trough and everything — the cracked asphalt, the dead grass circles, the mailboxes — everything has a new name.

The sky is the color of old pennies.

He's never been to a real Main Street. He doesn't need to have been.

At the far end of the cul-de-sac, where the four stop signs used to be, something broad-shouldered stands very still in the amber light. It has a badge where its face should be.

He checks his grip.

He walks toward it.

## Room Flavor Lines

**Saloon Row:**
"Three saloons. Which one has the people who need convincing and which one has the people who'll help. That's always the question."

**Telegraph Gulch:**
"The mailbox says something but the handwriting is too grey to read. That's fine. He knows the message anyway."

**Marshal's Standoff:**
"End of the street. No wagons past this point. That's the rule. He didn't agree to that rule."

## Ambient Lines — Unimaginative NPCs

1. "The HOA dues are still due. I don't know who I'm even paying anymore."

2. "I keep meaning to put the trash out. There's no pickup day now. I still keep meaning to."

3. "My neighbor hasn't moved her car in three weeks. I don't want to knock. What would I say."

4. "The cul-de-sac used to have kids in it. Running around. I don't know where they all went."

5. "I ordered something before the Internet stopped. It never came. I still check the porch."
