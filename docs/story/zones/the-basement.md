# Zone Lore — The Basement

**World 5.** Reality: a basement that was once a music room — carpet squares in four different colors, a drum kit with two cymbals missing, a faded band poster, a futon with a blanket someone left. Microphone stand. A rack of cardboard-sleeved records. Extension cords. The smell of old carpet and wood glue. The Unimaginative tagged everything visible and took nothing — there was no cardboard, no machinery, no infrastructure. Just someone's things. They locked the door at the top of the stairs and left it all here. Imagination: a deep underground amphitheater carved from living rock. The stage is lit from below. The seats go back into the dark farther than they should. The acoustics are enormous — every step, every hit, every word reverberates. The drum kit is still there. It sounds like thunder.

---

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Bottom of the Stairs. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the 3-second assembly beat as the amphitheater builds around her.)*

THE BASEMENT

The stairs go down longer than they should. At the bottom, there's carpet — soft under my feet, still soft after everything. The ceiling comes up in the dark, farther up than the house has floors.

The drum kit is in the corner. Two cymbals missing. Somebody left it exactly where they played it last.

When I step off the last stair, the floor remembers. It sounds like a stage.

KID: "Okay. So we're doing this."

## Zone Identity

The Basement is where the music was. Not practiced — *lived*. The band poster on the wall is hand-drawn. The setlist taped to the floor is from a show that maybe happened and maybe didn't. The microphone stand is adjusted to exactly one person's height and nobody moved it after.

The Unimaginative didn't understand any of this. They tagged the drum kit. They tagged the records. They left when nothing had a barcode that matched their manifest. You cannot catalogue a room where someone played music alone for years and felt like that was enough. It doesn't fit in a box. Not their kind.

Kid walks down here and the box reads it as a performance hall because that's the truest name for what it is: a place built for sound, for being heard, even if no one was listening. The amphitheater doesn't appear because the basement is grand. It appears because the basement was always trying to be.

What Kid learns here: the basement is still alive. The music didn't leave. The Unimaginative locked the door and the music had nowhere to go, so it stayed. Every room she clears, she can feel it in the floor.

## Enemy Roster

### The Static Shamblers

*(Room 1 and 2 — standard encounter enemy)*

**What they actually are:** The tangled extension cords. There are a lot of them — a decade of cables from amps, pedals, monitors, chargers, all coiled and piled and partially sorted and never fully sorted. The Unimaginative saw them and decided they were infrastructure waste. They didn't touch them. Nobody was going to plug them in.

They've been lying on the carpet in the dark since the door was locked, conducting small things through each other, quietly, the way old cables do when they've been asked to carry too much current for too long.

**What the box sees:** Crackling, low-to-ground creatures — slithering, multi-limbed, sparking at the joints. They move the way cord-tangles move: wrong directions at once, coiling back on themselves, parts of them always catching on something. They leave scorch-marks on the stone floor. The sparks are small but constant. They sound like static.

**Role:** Swarmer/hazard. They don't hit hard. There are just a lot of them and they come from everywhere at once. Each one is manageable. Three at a time, with restricted movement in a carpeted room, is a different problem.

**Behavioral Note:** Static Shamblers don't have a patrol. They move in directionless loops, doubling back, crossing paths, getting briefly stuck on each other. When Kid enters the room, they orient toward her — not all at once. One notices. Then the next. The awareness spreads through the tangle the way electricity does, from one to the next, until they've all found the same direction. The moment before they all turn: a long sparking hiss. One beat. Then they come.

What breaks the formation: anything that disrupts the floor. They lose cohesion when they can't get traction. On rough stone, they trip over each other. On smooth stone, they're fast. Kid learns to find the rough patches.

**Barks:** A sustained crackle, rising in pitch as they close in. No words. They are not creative enough for words.

---

### The Record Keepers

*(Room 2 and 3 — patrol enemy)*

**What they actually are:** The record sleeves. The cardboard sleeves — *cardboard*, but the Unimaginative didn't notice: they were looking for raw stock, not finished goods. The record sleeves have been standing in their rack in the dark for months, sleeved around vinyl, holding the exact shape of what they were printed to contain.

They've started walking with the records inside them like spines.

**What the box sees:** Flat, narrow heralds in stone-grey tabards, each one marked with a glyph — not a band name anymore, but a seal. They are precise in their movement. They patrol in straight lines, turn at exact right angles, return on the same path. They are keeping order in a place where no one asked them to keep order. They are doing it anyway.

**Role:** Patrol/blocker. They don't hit hard. They get in the way, consistently, without variance. Their predictability is the puzzle: they are perfectly regular, which means they can be read, which means the window is always there — Kid just has to watch one full cycle before she can time through it.

**Behavioral Note:** The Record Keepers patrol without pausing. They don't check corners. They don't deviate. When Kid disrupts one — damages it, moves it, breaks its path — the others stop for a full two-second beat. The patrol is synchronized. One broken link and the whole system has to recalibrate. That two-second beat is the tell. The window opens as soon as the others stop.

When a Record Keeper is defeated, the vinyl inside it slides out and lies flat on the stone floor. Undamaged. Still perfect.

**Barks:** A single long tone when they start moving. A click when they turn. Nothing else.

---

### The Feedback Stalker

*(Room 3 and 4 — elite. One per room.)*

**What it actually is:** The amplifier. Sixty watts, still warm. The Unimaginative tagged it and couldn't move it — the dolly they brought wasn't rated for it. They left it in the corner with a tag that says DEFERRED. It has been deferred for three months, still drawing current from somewhere, still warm at the grill.

**What the box sees:** A slow, enormous presence — a speaker cabinet that has learned to walk, moving on its four corner bumpers, the grill cloth rippling like breathing. It does not rush. It closes distance with terrible patience, the same way loud sound fills a room: from everywhere at once, until suddenly it is here.

Its attack is a sound wave. A visible one — a ring of compressed air that distorts everything in its path. In the amphitheater version it looks like a ripple in stone. In reality, it's just volume.

**Role:** Slow elite. It doesn't rush. It just gets closer. It creates a zone of distortion around itself — the screen shakes slightly within five feet of it, which disrupts Kid's read on other enemies. The solve: don't fight it in close quarters. Pull it into an open space with the rope. Let it commit. Dodge the wave. Punish the recovery.

**Enemy Behavior Note — for animators:**
The Feedback Stalker doesn't patrol. It listens. It is absolutely still until Kid makes noise — a hit on a Static Shambler, a boot on the stone floor, anything. Then, very slowly, it turns toward the sound. Once it's oriented, it moves. Not fast. Steady. It does not stop moving until it is defeated or Kid is out of the room. There is no idle state once it has heard. It remembers. The player cannot make it forget.

The moment it fires its wave: the whole cabinet shudders, the grill cloth billows out, and then — a half-beat of complete silence before the wave lands. That silence is the tell. The dodge window lives inside the silence.

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Futon Appearance

*(Room 3 — The Practice Corner. After the room fight. The Cowgirl is not here. The Female Ninja is not here. This is the quietest room in the zone.)*

*Design note: The player has been fighting Skeptic grunts throughout this zone — kids with grey boxes and clipboards, processing the room contents. This is not one of them. No clipboard. Not doing a job. Sitting on the futon reading the setlist. The grunts don't sit. The grunts don't read things that aren't on their manifest. Same box — different presence entirely.*

---

### The visual moment

The practice corner is unchanged. Everything the Unimaginative didn't tag, left where it was: the futon, the blanket, the setlist taped to the floor. The microphone stand. One floor lamp, still working.

The blanket on the futon is folded. Someone folded it after the last person left.

The one who keeps watching is sitting on the futon. Not performing sitting — actually sitting. Elbows on knees. Head forward, low. The box is on. The grey box, no crayon, nothing drawn. They are looking at the setlist taped to the floor in front of them.

They don't move when Kid enters.

They read the setlist for a long moment — or they've been reading it for a while, and Kid just arrived.

Then they look up.

---

### The line

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "Nobody's coming back to play this."

*The setlist is taped to the floor between them. The handwriting is a kid's — block letters, some corrections, band name at the top crossed out and rewritten. Six songs. Two of the song titles have been marked with asterisks but there are no notes explaining why.*

*The one who keeps watching isn't saying the music was bad. They're not saying it didn't matter. They're saying the specific people who made this specific setlist are not coming back to this room to play it.*

*They are not wrong.*

---

### What they leave behind

The blanket, folded.

Kid passes the futon on her way to the next room. The setlist is still taped to the floor. She can read it. The song titles are:

1. Opening\*
2. The Back One
3. Long Drive (reprise)
4. Opening (fast)\*
5. New One
6. Goodnight

*The asterisks have no notes. They never had notes. The notes were in whoever made this list's head, and they never wrote them down because they thought they'd remember.*

---

### Silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is the fifth time the player sees this kid. Every zone, they have been standing. In the Garage, they were at the workbench. In the School Hallway, they were at the trophy case. Here, they are sitting.

This is the only time they have sat down.

The animator should not explain this. Let it accumulate. Something about this room stopped them in a way the other rooms didn't.

**The read:** They came down here to do their job. They read the setlist. And they sat down.

**The departure:** They stand up. They fold the blanket — it was already folded, so they're refolding it. The exact same fold. Then they leave through the same door they came in, the direction they always go: toward whatever's ahead of Kid.

Whether they looked at the microphone stand is the animator's choice. Shoot both.

---

### How the space feels when they leave

The futon is there. The blanket is folded. The floor lamp is on.

The setlist is still taped to the floor. The lamp makes a warm circle and the setlist is inside it.

The room is quiet in a way the other rooms weren't quiet. Not empty. Quiet. There's a difference and Kid knows the difference.

She stands in the practice corner for one beat. She doesn't read the setlist again.

She goes.

## The Boss — The Session

*(Room 5 — The Stage)*

---

### What it actually is

The drum kit. The one with two cymbals missing — somebody took them when they left and didn't take the rest, or the rest wasn't theirs to take. Left behind: kick drum, snare, hi-hat, three toms, one ride cymbal, one crash cymbal, all the hardware, all the floor stands, and the bass drum pedal, which is the good pedal, the old one.

The Unimaginative tagged it. The asset tag is still on the kick drum: #A-0047. They couldn't load it onto the truck — it didn't break down right, or nobody knew how, or nobody had the time. They left it with the DEFERRED tag and moved on.

The drum kit has been in this room for three months with the door locked above it. Nobody coming down. Nobody playing. The stage lit from below — not by anything visible, just lit. The way a stage is lit when it's waiting.

It has been waiting.

---

### What the box sees

A creature of percussion — not a person, not a machine, something between: the drum kit assembled into a figure that has no single form, only motion. The kick drum is at the center of its chest. The toms cluster around it like orbiting mass. The ride and the crash extend on their stands like wing-arms, sweeping wide. The hi-hat opens and closes at irregular intervals — not as an attack, just as rhythm, constant rhythm underneath everything else.

It doesn't have a name on the stone lintel above the stage.

The stone lintel reads:

**THE SESSION**

---

### Boss Intro Text

*(Flavor card — one sentence.)*

"It's been sitting in the dark for three months, keeping time for nobody. Now there's somebody."

---

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — The Beat.** The Session fights in rhythm. Everything it does is on the beat — attacks land on one and three, sweeps land on two and four, the hi-hat marks the tempo in between. The tempo is moderate at first. Learnable. The first ten seconds of the fight, The Session just... plays. No aggression. It establishes the pattern. It waits for Kid to find the rhythm.

Once Kid has moved with the beat three times — dodged on the beat, attacked on the beat — The Session accelerates. This is the tell: the hi-hat doubles. The tempo goes up by thirty percent. Everything is the same pattern, faster. Kid has to let go of the specific tempo she learned and feel the new one. The lesson from the Garage applies here: stop reading the diagram, start moving with it.

Between the beat attacks: the Ride cymbal sweeps — a wide, low arc that crosses the full stage. The tell is the stand rotating. One rotation before the sweep. Kid has to see the rotation before the sweep starts.

**Phase 2 — The Breakdown.** The beat stops. Complete stop. The hi-hat closes. The room is absolutely silent for three seconds. Then: one kick drum hit. One. The Session is now fighting with no pattern — each hit isolated, no tempo, no predict. A single kick. A crash arm sweep. Silence. Another kick, different direction. The player spent Phase 1 learning rhythm. Phase 2 removes it.

What Kid has: instinct. And the rope.

The rope can hook the bass drum pedal — the heaviest anchor point on the stage. If Kid hooks it and pulls during The Session's windup, she yanks the center of mass off-balance. The whole structure staggers. The window is enormous — three full seconds of recovery. But she has to be close enough to hook it and far enough away from the crash arm, both at once.

The Cowgirl left a rope hook near the stage door. If Kid brought her grapple in, she already knows what to do.

**Phase 3 — The Last Song.** The two missing cymbals reappear. They arrive from opposite sides of the stage — sliding in across the floor, onto their stands, clicking into place. The Session is complete.

The beat returns. But it's different now: it's playing something, not attacking with it. It is playing the setlist. The one taped to the floor in Room 3. Track 1, Opening. Track 5, New One. Not in order. But the song shapes are there — a 4/4 groove for "Long Drive," the doubled kick for "Opening (fast)." The player can hear it if they're listening.

The fight in Phase 3 is everything: both phase patterns running together, the full kit, tempo fluctuating between the tracks. It is not harder than Phase 2. It is fuller. It is the most alive The Session has been in three months.

Kid has to fight it at full speed, and she can hear it playing, and it's actually good.

### Victory Moment

The last hit lands and The Session stops. All at once. Mid-phrase — whatever track it was playing, it stops on a beat before the end.

The hi-hat settles open. The ride cymbal sings out one last note and fades.

The bass drum pedal is still.

Kid stands on the stage. The amplifiers are warm. The floor lamp from Room 3 is visible through the doorway.

Then, from somewhere in the room — from the walls, from the stone, from everywhere the sound has been bouncing for three months — the last note comes back. The one The Session stopped on. It returns, resonated, softer than it was, like an echo that took a second to arrive.

It fades.

KID: "Good set."

*She means it.*

## Zone Lore Hook

The Basement is where the music was put. Not displayed — put. Hidden, in the good way, the way you put something somewhere safe. The Unimaginative came down and saw gear and carpeting and nothing worth taking, and they were right about that. You can't take music. You can remove the instruments and the music doesn't go with them. It stays in the room where it was played. It waits.

Kid goes down here and the box reads it as a stage because that is the truest name for any room where someone played and believed it mattered. The amphitheater is enormous not because the room is large but because the music in it was. Three months of silence and it's still enormous. The acoustics work. The reverb comes back.

What the Unimaginative missed: they locked the door at the top of the stairs.

The music couldn't get out.

It's been building in here ever since.

### World Tree breadcrumb *(very light — barely a thread)*

In Room 4, there is a record on the floor. Not from the rack — the rack's records are untouched. This one was on top of the amplifier. It was taped there, face-up, and the tape failed, and it slid down and landed face-up on the floor, and it has been there since.

The sleeve is hand-drawn. Kid-made — crayon and marker. The band name on the sleeve is a name that doesn't mean anything.

But in the corner of the sleeve, very small, drawn in pencil over the crayon: a tree. Not a specific tree. Just a tree. Old and large, the kind you draw when you want to draw a tree that has been there for a long time.

Kid picks it up. She turns it over. The back of the sleeve has a track listing written in the same kid-handwriting as the setlist in Room 3.

Track 7 has been scratched out. Below it, in smaller writing, someone wrote: *ask the tree.*

Kid puts it in her pocket.

She doesn't say what she found. Not yet. The Cowgirl is ahead of her on the stairs.

## Weapons Introduced

### The Drumstick Pair

*(Picked up from the snare drum after clearing Room 3. They were resting on the snare, crossed, the way you leave them when you're done but coming back.)*

**Real object:** Two drumsticks. Hickory, worn at the grip. One has a crack near the tip that someone wrapped with electrical tape.

**Imagined weapon:** Twin impact mallets — short-range, rapid-strike, both hands. They don't have range. They have *rhythm*. Consecutive hits build a combo multiplier that resets if Kid pauses more than a half-second between strikes. Maximum combo: eight hits, after which the last strike lands with a sound like a full drum fill — audible to enemies in adjacent rooms.

**Flavor text:**
"Drumsticks → The Two"
"The tape on the right one is older than the tape on the left one. Different player, different grip. They figured it out."

**Diegetic tutorial moment:**

*(Room 4, first enemy cluster. Kid has the sticks. She's never used them. She looks at the Static Shamblers coming in.)*

KID: "Okay. I have to be fast."

*(She hits the first one. The combo counter starts. She doesn't look at it. She just keeps going.)*

### The Grapple Rope — Third Use

The rope gets its third and final teaching here: hook an anchor point during a boss windup to redirect the boss's own weight. The Cowgirl left the hook near the stage door. Kid finds it. She figures out what it's for by looking at the bass drum pedal and looking at the hook.

She doesn't say anything about the discovery. She just picks up the hook and tests the line.

## Tone Note — What Makes This Zone Different

Every zone before the Basement was about movement. The hallway moved you forward. The garage made you a maker. The dojo shaped you. The cul-de-sac opened you up.

The Basement is the first zone where somebody already finished something. The music was made here. Not in progress — finished, rehearsed, performed for no one or for each other. The setlist was complete. The good pedal was left on purpose. The blanket was folded.

The Unimaginative locked the door and assumed they had contained it. They don't understand that things that are finished don't need to be tended. They just wait.

Kid comes down here and everything she finds has already been cared for. She doesn't have to save it. She has to free it.

That's a different task than the ones before. She will carry it to the next floor.

The stairs at the end of the zone go up. The Attic is above her.

She can hear it from here.

She doesn't know what she's hearing yet.
