# Zone Lore — The Backyard (Dojo)

**World 2.** Reality: an overgrown backyard flagged for lot clearance. Imagination: a feudal dojo courtyard under a cherry tree.

---

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Back Gate. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the 3-second assembly beat as the dojo builds itself around her.)*

THE BACKYARD

I push through the back gate and the weeds go quiet. The grass stops leaning. It stands up straight, all of it, in rows. The shed's roof lifts. Stones find the path like they'd been waiting to. There's a tree at the far end shaking pink all over the ground.

I've never done this before. That's a lie. I've done this a hundred times, right here, with a stick. This is where you learn it.

KID: "Let's go."

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Koi Pond Appearance

*(Room C, The Koi Pond. Placement: after the room's fight settles, as the player crosses the engawa boards toward the shed. Quiet, not cruel. Leave silence after the line. No rebuttal.)*

*Design note — two kinds of Skeptics:*
*The player has been fighting Skeptic grunts throughout this zone — kids with grey boxes and clipboards, doing their assigned job. This is not one of them. No clipboard. No task. Just standing in the shed doorway, holding a folded lawn chair, looking at the koi pond. They are not assigned here.*

*This is the second zone. The player may not have consciously connected this to the kid from the Cul-de-Sac yet. The recognition builds slowly — by the third zone, it begins to land. See `docs/story/characters/the-skeptic.md`.*

---

### The visual moment

The dojo is at its most finished here. The pond is glass-still. A wind chime turns on the veranda with one tube missing, still trying to ring. Two pairs of flip-flops sit neat on the boards — one adult-sized, one child-sized. A coffee mug on the rail with a dried ring inside. It's the kind of place someone loved on a morning that didn't come.

The one who keeps watching is standing in the open shed doorway. Just a kid — grey box, plain clothes, nothing special about the way they're standing. They could be waiting for a bus. They're holding a folded lawn chair.

They set it down on the engawa. Not dramatically. Not to make a point. Just — it was in their hands, now it's on the boards, one motion, flat and open like something waiting to be loaded onto a truck. The petals stop landing on that one spot. The chime's tube goes the color of concrete.

The kid looks at where Kid is standing — at the pond, at the blossoms, at all of it — and their face does not change.

Then they say their line.

Then they leave. The doorway's empty by the time Kid crosses to it. The lawn chair is still there, still flat, still grey. Nothing lands on it.

---

### The line

*(Confirmed. Flat delivery. One sentence. Then silence.)*

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "Nobody's coming back for the chairs."

*This line is the loneliest of the three original options. It points at the two pairs of flip-flops on the boards — one big, one small — and names the fact of their abandonment without naming anything about imagination. The one who keeps watching isn't making an argument. They're reading the yard correctly. The two pairs of shoes are just there. Nobody is coming back for them.*

*What the player feels is not the line. It's the silence after the line. The chime keeps turning with its missing tube. The petals keep falling — except on the one grey spot where the chair is.*

---

### The silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is a kid who has been inside for years, looking at screens, watching other people's lives at the remove of a feed. When The Hush came, they walked outside for the first time in a long time. They stood in a yard like this one. They looked at it.

They saw a yard.

Now they work for the Unimaginative. Not because they were recruited with speeches or promises. Because when the grey organization showed up with structure and a role, it felt like finally being handed a phone again — something clear to hold, something that told you what to do. They fit.

**Idle behavior:** They stand the way someone stands when they've been told to wait and they're good at waiting. Weight slightly back. Not tense — there's nothing to be tense about. They look at the thing Kid is doing the way you look at a loading screen. Not impatient. Just present until it stops.

**Movement:** Even and purposeful. They don't browse or wander. They have somewhere to be and they're going there. The disturbing thing is the pace — a kid should have more somewhere to be than this. They shouldn't move this much like an adult who has stopped being surprised by things.

**When they leave:** They just go. No look back. No drama. The lawn chair was already doing the work.

---

### How the space feels when they leave

Nothing dramatic. No fight, no damage. Just: for a second the pond doesn't reflect the tree. Then it does again. The chime finds its turn. The petals come back.

But the lawn chair stays flat on the boards, open and empty, and Kid has to step around it, and keeps half-looking at it the rest of the room.

That's the whole trick of the Skeptic. They don't take the color. They just leave one grey thing behind and let Kid do the rest.

### Victory beat — what Kid notices after the Grasscutter falls

*(Plays after the boss defeat, over the Imagination Restore bloom. Short. Sensory. Kid's POV.)*

The cherry tree goes all at once — not petal by petal, all at once. Pink everywhere. I can smell it. I didn't know a tree could smell like that.

I look back at the engawa. The chair is still there.

KID: "Somebody planted you."

*(To the tree. Quiet. Then she goes.)*

## Zone Lore Hook

The backyard is where kids *learned* — the first place imagination was practiced instead of just used. A stick became a sword here before it became anything anywhere else. Reclaiming this zone restores the idea that skill is made, not handed to you. That's why the enemies here teach: the Crane duels you into patience, the Grasscutter opens as a blade-master before it becomes a machine.

### World Tree breadcrumb *(light — do not reveal)*

The cherry tree at the center of the court is younger than everything around it. Too young to be this tall. Too young to bloom this hard. Kid notices, in the victory beat, that *somebody planted it* — and doesn't have a word yet for why that matters.

Keep this a texture, not a plot point:
- The tree is the only thing in the yard nobody could have raked, mowed, or bagged into submission. It's the one living thing.
- Its blossoms are the zone's unique Imagination Restore trigger — the yard comes back *through* the tree.
- No character says "World Tree." No note explains it. The single load-bearing line is Kid's "Somebody planted you," delivered as wonder, not exposition.
- Payoff belongs to a later zone. This is the first time a player might feel that living things are *seeded*, not just found — a feeling, not information.
