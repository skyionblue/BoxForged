# Zone Lore — The World Tree

**World 8. The final zone.** Reality: a three-hundred-year-old tree at the center of an industrial ring — alive, surrounded, still growing. Imagination: a living fortress, the last one standing, the only one that always was.

---

## Canonical Decisions — For All Writers

- **The World Tree is a seed bank, not a battery.** Kid does not drain it or absorb its power. She opens the gate to the seed chamber. The tree does the rest — it makes the next tree, the next hundred trees, the next three hundred years of cardboard reaching kids who need it. Kid's imagination was always hers. The tree makes the *next* kid's access possible.
- **The cherry tree at the Backyard Dojo is a descendant of the World Tree.** This is a breadcrumb — no character says it. It is too young and too tall. It was planted. Kid notices, without connecting the dots. Payoff lives here.
- **Mr. Wen's tomato plant is in the World Tree root system.** Not a prop with a quest marker. An environmental detail, discoverable at play level, no dialogue required. The connection is not stated. It is true.
- **The Processing Authority does not collapse when defeated.** It stands. It did its job correctly. Do not give it a dramatic death. It stands with what it completed.
- **The paper crane is Kid's final act.** Folded from the blank forms she carried out of the Cardboard Mill. Set on the root. The wind takes it. No dialogue explaining why. The player who has been paying attention will know why.
- **Kid does not take the box off.** Not here, not in the credits. If it ever comes off, that is a later story. This ending is not it.
- **The Cowgirl's rope is a grapple only — no swing.** No Tarzan. No pendulum arc. It pulls. It pulls Kid toward a point, or it pulls an enemy toward Kid. Two uses. No swing. This is established in the Garage and carries forward.

## Before the Zone Begins

*(This is not a zone intro. This is what happens before the gates open.)*

Kid walks out of the Cardboard Mill into open air for the first time since the School Hallway.

She stops.

The mills are enormous up close — they ring the tree in a full circle, grey walls four stories high, no windows on the outer face, the low vibration of the presses coming through the ground into her feet. She can see the top of the tree over the walls. Just the canopy. Green. It moves slightly in a wind she cannot feel down here.

She has seven zones of material in her pockets. The World Tree fragments. The papers from the Attic. The blank forms she folded at the mill. The grapple rope. The Waterwhip, coiled.

She does not think about any of this. She is looking at the canopy.

The hum begins in her chest, low and continuous, the same frequency she felt at the Dojo cherry tree. But stronger. The box is warm against the sides of her head — not hot, just: warm. Like something that has been waiting to get warmer, and the wait is almost over.

She has been here before, somehow. Not this building, not this ring of walls. The hum. The weight of something alive pressing through stone and metal and the cold ground under her feet.

KID: "I can feel it."

*(She wasn't going to say that. She says it anyway.)*

KID: "Let's go."

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Root Passage. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the assembly beat as the World Tree zone constructs itself — the grey walls becoming something older, something that knows its own shape.)*

THE WORLD TREE

The roots broke through the mill floor years ago. The Unimaginative documented it and continued operations. I am standing in a crack in their floor wide enough to walk through, and the walls on either side are stone that didn't come from here — smooth, dark, older than this neighborhood, older than the road, probably older than the name of this city. The tree made this passage. Not for me. But I fit.

Something up in the canopy makes a low sound. Not wind. Not birds — there are no birds anymore, not around this much grey. It hums. I can hear it with my body before I hear it with my ears.

I know what this is. I've been walking toward it since the backyard.

KID: "I knew it was bigger."

*(A breath.)*

KID: "Okay. I'm ready."

*(She isn't, quite. She goes anyway.)*

## Zone Identity

Every zone before this one was a place Kid arrived in.

The World Tree is a place that has been waiting.

The difference is in the structure. The mill ring, viewed from inside, is not the same as the mill ring viewed from outside. Outside: grey walls, industrial, featureless. Inside: the grey is a shell. The shell cracked when the roots came through, when the branches grew over, when the tree kept growing in every direction that was available to it. The Unimaginative repaired the cracks as they appeared — that's documented in Note 2, found in Room 3. They kept repairing. The tree kept cracking. Three hundred years of not stopping.

What the box sees: a living fortress that was never constructed. It grew. The roots are the walls — not grown into the shape of walls, but substantial in the way of something that has been here long enough to become load-bearing by default. The canopy overhead is the ceiling, not light-blocking but light-filtering, breaking the grey into moving pieces. The ground is root-surface, not flat, requiring stepping and balance, no two surfaces the same. Everywhere: the texture of something that has decided to stay.

Kid has been moving through drained places for seven zones. Places that used to be alive and were taken. This place was taken and is still alive. The Unimaginative kept it alive because they needed it. The tree did not know this and would not have cared. It grew anyway. Three hundred years of growing regardless.

This is the zone where imagination and the living world are the same thing. Not metaphorically. The tree is real. The hum is real. The thing Kid has been moving toward is an actual tree that has been here longer than any of The Unimaginative's paperwork.

That's the whole argument. The tree makes it by existing.

## Enemy Roster

### Root Guardians

*(All rooms except the safe zone and the boss arena. Standard encounter enemy for this zone.)*

**What they actually are:** The Unimaginative's final line of Skeptics — the ones assigned to the innermost ring. Not the most experienced, but the longest-serving in this specific location. They have been processing zone compliance at the World Tree since month one. They know every root, every crack, every place the floor becomes uneven. They have clipboards with the full manifest of everything the tree has produced: board footage, fiber weight, projected yield. They know the tree as numbers. They have never looked up at it.

**What the box sees:** The same grey-boxed Skeptics Kid has been fighting since the Cul-de-Sac, but the imagined space does something here: they are large. Not larger than before — the imagination does not scale them up for drama. They are large in the way of something standing between you and something you've walked this far to reach. The space matters. They are in it.

They fight with clipboards and document stacks the same as always. The terrain — the roots, the uneven ground — works against them as much as Kid. They have the plans for this place. They don't have its shape in their bodies.

**Behavioral note — for animators:**
Root Guardians know the floor. They have been navigating this root-terrain for months and they have learned it intellectually — they know which roots are ankle-height, which gaps are crossable, which patches of ground are soft near the drainage. They navigate it by memory, looking down when they move. Which means they look down when they move. Their heads are down on the uneven ground. Up on the flat. Down on the roots. This is subtle, but an animator who gets it right creates something distinctive: a Skeptic who knows a place and has never once looked at it.

Kid reads the terrain through the box — she sees the roots as paths, not obstacles. She looks up while the Root Guardians look down. The fight is spatial: she has the high ground and the sight lines. They have the numbers and the manifest.

**Barks (flat, procedural):**
"Stop."
"This area is restricted."
"You're not on the access list."

### Permit Pulper Remnants — The Filed Forms

*(Room 2 and Room 3 only. Two per room. Mini-encounter.)*

These are not new enemies. They are the blank forms Kid picked up at the Cardboard Mill.

They are also what the Permit Pulper ejected from itself when she defeated it — the processed remainder of everything it had assessed and cleared. Each one is a thin figure, paper-flat, moving in the way that documents move: slid, stacked, filed. They are carried by the mill's lingering processes — a conveyor system that extends, invisibly, all the way back from the mill to the Tree.

**What the box sees:** Forms that have learned to walk. Flat. Each one a different size, corresponding to the asset it was meant to reassign. One is the size of a lawn chair. One is the size of a koi pond. One is the size of a school building. When Kid hits them, they scatter into fragments of language — partial words, assessment codes, approval stamps — that dissolve before landing.

**Role:** Nuisance encounter. They are not strong. They are fast. They come in pairs and they move in the opposite direction from each other — one goes for Kid, one circles to the exit point. The instinct is to stop the one coming at her. The smart move is to catch the one moving away first, because the one coming at her can be interrupted. The one moving away cannot.

This is the Permit Pulper's last act: a process that continues after the machine stopped. Kid doesn't finish it by fighting the forms. She finishes it by getting through the room before the forms can complete their circuit. The manifest is still running. She keeps moving.

**Behavioral note — for animators:**
They don't look at Kid. They look at the exit. They are not interested in her; they are interested in completing their route. This makes them read like paper in a wind: purposeful in a direction that isn't about her, indifferent to what she does along the way. The horror is the indifference. She fights them and they just keep trying to file themselves.

**Barks:** The rustle of paper. An administrative stamp sound. Nothing verbal.

## Room Structure and Pacing

### Room 1 — The Root Passage

The crack in the mill floor that the roots made. Narrow enough to require single-file movement at points, then opening into the first full root-chamber: a space where three large roots converge at a flat landing, a natural clearing that the tree created and the Unimaginative documented as "structural irregularity, non-load-bearing, do not remove."

Encounter: four Root Guardians. They know the passage but fight in a line — they cannot flank here. Kid learns the terrain rhythm: step up onto a root, step down on the other side, the ground is never flat, plan two moves ahead because the third move depends on what's under your feet.

This is the first encounter that explicitly teaches positioning through terrain rather than through enemy behavior. Kid learns it by doing it. The Root Guardians help, in their way, by not having learned it themselves.

**Safe zone beat:** After the room clears. A wide root at shoulder height makes a bench — it has always been a bench in this imagined space, the bark smooth on the top, the exact width of somewhere to sit. Kid sits. The hum is louder here, closer to the source.

*(She looks at the crack she came through. At the grey wall above it.)*

KID: "They kept fixing this."

*(She looks at the root she's sitting on.)*

KID: "It kept coming back."

*(A beat. She's not in a hurry for the first time in eight zones. She sits.)*

*(Then:)*

KID: "Okay. What's next."

*(Not a question. She gets up.)*

### Room 2 — The Lower Chamber

The first full interior space. The roots have built something here over decades — arches, natural vaulting, a floor that is entirely root-surface, no grey concrete visible. The Unimaginative built collection points here: four tap-and-funnel rigs that draw sap from the secondary roots, tube it up through the wall, and deliver it to the pressed-board process. The funnels are still running. The sap is amber in the tube — slow-moving, heavy, the color of the thing before it becomes cardboard.

Encounter: three Root Guardians and two Filed Forms. The Filed Forms move toward the chamber's two exits while the Guardians engage. Kid has to read both at once — the Guards in front, the Forms slipping around the sides. The terrain favors her: she can use the root-arches to move laterally fast, changing plane mid-fight.

**Zone weapon — introduced here:**

### The Root Graft

*(Found: in the sap-collection rig on the east wall — a maintenance tool for splicing tap lines, a long-handled device with a curved blade at one end and a clamp at the other.)*

**Real object:** A grafting tool for tree maintenance. Used to cut a notch in a branch and insert a scion — a splice. Makes new growth out of old root.

**Imagined weapon:** The Root Graft. Melee, mid-range, with a unique mechanic: the clamp end can latch onto an enemy for a moment, not to damage but to anchor — one second of hold, then release. During that second, Kid has the window. She learned the counter-window at the beginning of the game. The Root Graft gives her one she creates herself.

The imagined version looks like a living thing: the handle is dark wood with grain that moves slightly when held, like something still growing. The blade is stone-hard but organic-shaped, not machine-cut. It feels like the tree put it here for this specific moment.

**Flavor text:**
"Grafting tool → The Root Graft"
"A graft takes something living and gives it a new place to grow. The tree has been doing this for three hundred years. Kid figures out the second part fast."

**Diegetic tutorial:**

*(Kid picks up the tool. Tests the clamp — it opens and closes, spring-loaded.)*

KID: "It holds. Just for a second."

*(She looks at the Root Guardians. At the gap between their movements.)*

KID: "That's enough of a second."

### Room 3 — The Canopy Approach

The last room before the boss and the One Who Keeps Watching's appearance. The roots here have grown through the mill's upper floor — there is sky visible through the gaps, real sky, no grey ceiling for the first time in the game since Room 1 of World 1. The tree trunk is visible: enormous, dark, closer. Forty feet across at this height. The bark is deeply grooved and the grooves run upward, toward the canopy, and something in those grooves is moving — not sap, not water. Something else. The hum is loudest here.

Encounter: five Root Guardians and two Filed Forms. This is the hardest combat room in the zone — not because the enemies are stronger, but because the room is open and the gaps in the floor (where roots broke through the upper level) require Kid to manage her positioning or fall through. The Root Graft is essential here: anchor one Guardian long enough to reposition, deal with the others, come back.

The Filed Forms target the two openings in the floor. If they reach them, they fall through — and Kid hears, from below, the sound of something being filed. The forms have completed their route. She did not stop them. This doesn't affect the game mechanically, but the sound is specific and the player will remember it.

After combat: quiet. The hum. The trunk forty feet away, visible through the last mill wall. The roots that broke through the ceiling let in wind. Kid can feel the wind for the first time since Room 1 of the Cardboard Mill.

She looks at the trunk.

She looks at the grooves running upward.

KID: "It never stopped."

*(Not wonder. Certainty. She knew this. She just hadn't seen it yet.)*

### The One Who Keeps Watching — World Tree Appearance

*(Room 3 — post-combat. The fighting is done. The room is quiet. The hum fills the space the fighting left behind.)*

---

#### Context

Eight zones. Eight appearances. Eight lines that named the material facts of everything that was taken — the chairs, the water, the school, the tools, the words, the plans, the idea of building something here. Each sentence a flat reading of a world that has gone wrong, stated without argument, because why argue with what is simply true.

This is the ninth appearance. The last one.

---

#### The visual moment

The one who keeps watching is standing near the trunk. Close to it — closer than anyone has been in the game. Not touching it. Just near it.

Their box is grey. It was always grey. It has been grey through eight zones and two hundred rooms and the accumulated wreckage of everything the Unimaginative took.

But they are standing near the trunk and the hum is moving through the floor and through the roots and through the stone and it is moving through them too, whether they know it or not.

They are not looking at Kid.

They are looking at the trunk. At the grooves running upward. At the thing in the grooves, moving.

Their hands are open at their sides. They have been getting to open hands slowly — by the Mill, their hands were open. Here they are still open, and the open hands are different, because nothing is being offered and nothing is being reached for. They are just: open. As if the argument they've been having in their head for eight zones has run out of what to hold.

*(The animator should not make this a moment. It is a kid standing near a tree with their hands at their sides. Nothing more. The player brings the weight. The player has been carrying this figure through eight zones. Let them carry this too.)*

---

#### The silence

The one who keeps watching does not speak.

They stand there. The hum moves through the floor. The wind from the canopy gaps shifts the air in the room — the first wind in the zone, the first wind in two full worlds. The grooves in the trunk carry their moving thing upward.

The one who keeps watching looks at all of it.

They do not say anything.

After a moment, they leave. Same walk as always — purposeful, even, going somewhere. But there is nowhere assigned for them to be and the player knows that now. There is no mission here. They came here for the same reason Kid came here, and that reason cannot be filed or processed or reassigned to waste-paper status.

*(There is no line. Do not write a line. The silence is the line.)*

---

#### What they leave behind

Nothing grey. Not this time.

Where they were standing, on the root surface near the trunk, a small patch of moss. Real moss — green, damp, alive. It was already there. They were standing on it. When they leave, it is visible.

Whether they noticed it, whether they stood there deliberately, whether it means something — leave this uncertain. Both readings should be available. Let the player decide if the one who keeps watching chose the soft ground on purpose.

Kid looks at the moss.

She doesn't say anything about it.

She keeps going.

---

#### The design read for the player

By the eighth zone, the player has constructed a theory about the one who keeps watching. Multiple theories, probably — this is a figure designed to accumulate meaning without resolving it. The silence at the World Tree does not resolve the theories. It adds one thing to each of them: the one who keeps watching, in the presence of the tree, had nothing correct to say.

Whether that is the beginning of something or the end of the argument — the game does not say. The game has never said. The game leaves it where it leaves everything: in what the player carries out of the room.

## Boss — The Processing Authority

*(The Inner Ring — the circular clearing at the base of the trunk, where the Unimaginative built the access gate to the mill systems. This is the largest space in the zone and the only one where the full trunk is visible, all forty feet of circumference, the canopy above.)*

---

### What it actually is

The gate system. The final intake mechanism — the Unimaginative's designed solution for processing everything the tree produces in real time, without bottleneck, without interruption. Not a machine someone built. A process that accumulated: eight months of decisions, access logs, yield optimizations, and compliance reviews, all of which had to be authorized, all of which required sign-off, all of which generated paperwork that was filed here, at the base, where it could be cross-referenced and audited and corrected.

The Processing Authority is not a person. It is not a machine in the way the Permit Pulper was a machine. It is the accumulated weight of every decision that was made to keep the tree contained. Every form that authorized a new tap line. Every manifest that approved a fiber extraction. Every compliance review that confirmed operations within acceptable parameters. Every permit that was filed so that what was being done to the tree could be done correctly, because it is important to do things correctly even when what you are doing is this.

It does not know it is a thing. It has never needed to know. It has been authorizing itself for eight months.

When Kid enters the inner ring, the system recognizes a non-compliant variable. The assessment begins. The accumulated authority assembles.

---

### What the box sees

A figure built from permissions. Not from metal or compressed board — from the weight of what was decided. It takes a form because it has always had a form: the form of something official, something final, something that exists at the end of every process that requires authorization.

It is tall. Not impossibly — just taller than anything Kid has fought. The height of something that has been in charge for a long time. Its surface is layered: form upon form upon form, each layer a different density, the oldest ones at the center, compressed and almost smooth, the newest ones at the edges, still sharp.

Its face, if it has one, is a seal. Stamped. The kind of stamp that makes a thing official — circular, detailed, meaningless to read but absolute in what it means. When the seal is visible, decisions have been made. Kid has been living in the aftermath of decisions made by something wearing that face since before the game started.

Its name — visible only through the box, the way all the bosses' imagined names are visible — is not stamped on the arena floor. It is stamped on the gate behind it: the gate that is the last barrier between Kid and the trunk's base, the place where the World Tree fragments belong.

**THE PROCESSING AUTHORITY**

---

### Boss Intro

*(One line. Flavor card. Before the music starts.)*

"It has been authorizing operations at the World Tree for eight months. It has never denied a request. It has never needed to. Nobody has filed one."

### Fight Notes

**Phase 1 — Initial Assessment.**

The Processing Authority does not move at the start. It stands in the center of the inner ring, the seal-face forward, and it assesses. Three full rotations — slowly, the whole circumference of the ring — reading every variable. Kid is a variable. The World Tree fragments in her pocket are variables. The Root Graft in her hand is a variable.

During the assessment, the authority does not attack. It reads.

Kid can attack during this phase. Each hit stresses the outermost layer of forms — they flutter, they scatter, new ones take their place from somewhere inside the structure. She is damaging process, not the thing underneath process. The outermost layers are not where the fight is. She needs to reach the layers that have been there longer.

The attack when assessment ends: a ruling. The Processing Authority extends both arms — not reaching, ruling — and everything in its radius becomes a decision that has been made. The zone of ruling is wide. The edges of it are visible, a grey shimmer like a light passing through paper held up to a window. Inside the zone, movement is slowed — not because of any physical force, but because every action inside the zone requires authorization, and authorization takes time. Kid has to be outside the ruling zone when it lands.

The counter: the Root Graft's clamp on the outermost layer of forms. One second of anchor. One second where the ruling cannot extend outward because the form-layer is engaged. In that second, Kid hits the layer underneath.

**Phase 2 — Reclassification.**

Some of the outer forms are gone. The inner layers are visible — compressed, dark, the older decisions. The Processing Authority moves now: a slow, orbital path around the trunk, keeping the gate behind it, maintaining the barrier between Kid and the access point. It moves the way a bureaucracy moves: inevitably. No urgency. No hurry. Just continuation.

New behavior: the Processing Authority begins processing Kid's weapons. Not destroying them — processing them. The Waterwhip flickers during Phase 2; its mid-range zone shrinks as the Authority reclassifies it from "weapon" to "pressure equipment" (assets in that category have restricted use zones). The Root Graft's clamp mechanic shortens in duration — from one second to half. The Authority cannot remove the weapons. It cannot take imagination. But it can make the working harder, slower, less clear. The rules keep shifting.

This phase is where the fight earns its place in the game. Kid has to work with tools that are being actively redefined underneath her. She has done this before — the Waterwhip at close range, the Grapple Rope as a repositioner — she has always been learning the second layer of what a thing can do. Now the Authority is trying to reduce everything to a first layer and keep it there.

She doesn't let it.

The pattern: Waterwhip extension at the maximum range that's still available, Root Graft clamp at the end of the reach, Waterwhip follow-through into the compressed inner layers. Three hits per window. The window closes when the Authority completes its next orbital pass and the ruling arm extends.

**Phase 3 — Final Processing.**

The compressed layers are visible now. The forms are almost gone — scattered across the arena floor, readable for the first time: approval dates, signature lines, the formal language of authorization. Kid can read fragments of them as she moves. She doesn't stop to read them. She keeps moving.

The Processing Authority stops orbiting. It stands between Kid and the gate. Still.

This is not a dramatic final stance. It is a bureaucratic fact: the gate is here, and the gate requires authorization, and authorization requires the Authority, and the Authority has not authorized this.

It is not trying to stop Kid anymore. It simply has not authorized her to proceed. Whether it can feel the difference — the game does not say.

It rushes. Not with anger — with completion. Every process it has run for eight months has been building to this point: one decision, final, requiring the full accumulated weight of every prior decision to enforce it. It carries everything it ever decided. That is what it runs at her with.

Kid's job: three full Waterwhip extensions, timed to the gaps between the authority's accumulated weight shifting. The compressed forms make it slow — it cannot turn, it cannot adjust, it can only commit and complete. She moves around the commitment. She steps outside the ruling zone. She hits the gaps.

Three.

**The final moment.**

The authority slows. The forms fall. Not dramatically — they settle the way a stack of papers settles when the bottom is pulled. Each form finds the ground. The seal-face dims.

It does not collapse. It stands. Every process it ran was run correctly. It did not make errors. It did not fail to authorize anything that should have been authorized. It did its job, completely, for eight months, and the job was this.

It stands with that. Then it is still.

Kid walks past it to the gate.

The gate is a physical mechanism — a lock system of tubes and valves that control the final output line. The World Tree fragments fit into specific slots in the mechanism, each one a key that the Unimaginative designed to require a formal authorization code. The authorization code was designed so that authorization could not come from outside the system. The system required itself to open itself.

Kid puts the first fragment in.

The lock was designed for a code. The fragment is not a code. It is a piece of the tree — actual living fiber, kept living somehow, hers for seven zones. The mechanism accepts it anyway. The tree recognizes what belongs to it.

Second fragment. Third. The lock opens with a sound that is not mechanical — it is the sound of the hum, louder, and then louder than that, and then the gate opens and the inner mechanism engages and somewhere above the canopy begins to move differently, a slow turning toward something that has been waiting three hundred years to arrive.

### Victory Moment — The Mills Change

*(Plays in sequence after the gate opens. This is not a brief beat. This is the ending beginning.)*

---

The gate opens.

The inner ring shifts — the access tubes that were pointed at the trunk, extracting, rotate. The mechanism the Processing Authority was built to protect was not designed for what Kid just did with it. The fragments unlocked something the Unimaginative did not know was there to unlock, because their engineers designed the gate and never looked at what was behind it.

What's behind it: the seed chamber. The part of the tree that is not cardboard and not fiber and not yield. The part that makes the next tree. That makes the next hundred trees. That makes the next three hundred years of living cardboard, reaching every kid who has been sitting in a quiet house since The Hush with nothing but the box they found in the recycling and a pocketknife and a gap in a fence that leads somewhere.

The mills do not stop. This is important. Kid did not turn them off. She changed what they are for.

The hum shifts register — lower and lower until it is below sound and is just: present. Like weather. Like something the air does here and has always done here and was doing even when the mills were running at capacity and the grey board was moving and the forms were being filed and everything was correctly, efficiently, completely wrong.

The first thing that changes: the board on the mill floor. The grey board. It pulses once — the bloom Kid saw at the end of World 7, the natural brown moving through the grey like a wave — and this time it doesn't settle back. It keeps going. The brown reaches the edges. Then past the edges. Fiber by fiber. The grey surface that the Unimaginative designed for neutral reception, for a surface that takes a label without suggesting anything else, becomes: itself. What cardboard is when nobody tells it what to be.

Kid is standing at the base of the trunk. She has her hand on it. The bark is rough under her palm and warm — not from sunlight, from the inside, from three hundred years of living. She can feel the hum in her palm. In her wrist. In her elbow and her shoulder and the side of her face against the box.

She doesn't say anything for a long time.

*(Let this breathe. Kid has been talking since Room 1 of World 1. She was always going to get here. Give her the quiet.)*

Then:

KID: "Hey."

*(To the tree. Barely above a whisper. She doesn't need it to answer. She just wanted it to know she was here.)*

### The Imagination Restore

The World Tree's Imagination Restore is not a bloom that moves through one zone. It moves through all of them.

The game does not cut to show this. The player will learn it from what Kid finds when she comes back through the zones — and some players won't come back. Some players will finish the game and go. Both are correct.

What is visible from the inner ring, for anyone standing at the base of the trunk looking outward through the open gate:

The canopy moves. The whole canopy, all at once, a slow rotation like something stretching after a long stillness. Leaves fall from it — not dead, not shed, but intentional, the way something releases what it no longer needs. They fall through the breaks in the mill roof, through the gaps the roots made in the ceiling, down into the grey and the scattered forms and the settled board.

Green against grey. Landing everywhere.

Within the day, the board is brown again. The Unimaginative's neutralization process has been reversed at the fiber level — not by Kid, by the seed chamber, by three hundred years of accumulated understanding of what cardboard is for. It is for being made into something. You can feel it at the fold.

Within the week, boxes are moving.

Within the month:

A kid in an apartment building in a city two thousand miles away puts a box on her head.

She doesn't know why. She stands in front of the bathroom mirror. She looks at herself.

Then he looks around his room and decides it's a castle.

## Mr. Wen

*(Not a gameplay moment. A texture. The player may notice or may not. Do not flag it.)*

Mr. Wen is Kid's neighbor. Before: a martial arts instructor, ran a small dojo a few blocks away, taught kids after school. Grew tomatoes in the window boxes that ran the length of his first-floor apartment. Had a laugh you could hear through closed windows, through an autumn afternoon, through whatever you were doing. On Sunday mornings he was gone — to the old tree at the edge of the neighborhood, to sit in its roots. He knew where the World Tree was before Kid did. He knew what it was for.

After The Hush: grey. Not sad, not angry — not anything. The window boxes: empty. He walks past the spot on the sidewalk where he always stopped to talk and he does not pause. He walks home. He goes inside. He does not come back out until the next morning when there is something to do.

Kid has never said his name. She carries him the way you carry a person who was part of the texture of being alive in a place — not consciously, just present, the way a sound you're used to is present until it stops.

The game does not show Mr. Wen. It does not have a cutscene. It does not have a beat.

What it has:

In the World Tree's inner ring, on the root surface near the trunk — not a featured prop, not a marked discovery, just: something in the world for someone who looks — a single tomato plant. Small. Growing through the root surface. The tomato is red, vivid, the kind of red that means it happened in spite of everything. It grew here somehow. Three hundred years of roots. One tomato plant.

Kid may walk past it. Most players will walk past it. They are going to the boss.

But it is there.

If Kid looks at it — if a player stops and looks, if the game has a look/examine prompt here, which it should — there is one moment. Not a line. A long beat of Kid looking at the tomato plant in the root system of the World Tree.

Then she keeps going.

No dialogue. No connection made explicit. The connection is: this is what the World Tree makes possible. Not just cardboard. Not just boxes. Everything that grows. The tomato. The cherry tree at the Dojo that was too young and too tall. The apple tree in Kid's backyard that has been dead for two years.

The connection between the World Tree and Mr. Wen's tomatoes is not stated. It is true. The player who carries it out of the room carries it for the right reason.

## The Final Beat

*(After the gate opens. After the mills change. After Kid's hand on the trunk. After "Hey.")*

Kid sits at the base of the trunk, in the roots. The box is still on. Of course the box is still on. She doesn't take it off here. This is not the moment for that — it's possible that moment never comes, that she's still wearing it, that the box has been on so long it's just: where her head is.

The hum is everywhere. The leaves are falling through the gaps.

She reaches into her pocket. The blank forms she folded at the Cardboard Mill — she takes them out. Three of them. Still blank. She found them on the floor of the Permit Pulper arena and she folded them and she put them in her pocket and she didn't say why.

She starts folding. Not a box — she's been in a box. Something else. The fold goes: corner to corner, valley fold, mountain fold. It takes a few tries. She tears one form and starts with a different one. On the third try:

A bird. Paper crane. Small, imprecise, the kind a kid makes when they're still learning how.

She sets it on the root next to her.

The wind from the canopy takes it.

She watches it go.

*(Beat.)*

KID: "Okay."

*(Not said to anyone. Not an ending. Just: the acknowledgment that something is done. That what she came here to do is done and she knows it and the box knows it and the tree knows it and somewhere two thousand miles away a kid is standing in a room they've decided is a castle and the whole thing connects, all the way back to the backyard and the trebuchet and the afternoon she found the box in the recycling bin and looked through it and the backyard looked like something.)*

KID: "Okay."

*(She gets up.)*

*(She goes.)*

## Tone Note — Why This Zone Feels Different

Every zone before this one ended with Kid reclaiming what The Unimaginative took.

This one ends with the tree resuming what it always did.

The difference is: Kid did not save this. She restored access. The tree was always going to do this — it was growing through the walls in the same place for six months in a row. It did not need a kid with a box and a Waterwhip. It needed the gate open.

Kid opened the gate.

That is the whole thing. That is eight zones and eight zones of material in her pockets and a paper crane and the one who keeps watching standing near the trunk with their hands open and Mr. Wen's tomato and the cherry tree that was too young to be that tall.

She opened the gate.

The rest — the boxes moving, the kid in the apartment building, the castle — the rest is what was already going to happen once the gate was open. The tree does not need Kid's imagination to make cardboard that means something. The tree just needs to be the tree.

Kid gave it back to itself.

That's the whole argument. The box was always a door. Kid just found out where it led.

## Continuity Notes

- The paper crane connects to the blank forms from the Cardboard Mill. Players who remember the zone victory beat in World 7 ("Some things you carry in case you need to start over") will understand what she was carrying them for. Players who don't will still get the beat. Do not flag the connection.
- The tomato plant is not a prop with a quest marker. It is environmental storytelling. It should be discoverable at the same zoom level the player uses for combat — not zoomed in, not in a cutscene. Just there, if they look.
- The one who keeps watching's silence does not resolve their arc. It ends the question the player has been carrying. "Will they ever have nothing to say?" The answer is yes. Whether that's the beginning of something is not the game's to say.
- The adult narration from the ending file (`docs/story/narrative/the-ending.md`) follows this beat immediately. The zone victory beat and the ending narration are one continuous sequence. Do not put a loading screen between them.
- Kid does not say goodbye to the tree. She says "Hey." This is correct. Goodbye is for things that are over. What the tree is doing is not over. Kid knows this. She says "Hey" and she goes, the way you go somewhere you know you'll be back to.
- Kid does not take the box off at the end. This should not be a decision that is made for the player. If the credits roll with the box on, that is also correct.
