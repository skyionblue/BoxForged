# Zone Lore — The Cul-de-Sac

**World 1.** Reality: a cracked suburban cul-de-sac with empty houses and dead grass. Imagination: a Wild West main street at golden hour.

---

## Zone Intro

*(Room 1 — The Arrival. Kid's POV, present tense. Plays over the 3-second delay before enemies aggro.)*

THE CUL-DE-SAC

I come around the corner and it opens up wide. The minivans are covered wagons. The birdbath at the end of the road is a water trough. The sky is the color of an old penny.

A tumbleweed rolls through. I watch it go.

I've never been to a real main street. I don't need to have been.

KID: "Let's go."

## The One Who Keeps Watching — Mailbox Row Appearance

*(Room 4, Mailbox Row. Placement: after the room's fight settles, before the exit gate opens. Quiet, not cruel. Leave silence after the line. No rebuttal.)*

*Design note — two kinds of Skeptics:*
*The player has already fought Skeptic grunts in earlier rooms — kids with grey boxes and clipboards, working the zone. Those are the enemy type. This is different. This is one specific kid who is not processing the room. They have no clipboard. They're not doing a job. They're just standing there looking at the same thing Kid just fought for.*

*This is the player's first encounter with the one who keeps watching. They won't know yet this is someone they'll see again. See `docs/story/characters/the-skeptic.md`.*

---

### The visual moment

The mailboxes line both curbs. One door hangs open on its hinge — there's a child's drawing taped to the inside of it. Red crayon. A figure with a box on its head. It's been there a while. The birthday balloon on the nearest post has gone completely flat.

A tumbleweed drifts past the open gate at the north end. No wind. Just — drifts.

The one who keeps watching is standing beside the nearest row of mailboxes. Just standing. Grey box, plain clothes, nothing about them that announces anything. They look like a kid waiting for someone who is already late.

They look at the street. At the covered wagons, the amber sky, the wanted posters, all of it. Their face doesn't change.

They do not approach Kid. They don't need to. They just look at the same thing Kid is looking at, and see something different.

Then they say their line.

Then they walk north, through the gate, and they're gone.

The balloon on the post doesn't move. The drawing in the mailbox is still there, still red.

---

### The line

*(Confirmed. Flat delivery. One sentence. Then silence.)*

THE ONE WHO KEEPS WATCHING: "Somebody was going to bag those leaves."

*Design note: The leaves are real. There are dead leaves against the curb, the kind that pile up when no one rakes anymore. The one who keeps watching sees the leaves. Kid sees the dust on a main street no one has swept in years. They are standing in the same place, looking at the same thing, and one of them sees a chore and one of them sees a frontier.*

*The line doesn't attack imagination. It doesn't argue. It is simply, completely accurate about the material world — and it leaves a residue. For a second after, the leaves are just leaves. Then they're not. But the Skeptic is already gone.*

---

### The silent story — what the one who keeps watching looks like to an animator

This is the player's first encounter. They don't know yet this is someone they'll see again.

The kid is roughly the same height as Kid. Same age. The grey box has no crayon on it, no windows cut in it — just a stock box, like it came off the shelf and was never opened. Their clothes are plain. Nothing branded, nothing individual.

**Idle behavior:** They stand with the stillness of someone who has spent years in a chair, indoors, watching something that didn't require them to move. No fidgeting. No looking around. They face the street the way you face a television — not expecting to interact with it, just receiving.

**Movement:** When they go, they go directly. No pause. No last look. Whatever they needed to confirm, they confirmed.

**The key read for this first encounter:** they are not sinister. They are just a kid who looked at the same thing and didn't see it. The horror, if there is any, is quiet. The player should feel something they can't immediately name. Sadness is closer to it than fear.

*Note for animators: the Skeptic grunts the player has already fought move with purpose toward a task. This one doesn't have a task. That's the only visible difference — and it's enough. Same box, same flat affect, same unhurried pace. But nothing in their hands. Nothing to do. Just watching.*

### The confirmed details

- **The tumbleweed:** stays. It drifts during the 3-second arrival delay in Room 1, and a second one drifts past the gate in Room 4 when the Skeptic leaves. The same tumbleweed, maybe. The Cul-de-Sac keeps moving things that aren't trying to go anywhere.

- **The mailbox letters:** *open question — not resolved.* The mailboxes are marked with grey flags (Unimaginative extraction markers). The doors hang open on some of them. Whether there are letters inside — what they say, who they're for, whether they're readable — is a design and lore question that remains open. Do not write content for the letters until confirmed. The drawing in the open mailbox is confirmed.

---

### How the space feels when they leave

The balloon doesn't move. The drawing is still red. The exit gate is open.

Kid stands in the street for a second. Not long. Long enough that the player notices.

Then they go.

*No rebuttal. No reaction line. Kid doesn't say anything. The silence is correct.*

## Zone Lore Hook

The cul-de-sac is the edge of the neighborhood — where the planned suburb ends and the unplanned world begins. Every house here was identical once. The Unimaginative love a cul-de-sac: contained, no through-traffic, easy to mark and process. The grey flags on the mailboxes say: inventoried. Tagged. Managed.

What they didn't inventory was the drawing in the mailbox door. Red crayon. A figure with a box on its head. No signature. No date. It was already there when they arrived.

It's still there now.
